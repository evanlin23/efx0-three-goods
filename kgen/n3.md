## 1. Definitions (as in k4/sdc.md §29 and its code)
- **Setting.**
  - Agents 1, 2, 3 have additive values v_i(g) ≥ 0, with R_i = {g : v_i(g) > 0}.
  - An order π gives serial-dictatorship picks y_i: each agent in turn takes its favourite remaining good of R_i, ties
    to the smaller index, and nothing if none is left.
  - J is the set of unpicked goods.
- **Chain** (k4/sdc/de_free.py). Distinct agents x₀, …, x_t, with every passed base nonempty.
  - x₀ takes H₀ ⊆ J, plus goods of B_{x_t} when t ≥ 1. When t = 0, H₀ is *added* to x₀'s pick.
  - Each x_i with i ≥ 1 takes B_{x_{i−1}} ∪ H_i, with H_i ⊆ J.
  - Every H lies inside its receiver's R. Every agent on the chain strictly gains. The rest of B_{x_t} becomes junk.
- **One-absorber completion of bases B.** A complete EFX₀ allocation X ⊇ B in which at most one agent receives two or
  more goods outside its base.
- **OC₁(t ≥ 0)**, as tested by `k4/sdc/oc_absorb.py`: for every order, the picks have a one-absorber completion, or
  some chain with t ≥ 0 gives bases that have one.
- **OC₁(t ≥ 1)**: the same with t ≥ 1, as written in §29.

**Reachability of a fixed allocation (used by the brute force).** Taking every H as large as X allows is optimal: the
gains and the absorber count are monotone in H. So whether a given EFX₀ allocation X is reachable from the picks is a
direct test on X (`kgen/oc3.c`, header):
- t = 0: every pick stays with its picker, and x₀'s bundle adds X_{x₀} ∩ J ∩ R_{x₀};
- t = 1: y_{x₀} ∈ X_{x₁} and y_z ∈ X_z;
- t = 2: y_{x₀} ∈ X_{x₁} and y_{x₁} ∈ X_{x₂};
- in each case with the strict gains, then the absorber count.

`kgen/verify_oc1.py` enumerates the chains explicitly instead, to cross-check this.

## 4. What repairs the hard starts (`kgen/shapes3.py`, 2,000 random instances, m = 6–11, 2,390 hard starts)
- A **t = 1 chain** (one hand-off x₀ → x₁, the third agent z keeps its pick) repairs every hard start. t = 2 is never
  needed there.
- One such chain always has **z as the absorber, or no absorber** ("T1Z"). The chain agents absorb nothing beyond
  their sets H.
- No fixed role works:
  - z = the first agent covers 2,286 of 2,390;
  - z = the last agent covers 2,320;
  - x₀ frozen covers 1,377.

  At large k the chain is driven by the amount of junk, not by who wants whose pick.
- On the climbs' counterexamples to OC₁(t ≥ 1) no t = 1 chain repairs, but a t = 0 move does: the instance needs a
  second agent that absorbs goods it values.
