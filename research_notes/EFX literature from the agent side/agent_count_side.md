# EFX and EFX₀ existence by number of agents and goods (the agent side), to 26 November 2026

Scope: results that restrict the number of agents n, the number of goods m relative to n, the number of unallocated goods (charity), or the number of valuation types, plus where each proof uses its restriction, and where a "few relevant goods per agent" result can meet them. Search date 2026-11-26. This file builds on `proofs/citations.md`, `proofs/novelty.md` and `proofs/pq_bounded.md` (read first); papers already digested there are cited to them and not re-read unless stated.

Notation: **EFX₀** = v_i(X_i) ≥ v_i(X_j ∖ {g}) for every g ∈ X_j (goods worth 0 to i included). **EFX** = only goods with v_i(g) > 0. Almost every paper below defines EFX in the all-goods form, which with nonnegative values is EFX₀. Several (CGM, Mahara, three types) then restrict to *non-degenerate* instances (no two bundles tie, hence every good has positive value for every agent) and recover general instances by the perturbation lemma; this still yields EFX₀ for general nonnegative instances, but it matters for the gap analysis (§7).

Read levels: **full**; **section** (named pages or sections read in the PDF text); **grep** (full text searched, matches read in context); **abstract**; **repo** (read in an earlier session, recorded in the named repository file, not re-read). Preprint vs peer-reviewed is stated for each item. PDFs were fetched from arxiv.org and converted with `pdftotext` in the container; page numbers are PDF pages.

## 1. Two and three agents (Plaut–Roughgarden; Chaudhury–Garg–Mehlhorn; Akrami et al.), and is polynomial time for three agents open?

### Takeaway
n = 2 is settled for all monotone valuations (EFX₀, cut-and-choose with leximin++); n = 3 is settled for additive valuations (EFX₀, CGM), extended to nice-cancelable and to "one MMS-feasible agent + two arbitrary monotone", and as of November 2026 the monotone three-agent case has an exact threshold: EFX exists for m ≤ 7 and can fail at m = 8. Polynomial-time computation for three additive agents is still open (stated as open in July 2026); the best known algorithm is pseudo-polynomial.

### Inferences
- Polynomial time for three additive agents is open as far as anything found shows (latest statement of openness: July 2026; none of the 121 arXiv papers returned by nine date-sorted searches claims it). The project's Draft and Exchange algorithm is polynomial for every n when every agent values ≤ 3 goods; restricted to n = 3 that is a (small) class where polynomial-time EFX₀ is known.
- For **monotone** valuations the relevant-goods threshold is pinned between 3 and 8: EFX₀ holds when every agent has ≤ 2 relevant goods (evanlin23/mrd-efx `MRDMono`, per `proofs/citations.md`) and fails with 3 agents who each value all 8 goods (Thm 1.1, singletons all 1). Whether monotone EFX₀ holds for k = 3,…,7 relevant goods was not found anywhere.
- At n = 3 additive, CGM subsumes any relevant-goods result; nothing on the agent side at n ≤ 3 needs the project's techniques, and the project's k ≤ 3 theorem adds nothing at n ≤ 3 except a polynomial algorithm.

## 2. Four agents: Berger–Cohen–Feldman–Fiat, Alkassar–Fouz–Mehlhorn, and anything newer (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Complete EFX for four additive agents is still open (November 2026). What exists: EFX with at most one unallocated, unenvied good (BCFF, nice-cancelable, AAAI 2022); complete EFX₀ for four additive agents with m ≤ 9 (AFM, computer-certified preprint, Aug 2026); EF2X for four cancelable agents (AAAI 2025); four agents with ≤ 3 distinct valuations or with two identical agents (types results, §5). For monotone valuations at n = 4: m ≤ 7 exists, m ≥ 9 can fail, m = 8 open.

### Cited findings
- **Berger–Cohen–Feldman–Fiat, "(Almost Full) EFX Exists for Four Agents (and Beyond)", arXiv 2102.10654 (AAAI 2022, 36:4826–4833).** Peer-reviewed. Read: abstract; section (§1–1.2, pp. 2–4). Theorem 1: every setting with 4 additive agents has an EFX allocation with at most one unallocated item, not envied by anyone; extends to nice-cancelable valuations (cancelable: v(S∪g) > v(T∪g) ⇒ v(S) > v(T); "nice" adds a cancelable non-degenerate approximation, p. 3). Prior bound for 4 agents was ≤ 3 unallocated (CKMS) (Table 1, p. 3). — [arXiv 2102.10654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10654)
  - Technique (pp. 2–4): CGM's lexicographic potential with envy edges, champion edges i →g j, and new **generalized champion edges** i →(H|S) j (champions relative to sets of goods, allocated or not); "the goal is to find a cycle consisting of envy, champion, and generalized champion edges, such that the union of all sets S freed up along with the currently unallocated items suffice for the suggested allocation along the cycle". Without envy edges they Pareto-improve; with envy edges they advance the potential.
  - Where n = 4 is used: they show "for any EFX allocation with at least two unallocated items" with four agents a reshuffle advances the lexicographic potential ("a complex puzzle", p. 3). They cite CGM+21's obstruction: "an instance with 4 additive agents in which there exists an EFX allocation with one unallocated item such that no progress can be made based on the lexicographic potential function"; so allocating the last item needs "a different potential function" (p. 3).
- **Alkassar–Fouz–Mehlhorn, "Complete EFX Allocations Exist for Four Additive Agents and Up to Nine Goods", arXiv 2608.08590v1 (9 Aug 2026).** Preprint; "AI-assisted throughout" (title-page footnote). Read: repo (abstract, §1, Def. 1, Thm 1). Complete **EFX₀** (Definition 1 distinguishes EFX0 and EFX) for n = 4, additive, m ≤ 9. Method: hand reductions + covering the valuation polytope by sub-polytopes with fixed candidate allocations, each certified by a QF_LRA UNSAT verdict and an independent certifier. — [arXiv 2608.08590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08590); [repo citations.md](/home/user/efx0-three-goods/proofs/citations.md)
- **Ashuri–Gkatzelis–Sgouritsa, "EF2X Exists For Four Agents", arXiv 2412.00254 (AAAI 2025, 39:13555–13563 per 2604.18216v4 references).** Peer-reviewed. Read: abstract; section (§1 p. 2, §3 pp. 6–7). EF2X (envy vanishes after removing any two goods) exists for four agents with cancelable valuations, and one of the four may have an arbitrary monotone valuation; pseudo-polynomial. Technique: start from an arbitrary partition into four bundles and transform it through "a sequence of stages" with structural invariants; potential φ(X) = v_1(X_1) (Def. 3.2, p. 7). They note EFX proofs for three agents "tend to be quite long … extending those arguments to instances with four agents becomes rather intractable" (p. 2). — [arXiv 2412.00254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00254)
- **Monotone four agents.** 2604.18216v4 §7 (p. 25): monotone EFX exists for m ≤ n + 3 (Mahara) and fails for m ≥ n + 5 (n ≥ 3), so "the question is therefore open exactly for n ≥ 4 and m = n + 4"; for n = 4 that is m = 8. "Whether EFX allocations always exist for additive valuations beyond three agents remains open." — [arXiv 2604.18216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18216)
- **Approximate.** Amanatidis–Filos-Ratsikas–Sgouritsa (EC 2024; arXiv 2406.12413): 2/3-EFX for n ≤ 7 additive agents (repo, `proofs/citations.md`; also stated in 2604.18216v4 p. 5). — [arXiv 2406.12413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12413)
- **Other 2026 four-agent material.** Wang (arXiv 2608.30203, preprint; repo): rival-injective EFX₀ certificates; states additive EFX₀ is open for n ≥ 4 and that its 4 × 10 family "is not an unrestricted 4 × 10 theorem". A web search for 2026 four-agent EFX found only BCFF, AFM and the open-status statements. — [repo citations.md](/home/user/efx0-three-goods/proofs/citations.md); [AAAI page of BCFF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/20410)

### Inferences
- The project's k ≤ 3 theorem gives complete EFX₀ for four agents with **unbounded m** whenever each agent values ≤ 3 goods: a four-agent class not covered by AFM (m ≤ 9), BCFF (one good left over), or the types results. For k = 4, the project's ledger row K4.R4 already certifies every connected k = 4 core with n = 4 (1,002 cores), so no four-agent paper extends the project's coverage at k = 4 ([LEDGER.md](/home/user/efx0-three-goods/LEDGER.md), rows K4.R3–K4.R5).

This is trivial but it marks where relevant-goods structure enters BCFF's statement.

## 3. Results bounded in m relative to n, and non-existence (n + 3, n + 4, n + 5)

### Takeaway
Refereed: complete EFX₀ for monotone valuations when m ≤ n + 3 (Mahara, MOR 2024); additive m ≤ n + 2 earlier (Amanatidis–Markakis–Ntokos). Unrefereed: m = n + 4 additive (Bratby, Zenodo, AI-generated, still v1.0). Negative: monotone (even weighted-coverage/submodular) EFX fails for every n ≥ 3, m ≥ n + 5, so for monotone valuations only m = n + 4, n ≥ 4 is open. Neoh–Teh show any "m = n + ω(1)" existence theorem would settle EFX in general, and the padding lemma of 2604.18216v4 (valid for additive too) propagates counterexamples upward in n at fixed excess.

### Inferences
- **Monotonicity in n at fixed excess (additive).** Lemma 3.5 holds for additive valuations and needs positive singletons, which the perturbation lemma (CGM Lemma 1 / project L1) supplies. Contrapositive: if additive EFX₀ holds for all instances with N agents and N + c goods, it holds for all instances with n₀ ≤ N agents and m₀ ≤ n₀ + c goods (m₀ ≥ n₀ + 1). So, e.g., a proof of additive EFX₀ for m = n + 5 at any single N ≥ 4 would imply AFM's (4, 9) case. (My derivation from the lemma's statement; the paper states only the upward direction for counterexamples.)
- **Excess-bounded results reach cores only at small n.** Project cores have m ≤ 2n for k = 3 (PROMPT.md L4) and m ≤ 2n₃ + 3n₄ ≤ 3n for k = 4 (`k4/SCOUT.md`, ledger K4.L), because cores keep private goods. (The bound m ≤ kn/2 quoted in the task holds only for cores without private goods.) The excess e = m − n is therefore up to n (k = 3) or 2n (k = 4). For a connected k = 4 core in which every agent has 4 goods, β = 3n − m + 1 (SCOUT L4), so e = 2n + 1 − β: Mahara (e ≤ 3) covers β ≥ 2n − 2, Bratby (e = 4, unrefereed) adds β = 2n − 3. The project's K4.MC7 covers β ≤ 4. For such cores the window **5 ≤ β ≤ 2n − 4** (refereed: 5 ≤ β ≤ 2n − 3) is covered by neither side; it is non-empty from n = 5 on. The agent side covers the "dense" end (few goods, high β), the project's minimal-counterexample work the "sparse" end (low β). — [PROMPT.md](/home/user/efx0-three-goods/PROMPT.md); [k4/SCOUT.md](/home/user/efx0-three-goods/k4/SCOUT.md); [LEDGER.md](/home/user/efx0-three-goods/LEDGER.md)
- Neoh–Teh's Theorem 3.4 and Lemma 3.5 both pad with agents or copies that value many goods (the new goods are valued by the new agents and by an old agent, or the copies value all old goods plus all k new ones), so neither padding stays inside a bounded-relevant-goods class. The relevant-goods parameter and the excess parameter are therefore genuinely independent; neither reduction transfers results between them.
- For monotone valuations, Mahara's m ≤ n + 3 is now tight up to the single open column m = n + 4 (n ≥ 4).

### Gaps
- Bratby's proof was not checked (here or in the repository); no review, citation or refutation of it was found.
- Amanatidis–Markakis–Ntokos's m ≤ n + 2 result was not read; whether it is stated as EFX or EFX₀ is unknown here.
- No paper found proves an excess-bounded result for additive valuations beyond n + 4 for all n; AFM's (4, ≤ 9) is the only e = 5 result.

## 4. EFX with charity / partial EFX: how the number of unallocated goods depends on n

### Takeaway
Exact EFX with an unenvied pool: < n goods (CKMS, general valuations), then ≤ n − 2 (BCFF for nice-cancelable; Mahara for general monotone), ≤ 1 for n = 4 (BCFF) and for n = 3 (Mahara's n − 2), and ≤ k − 2 for k distinct valuations independent of n (GHNV). Approximate (1 − ε)-EFX: the rainbow-cycle line drove charity from O((n/ε)^{4/5}) to O(√(n/ε)) (Sivashankar, July 2026), which is optimal for that reduction; with k types it is Õ(√(k/ε)). No charity bound parameterized by relevant goods per agent was found.

### Cited findings

### Inferences
- For exact EFX the charity bound is linear in n (n − 2) in general but **independent of n** under few types (k − 2). The project's parameter (relevant goods per agent) is also n-independent, and k ≤ 3 already gives charity 0. A natural meeting statement, not found in any paper: "every instance with ≤ k relevant goods per agent has an EFX₀ allocation with ≤ f(k) unallocated goods that nobody envies". For k = 4 this would be a fallback to TARGET₄, and the champion/PI-cycle machinery behind n − 2 is the obvious tool.
- Under EFX₀ the pool can always absorb goods that nobody values (they can go to an envy-graph source, project L3), so for k-bounded instances only pool goods with ≥ 1 valuer count. If every pool good has a single valuer, giving it to that valuer is safe for everyone else exactly when that valuer is unenvied or her bundle contains no good worthless to an envier; this is where the EFX₀ subtlety enters a charity-to-complete step.
- The rainbow-cycle line is about approximate EFX and is optimal within its reduction (Sivashankar); it does not bear on exact EFX₀ for few relevant goods.

### Gaps
- Searches turned up no paper on EFX or EFX₀ with charity parameterized by the number of relevant goods per agent, or by the maximum number of valuers per good beyond the (p, q)-bounded results.
- Whether BCFF's and Mahara's n − 2 constructions keep the pool unenvied *under EFX₀ with zero values* follows only through the perturbation lemma; not checked separately.

## 5. Few valuation types (two, three, four)

### Takeaway
Complete EFX (EFX₀ via perturbation) exists for two additive types (Mahara), two general types (Mahara), three additive types for any n (Prakash HV–Ghosal–Nimbhorkar–Varma, EC 2025), and n − 2 identical agents plus two arbitrary MMS-feasible outliers (Ghosal et al., AAAI 2025). Four types is open for exact EFX: only 2/3-EFX and (1 − ε)-EFX with Õ(√(k/ε)) charity are known. Types are agent classes, so three types generalize n = 3.

### Cited findings
- **Mahara, "Existence of EFX for Two Additive Valuations", arXiv 2008.08798 (v4 Feb 2025; *Discrete Appl. Math.* 340, 2023 per repo).** Read: abstract. Complete EFX when each agent has one of two identical additive valuations, any n and m; constructive: "we iteratively obtain a Pareto dominating (partial) EFX allocation from an existing partial EFX allocation". — [arXiv 2008.08798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08798)
- **Mahara 2107.09901, Theorem 1:** two general (monotone) valuations, via the new partition leximin potential (p. 4). — [arXiv 2107.09901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09901)
- **Prakash HV–Ghosal–Nimbhorkar–Varma, "EFX Exists for Three Types of Agents", arXiv 2410.13580v3 (EC 2025, pp. 101–128 per 2604.18216v4 refs).** Peer-reviewed. Read: abstract; section (§1 pp. 2–3, §2 definitions p. 4). Theorem 1: an EFX allocation always exists for n agents with at most three types, agents of a type having identical additive valuations; "generalizes and unifies both the three-agent case [CGM24] and the two-types case [Mah23]". Definition 5 is the all-goods form (∀g ∈ X_aj); they restrict to non-degenerate instances ("This implies that all goods are positively valued by all agents", p. 4) via the reduction of Akrami et al. - **Prakash HV–Mehta–Nimbhorkar, "Almost and Approximate EFX for Few Types of Agents", arXiv 2508.15380 (Aug 2025).** Preprint. Read: abstract. Hosseini et al. 2608.10397: EFX ∨ MMS for additive mixed items with ≤ 3 valuation types when one type is a singleton (abstract). — [arXiv 2601.03438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03438); [arXiv 2608.10397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10397)

### Inferences
- The perturbation used to reach non-degenerate instances adds the same ε·2^j to every agent's value of good g_j (CGM Lemma 1, p. 5), so it preserves the number of types; the three-types theorem therefore gives EFX₀ for nonnegative additive instances with ≤ 3 types.
- In a few-relevant-goods instance two agents of the same type share the same relevant set and values, so a core with more than three distinct agents has more than three types; the types theorems cover essentially no project cores. What may transfer is the *technique*: grouping agents with identical valuations so that a group behaves like one agent with several bundles (Plaut–Roughgarden's identical-valuations leximin++, Ghosal et al.'s leading agent). In a core, "twins" (agents with the same relevant set and the same ranking) could be handled this way.
- The exact frontiers "four types", "four agents" and "four relevant goods (EFX₀)" are all open; the project's k ≤ 3 theorem is the relevant-goods analogue of the three-types and three-agents theorems.

### Gaps
- No exact (complete) EFX result for four or more additive types was found, published or preprint.
- Full texts of Mahara's two-types paper and of the three-types case analysis were not read beyond the overview.

## 6. Techniques, and exactly where each proof uses its restriction

### Takeaway
Agent-count proofs use a global progress measure (lexicographic or min-bundle potential, over partial EFX allocations or over partitions) plus local improvement moves (envy cycles, champion or PI cycles, half-bundle swaps); the restriction on n, m or types enters in the final case analysis of "stuck" configurations, or in a counting lemma that limits how many large bundles a stuck state can have. Computer-assisted proofs (SAT/SMT, polytope covers) use the restriction for finiteness.

## 7. Gap analysis: where a "few relevant goods" result can meet the agent side

### Takeaway
Today the two sides meet only at small n, and there the project's own certified results (TARGET for k ≤ 3; K4.R3–R5 and K4.MC6–MC7 for k = 4) already reach further than any agent-count theorem. The useful junctions are (i) per-component combination, which is immediate under EFX₀; (ii) Mahara's few-large-bundles counting; (iii) an n-independent charity bound in terms of k, as types give for exact EFX; and (iv) a caution: agent-side champion machinery runs on perturbed, non-degenerate instances in which every good is relevant to every agent, so it cannot see relevant-goods sparsity as written.

### Cited findings
- Under EFX₀, agents in different connected components of the agent–good relevance graph value nothing in each other's bundles, so components can be solved separately (project L6); goods valued by nobody must go to an envy-graph source (L3). — [PROMPT.md](/home/user/efx0-three-goods/PROMPT.md)
- The champion machinery assumes non-degeneracy: CGM "only deal[s] with non-degenerate instances. In non-degenerate instances, all goods have positive value for all agents" (p. 6); Mahara: "every agent j has a g-champion. Indeed, since instances are non-degenerate … X_j ∪ g >_j X_j" (p. 8); three types: "all goods are positively valued by all agents" (p. 4). — [arXiv 2002.05119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05119); [arXiv 2107.09901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09901); [arXiv 2410.13580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13580)
- Under ordinary EFX, multigraph instances are trivial by pairwise cut-and-choose; the difficulty is EFX₀ (Sgouritsa–Sotiriou Remark 1). EFX₀ can force a good onto an agent who values it at 0, and EFX orientations need not exist (Christodoulou et al.). Viswanathan–Mehta's 4-limited result is ordinary EFX and gives worthless goods to an arbitrary agent. — [repo citations.md](/home/user/efx0-three-goods/proofs/citations.md)
- The project's ledger certifies every connected k = 4 core with n ≤ 3 (K4.R3) and n = 4 (K4.R4, 1,002 cores), n = 5 cores with ≤ 2 four-good agents (K4.R5), and TARGET₄ for core components with β ≤ 4 (K4.MC7, using the multigraph theorem); a minimal counterexample to TARGET₄ has n ≤ 3(β − 1) (K4.MC4). — [LEDGER.md](/home/user/efx0-three-goods/LEDGER.md)
- Agent-side frontier (November 2026): additive EFX₀ is complete for n ≤ 3, for n = 4 with m ≤ 9 (preprint), for m ≤ n + 3, for m = n + 4 (unrefereed), and for ≤ 3 types; partial (one good) for n = 4; monotone fails at m ≥ n + 5. — sources in §§1–5.

### Inferences
1. **Per-component union theorem (immediate).** Discard goods nobody values, split into components, and solve each with whichever theorem covers it: ≤ 3 agents (CGM), 4 agents and ≤ 9 goods (AFM, preprint), m_C ≤ n_C + 3 (Mahara), every agent ≤ 3 relevant goods (project), every good ≤ 2 valuers (Afshinmehr et al.), ≤ 3 types (Prakash HV et al.). Then put the worthless goods at an envy-graph source of the union. Because cross-component envy is zero under EFX₀, the union is EFX₀. Applied after the project's core reduction, this is what `k4/SCOUT.md` §1 already does with Mahara, the multigraph theorem and AFM.
2. **No agent-side theorem extends the project's k = 4 coverage.** k = 3 cores have e = m − n ≤ n and k = 4 cores e ≤ 2n (§3), so excess-bounded theorems reach whole core families only for n ≤ 3–4 (k = 3) or n ≤ 2 (k = 4), and agent-count theorems only for n ≤ 4, where the project's exhaustive certificates already apply. The joint open region is k = 4 cores with n ≥ 5 and, for cores where every agent has 4 goods, 5 ≤ β ≤ 2n − 4 (§3). The cheapest new junction would be an existence theorem for **m = n + 5** (closing β = 2n − 4) or one extending K4.MC7 to β = 5.
3. **Sparsity is invisible to perturbed proofs.** CGM, Mahara and the three-types proof run on non-degenerate instances, where every good is relevant to every agent; there every agent envies X_j ∪ g for its own j, which guarantees champions. With genuine zeros (k-bounded instances, no perturbation), an agent that does not value g need not envy X_j ∪ g, so champion edges for g come only from g's valuers. A version of the champion-graph or PI-cycle argument that works directly with zero values would have a stuck-configuration analysis that touches only the valuers of the unallocated good and the owners of the bundles they envy. That is the most plausible route to a "local CGM" for goods with ≤ 3 valuers, i.e. the (k, p) = (4, 3) class that the ledger's K4.P3 evidence singles out. This direction is untested; nothing in the literature read does it.
4. **The EFX₀-specific constraint is local.** By definition, if X_j contains a good worth 0 to i, EFX₀ for i toward j requires v_i(X_i) ≥ v_i(X_j), i.e. no envy at all. EFX-feasibility of a bundle for agent i therefore depends only on X_j ∩ R_i and on whether X_j ⊆ R_i. Partition-space arguments (Akrami et al., AGS) test EFX-feasibility bundle by bundle, so with ≤ k relevant goods the feasibility test is a function of ≤ k goods plus one bit. This suggests partition-space potentials adapt more easily to few relevant goods than lexicographic ones. (Inference from the definitions; untested.)
5. **n-independent charity.** Types give exact EFX with k_types − 2 unallocated goods; the agent count gives n − 2. An analogous statement for relevant goods (≤ f(k) unenvied leftover goods with ≥ 1 valuer each) is unexplored and would be a legitimate fallback for k = 4. BCFF's generalized champion edges, which free *sets* along cycles, are the closest existing tool.
6. **Answer to "a known n ≤ 4 result with arbitrary goods that the approach could extend, or vice versa".** At n ≤ 3 the agent side (CGM, additive, any m) subsumes relevant-goods results. At n = 4 the only arbitrary-m statements are partial (BCFF, one good), relaxed (EF2X, 2/3-EFX) or type-restricted. The project's k ≤ 3 theorem is a genuinely new four-agent (and n-agent) complete class, and together with item 1 it extends CGM to instances whose components each have ≤ 3 agents or ≤ 3 relevant goods per agent. Conversely, a "4 agents, ≤ 4 relevant goods, any m" statement is already covered by the project's K4.R4 plus the core reduction. So the first agent-side cell with few relevant goods that is not settled is n = 5 with k = 4.
7. **Monotone extension.** For monotone valuations the counterexample has every agent valuing all eight goods, and EFX₀ holds at k ≤ 2. Pinning the monotone relevant-goods threshold in {3,…,8} is a clean question that sits exactly between the two sides: a 3-agent monotone counterexample with fewer relevant goods would have to use private goods, because m ≥ 8 is needed at n = 3 (Thm 1.2).

### Gaps
- Not established whether CGM's or Mahara's champion arguments go through on degenerate instances with genuine zeros; their texts avoid the question by perturbing.
- The full proofs of BCFF and of the three types theorem were not read, so whether their case analyses localize to small agent sets is unknown.
- No paper found combines an agent-count or excess bound with a bound on relevant goods per agent (other than Viswanathan–Mehta's ordinary-EFX abstract). The full EC 2026 and SODA 2027 programs, and non-arXiv repositories beyond Zenodo, were not searched in this session.
