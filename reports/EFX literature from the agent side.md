# Every EFX existence ladder stalls at four

No published theorem and no technique imported from another field closes the four-relevant-goods case (TARGET₄), and the agent-side literature cannot close it in its current form. Its existence theorems (three agents, four agents with at most nine goods, m ≤ n + 3, three valuation types) cover whole families of the project's cores only for n ≤ 4, where the project's own certificates already go further. The reductions that carry its results to larger instances add agents who value many goods, so they never enter a bounded-relevance class. Its proof machinery (champion graphs, lexicographic and Pareto potentials) runs on perturbed instances in which every agent values every good, which erases the zeros the project exploits. It also measures progress by agents' values, and the project's refutation K4.SDC.P1 rules out exactly that kind of progress at four goods. The structural literature is closer: the multigraph theorem settles every additive instance whose goods have at most two valuers, which makes (k, p) = (4, 3) the first open cell, and the only exact hypergraph theorem appears, on our reading, to need its girth hypothesis only around one parking vertex. Non-existence brackets the monotone case, since EFX₀ holds at k ≤ 2 and fails at k = 8; and because "arbitrary k" is exactly the additive EFX conjecture, any argument for all k must use additivity. From other fields only three tools fit: matroid union, which yields an exact Hall-type dual for completing a fixed serial-dictatorship order in a sub-regime; Haxell's alternating trees, a termination pattern that does not track agents' values; and independent transversals, for protecting frozen agents. The ranked directions at the end put the matroid dual first, the (4, 3) cell second and a termination argument that does not track values third.

**Reading conventions.** A sentence with a citation and no tag reports what that source states, read at least at abstract level; abstract-only, snippet-level and secondary reads are flagged, and facts about this project cite its own files. *[derived]* marks the researchers' own inference from a source's text or from the definitions, which the authors do not claim and nobody has checked line by line; *[spec]* marks an untested proposal; *[unverified]* marks a source nobody on the team has read. Preprints and AI-generated or AI-assisted works are flagged where cited. Notation follows the project brief ([PROMPT.md](../PROMPT.md)): R_i is the set of goods agent i values positively and k = max |R_i|; p is the largest number of agents valuing one good; β = edges − vertices + 1 is the cyclomatic number of a connected core's agent–good incidence graph. EFX₀ requires v_i(X_i) ≥ v_i(X_j ∖ {g}) for every g ∈ X_j, including goods worthless to i, whereas ordinary EFX removes only goods i values; nearly every paper below proves the EFX₀ form.

## Three agents, three types and three goods are settled; four is open on every ladder

The agent-count ladder is settled through three. **Two agents** with arbitrary monotone valuations always have an EFX₀ allocation, by cut-and-choose on the leximin++ partition ([Plaut–Roughgarden](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04769)). **Three additive agents always do** ([Chaudhury–Garg–Mehlhorn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05119), JACM 2024), a result since extended to nice-cancelable valuations ([Berger–Cohen–Feldman–Fiat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10654)) and to one MMS-feasible agent beside two arbitrary monotone ones ([Akrami et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07638)). For monotone valuations the three-agent case now has an exact threshold: **every instance with at most seven goods has an EFX allocation, and one with eight weighted-coverage goods has none** ([Akrami–Mackenzie–Mayorov–Mehlhorn–Srinivas–Suzuki–Weidenbach, v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18216), preprint). Four additive agents remain open. The best statements there are EFX with at most one unenvied good left unallocated ([BCFF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10654)) and complete EFX₀ when m ≤ 9 ([Alkassar–Fouz–Mehlhorn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08590), preprint, AI-assisted, certified by QF_LRA verdicts and an independent certifier). Running time is open even for three additive agents: the lack of a polynomial-time algorithm was restated in July 2026 ([Babaioff–Frosh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14668)), and the fastest known algorithm is pseudo-polynomial ([Ashuri–Gkatzelis–Sgouritsa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00254)).

The other ladders stall at the same height, as the table shows.

Two further results shape this frontier. Neoh and Teh prove that **an existence theorem for m = n + ⌊f(n)⌋ with any f ∈ ω(1) would settle EFX for all n and m** ([Neoh–Teh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03951), AAAI 2025), so the excess ladder collapses: climbing it far enough is as hard as the whole conjecture. For charity, exact EFX is known with fewer than n and then n − 2 unallocated, unenvied goods ([Chaudhury–Kavitha–Mehlhorn–Sgouritsa](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04596); [Mahara](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09901)). With t distinct valuations the bound is t − 2, **independent of n** ([Ghosal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10632), AAAI 2025). Approximate (1 − ε)-EFX now needs only O(√(n/ε)) unallocated goods, thanks to a linear bound on the rainbow cycle number ([Sivashankar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27455), preprint, abstract read).

In the hand proofs, the restriction enters only in the final analysis of configurations where progress is stuck. Chaudhury–Garg–Mehlhorn split cases by the number of sources in the envy graph and by the cycles of the champion graph ([CGM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05119)). BCFF solve "a complex puzzle" specific to four agents ([BCFF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10654)). Mahara's m ≤ n + 3 theorem is the exception, because its restriction is a local count. Lemma 16 shows that if the champion graph has no Pareto-improvable cycle, then m ≤ n + 3 forces exactly two two-good bundles and one unallocated good, and the argument rests on the observation that "no agent EFX envies 1-agents" ([Mahara](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09901)). That is the same fact behind the project's conjecture-D shape, at most one bundle with more than two goods, which is proved at k = 3 *[derived parallel]*.

Three barrier instances constrain all of these proofs. CGM's Section 5 shows a partial EFX allocation that no complete one Pareto-dominates ([CGM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05119)). CGM+21's four-agent, nine-good instance has a partial allocation that the first agent prefers to every complete EFX allocation, which defeats the lexicographic potential ([Akrami et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07638); [BCFF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10654)). Mahara's §6 instance (n = 3, m = 7) has one unallocated good and allows no lexicographic progress ([Mahara](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09901)).

What the project's k ≤ 3 theorem adds to the agent side follows directly *[derived]*. It gives complete EFX₀ for every four-agent (indeed every n-agent) instance in which each agent values at most three goods, with m unbounded. This class lies outside AFM's m ≤ 9, BCFF's one leftover good and the types theorems. Because the project's algorithm K3ALG runs in polynomial time ([README](../README.md)), it is also one of the few classes with polynomial-time EFX₀ at n = 3. Combined with per-component solving (next section), it extends Chaudhury–Garg–Mehlhorn to instances whose relevance components each have at most three agents or at most three relevant goods per agent.

## Agent-side proofs cannot see sparsity, and their reductions never reach it

The champion machinery assumes non-degeneracy, and non-degeneracy removes zeros. Chaudhury–Garg–Mehlhorn write: "From now on we only deal with non-degenerate instances. In non-degenerate instances, all goods have positive value for all agents" ([CGM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05119)). Mahara obtains a g-champion for every agent "since instances are non-degenerate" ([Mahara](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09901)). The three-types proof notes that "all goods are positively valued by all agents" ([Prakash HV et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13580)). Perturbation then turns these results into EFX₀ theorems for general nonnegative instances, but only by making every good relevant to every agent, so as written the proofs cannot use the project's parameter. With genuine zeros, champion edges for an unallocated good g come only from g's valuers *[derived]*. A zero-aware version of the champion or Pareto-improvable-cycle argument would then analyse stuck states involving only g's valuers and the owners of the bundles they envy. Nobody has tried this "local CGM" *[spec]*. The same densification blocks ordinary-EFX results. Replacing zeros by ε reduces EFX₀ to EFX ([Amanatidis et al., Prop. 2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09838)), but the perturbed instance is no longer sparse. So Viswanathan–Mehta's ordinary EFX for agents who each like at most four goods ([AAMAS 2024](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p2534.pdf)) says nothing about EFX₀ *[derived]*.

The second obstacle is that these arguments only ever raise agents' values. **The project's refuted conjecture K4.SDC.P1 is the four-goods twin of CGM's Section 5 barrier** *[derived parallel]*. At n = 4, m = 7, a partial EFX₀ allocation that is Pareto-maximal among partial ones cannot be completed, and every complete EFX₀ allocation lowers some agent below it ([LEDGER.md](../LEDGER.md), row K4.SDC.P1). CGM escaped their barrier with a fixed-priority lexicographic potential, and CGM+21 broke that potential at four agents. The project's fixed-priority test on level vectors likewise fails for some agent orders at n = 4 (instance Q), though every profile tested had some order that works ([k4/gm4.md](../k4/gm4.md)). Both sides have hit the same wall at the same height.

The agent-side approaches still advancing avoid potentials over partial allocations. They are partition-space repair over complete partitions ([Akrami et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07638); [Ashuri–Gkatzelis–Sgouritsa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00254); the critical conflicts of [AMM+ v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18216)) and group-minimum potentials for few types ([Prakash HV et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13580)). Partition-space arguments suit sparsity unusually well. By definition, agent i's EFX₀ verdict on a bundle X_j depends only on X_j ∩ R_i and on one bit, whether X_j ⊆ R_i, because a bundle that holds a good worthless to i must not be envied at all ([PROMPT.md](../PROMPT.md)) *[derived]*. With k ≤ 4, every such test therefore looks at no more than four goods plus that bit.

The padding reductions that propagate agent-side results all leave the sparse world. Neoh–Teh's Theorem 3.4 adds agents and goods, the new goods valued by the new agents and by one original agent ([Neoh–Teh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03951)). Lemma 3.5 of the counterexample paper replaces an agent by copies, each valuing k new goods plus every old good ([AMM+ v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18216)). Both raise some agent's number of relevant goods without bound. So **the excess and relevance parameters are independent: no known reduction transfers results between them** *[derived]*. Whether general additive EFX₀ reduces to bounded-degree instances, by some sparsification reduction, is not addressed anywhere the team searched.

Lemma 3.5 still helps in reverse *[derived]*. It preserves additivity and needs positive singleton values, which perturbation supplies. Its contrapositive therefore says: if additive EFX₀ holds for every instance with N agents and N + c goods, it holds for every instance with n₀ ≤ N agents and m₀ ≤ n₀ + c goods. For example, a proof for m = n + 5 at any single N ≥ 4 would imply AFM's (4, 9) case.

The geometry of the cores shows how little the two sides overlap. Cores satisfy m ≤ 2n at k = 3 ([PROMPT.md](../PROMPT.md), L4) and m ≤ 2n₃ + 3n₄ ≤ 3n at k = 4 ([k4/SCOUT.md](../k4/SCOUT.md)), so the excess can reach n or 2n. In a connected k = 4 core where every agent has four goods, β = 3n − m + 1, hence e = 2n + 1 − β *[derived]*. Mahara covers β ≥ 2n − 2, Bratby's unrefereed claim adds β = 2n − 3, and the project's K4.MC7 covers β ≤ 4 ([LEDGER.md](../LEDGER.md)). **For such cores the window 5 ≤ β ≤ 2n − 3 is covered by no refereed or certified result, and it is non-empty from n = 5 on.** The agent side holds the dense end and the project the sparse end. The agent-count side meets the same boundary. K4.R3–K4.R5 certify every connected k = 4 core with n ≤ 4, and every n = 5 core with at most two four-good agents. K4.MC0 shows that a minimal counterexample has n ≥ 5, with at least three four-good agents if n = 5 ([LEDGER.md](../LEDGER.md)). **The first cell with few relevant goods that neither side settles is therefore n = 5, k = 4, with three or more four-good agents** *[derived]*.

The only junction available today is the immediate one. Under EFX₀, agents in different components of the relevance graph value nothing in each other's bundles ([PROMPT.md](../PROMPT.md), L6). Each component can therefore be solved by whichever theorem covers it, and the goods nobody values are then handed to a source of the envy graph. The project's scouting notes already do this with Mahara, the multigraph theorem and AFM ([k4/SCOUT.md](../k4/SCOUT.md)).

Underneath all of this the two sides are dual *[derived]*. At fixed k, an agent's additive valuation matters only through the order of the subset sums of its relevant goods. A k = 3 core agent has six rankings and a four-good agent 288 strict types (K4.R3, [LEDGER.md](../LEDGER.md)). So **the relevance side is a finite-type problem over unboundedly many agents**, a combinatorial statement about typed hypergraphs quantified over all n. **The agent side fixes n and faces a continuum of values**, which is why AFM must cover valuation polytopes while the project enumerates cores and profiles.

## Graph theorems settle two valuers per good and leave (4, 3) as the first open cell

For goods with at most two valuers the picture is nearly complete. Christodoulou, Fiat, Koutsoupias and Sgouritsa prove polynomial-time EFX₀ on graphs for monotone valuations ([CFKS](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/elias.koutsoupias/Personal/Papers/fair-allocations-on-graphs.pdf)); in their model each good is relevant to at most two agents and each pair shares at most one good. **Afshinmehr, Ashuri, Mahmoudkhan, Mehlhorn and Shahrezaei prove polynomial-time EFX₀ on all multigraphs for cancelable, hence additive, valuations** ([arXiv 2606.18665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18665); listed as EC 2026 in the references of [Lianeas et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03171)). An independent EC 2026 proof by Christodoulou, Mastrakoulis, Sgouritsa and Sotiriou is reported but could not be found *[unverified]*. For monotone valuations, existence is known on bipartite multigraphs ([Afshinmehr–Danaei–Kazemi–Mehlhorn–Rathi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17002)), multi-trees ([Bhaskar–Pandit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06513)) and triangle-free multigraphs ([Afshinmehr–Ashuri–Mahmoudkhan–Mehlhorn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21644)). It is also known on multigraphs with girth ≥ 6 among non-parallel edges, or with at most ⌈n/4⌉ − 1 neighbours per agent ([Sgouritsa–Sotiriou](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09777)). Monotone multigraphs with triangles remain open; two groups conjecture that EFX exists there. Ordinary EFX is trivial on multigraphs by pairwise cut-and-choose, so "the difficulty is EFX₀" ([Sgouritsa–Sotiriou](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09777)).

These proofs share one skeleton. A first phase builds a partial EFX₀ *orientation*, giving every allocated good to one of its valuers; the triangle-free authors state that this phase applies "to any multi-graph instance" and that they use "the triangle-free condition only in the final phase" ([2512.21644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21644)). A final phase dumps the leftover goods on an unenvied agent who values them at zero, and the graph hypothesis certifies that the dumped goods stay invisible to their valuers. The canonical dump target, "the agent who envies i", exists because **in an EFX orientation of a multigraph any vertex is envied by at most one neighbour** ([2410.17002, Lemma 2.10](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17002)), and that is a fact about p = 2. When a good has three valuers, its holder can be envied by two agents, and this is precisely the step a hypergraph extension must replace *[derived]*.

Two papers already contain the needed "two parking spots" devices. The bipartite authors diagnose the general failure (two adjacent envied agents with no allocated shared good) and suggest requiring |S_i ∩ S_j| ≥ 2 ([2410.17002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17002)). Lianeas et al. park leftovers on two auxiliary unenvied agents j₁ and j₂ ([2608.03171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03171)). A *two-envier dump rule* is the natural generalisation to p = 3, and no paper attempts it *[spec]*. The project's (k, p) table names **(4, 3) as the first open cell**, because k ≤ 3 is settled for every p by this project and p ≤ 2 for every k by the multigraph theorem ([pq_bounded.md](../proofs/pq_bounded.md)). The ledger has evidence (EVIDENCE status only) that the cell is easier than TARGET₄: bounded LB₄ʳ never fails on k = 4 cores with p ≤ 3, a class that excludes every member of the hard family H_t (row K4.P3, [LEDGER.md](../LEDGER.md)).

The only exact results with p ≥ 3 are two monotone theorems of Lianeas, Sgouritsa and Sotiriou ([2608.03171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03171), AAAI-26). Hypergraphs of girth ≥ 4 admit EFX₀ allocations in polynomial time (Theorem 1). A multi-hypergraph version (Theorem 2) needs some vertex whose incident edges have multiplicity at most the edge size minus 2. A full reading of the proof suggests a free strengthening *[derived]*. In Theorem 1's proof, girth ≥ 4 (Observation 2.2) is used only in Lemma 3.4, and only for two neighbours of the pre-chosen parking vertex 0 that lie in different edges through 0. Every other step uses only linearity (two vertices share at most one edge) or the priority order. If this reading holds, the proof gives EFX₀ for **every linear hypergraph with at least one vertex on no Berge 3-cycle**, and degree plays no role anywhere.

The strengthening reaches few project cores, though. A core's private goods form repeated singleton edges, so Theorem 1, which needs a simple hypergraph, does not apply, and Theorem 2 forces vertex 0 to have no private goods *[derived]*. A cheap test remains worthwhile: does some vertex of H_t avoid all Berge triangles? H_t is linear, with p = 4 and Berge girth 3 ([pq_bounded.md](../proofs/pq_bounded.md)). Everything else with p ≥ 3 is approximate or partial: EF2X, √2/2-EFX, or partial EFX₀ discarding at most ⌊n/2⌋ − 1 goods ([Kaviani–Seddighin–Shahrezaei](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05139); [Kakatelis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26948)). Viswanathan–Mehta's degree-bounded result proves only ordinary EFX, is published as a three-page sketch, and hands leftover goods "to an arbitrary agent" ([AAMAS PDF](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p2534.pdf)).

Sgouritsa and Sotiriou's neighbour bound becomes a degree bound for p ≤ 2 *[derived; two checks needed]*. Model each private good as an edge parallel to an existing one, worth zero to its other endpoint. Then an agent with at most k relevant goods has at most k neighbours, and their Theorem 2 gives **monotone EFX₀ for every multigraph instance with k ≤ ⌈n/4⌉ − 1, i.e. n ≥ 4k + 1**. That would leave monotone p ≤ 2 open only for n ≤ 4k with triangles. Two checks are needed: that their model allows incident edges of zero marginal value, and that neighbours are counted in the multigraph as given. For additive valuations this adds nothing, since the multigraph theorem already covers p ≤ 2.

Orientations show that zero-valued placements are unavoidable. CFKS's four-agent K4 instance has no EFX orientation, and deciding orientability is NP-complete ([CFKS](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/elias.koutsoupias/Personal/Papers/fair-allocations-on-graphs.pdf)). K4 is 3-regular, so orientation-only strategies fail already at three goods per agent, at least for that instance's valuations *[derived]*. The literature places zero-valued goods by one of four devices: a pre-designated sink that is never envied (Lianeas, by lowest priority); safe sets (CFKS; 2410.17002); the unique envier (a p = 2 device); and an envy-graph source followed by a cycle rotation (Bhaskar–Pandit). The project's junk rule L3 is the last device in its simplest form. With degree at most k but p unbounded, invisibility cannot come from structure and must come from value budgets, which is how the project's k = 3 proof already works *[derived]*. The pre-designated sink looks like the most portable of the four *[spec]*.

## Value classes and the one counterexample bracket where k = 4 could fail

Several value classes are easy for every k:

- **Binary instances:** every maximum-Nash-welfare allocation is EFX₀ ([Amanatidis–Birmpas–Filos-Ratsikas–Hollender–Voudouris](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09838), TCS 2021).
- **Personalized bivalued additive valuations**, with values in {a_i, b_i} and b_i = 0 allowed: EFX₀ exists ([Byrka–Malinka–Ponitka](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14957)).
- **Binary marginals, non-additive:** EFX₀ exists and is computable in polynomial time ([Bu–Song–Yu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05503)).
- **Identical monotone valuations:** EFX₀ exists ([Plaut–Roughgarden](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04769)).
- **Lexicographic preferences:** "an allocation is EFX if and only if each envied agent gets exactly one good". One round of serial dictatorship, with leftover goods passed to unenvied agents, produces exactly the EFX and Pareto-optimal allocations ([Hosseini–Sikdar–Vaish–Xia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07680)).

Two consequences follow for the project *[derived]*. First, EFX₀ is unchanged when one agent's values are rescaled, so an agent who values all its relevant goods equally is effectively binary. An instance of such "flat" agents therefore has an EFX₀ allocation for every k, and **a counterexample to TARGET₄ needs at least one agent whose relevant goods take two or more distinct positive values**. Second, the lexicographic shape is the extreme case of the project's top-heavy agent. This supports peeling such agents by serial dictatorship before reaching the balanced core, as rule R1 does.

The only counterexample is dense. Akrami et al.'s three-agent, eight-good instance gives every singleton the value 1 ([AMM+ v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18216), preprint), so every agent values all eight goods. In the project's terms it is a single three-vertex hyperedge of multiplicity 8, with k = 8, p = 3 and infinite Berge girth *[derived]*. Their extensions to n ≥ 3 and m ≥ n + 5 keep every agent's degree at 8 or more. Monotone EFX₀ holds at k ≤ 2 ([citations.md](../proofs/citations.md), mrd-efx `MRDMono`). So **the smallest k at which monotone EFX₀ can fail lies in {3, …, 8}, and no source settles any of k = 3, …, 7 either way.** Because m ≤ 7 is safe at n = 3, a three-agent monotone counterexample with k ≤ 7 would need m ≥ 8, with every agent ignoring at least one good *[derived]*. The same instance shows that the multiplicity hypothesis of Lianeas et al.'s Theorem 2 cannot simply be dropped for monotone valuations: the girth is infinite, the multiplicity 8 exceeds |e| − 2 = 1, and no EFX allocation exists *[derived]*. Related negatives narrow the remaining routes. Even the agent-by-agent disjunction "EFX or MMS" can fail with three agents and eight submodular goods ([Hosseini–Khurana–Pathak–Vaish](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10397), preprint, abstract read). A preprint also claims an additive instance with all values positive that has no Pareto-optimal EFX allocation ([Mahabadi–Ramakrishnan–Tarnawski](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29201), abstract read).

**Arbitrary k is the additive EFX conjecture itself** *[derived]*. Every instance has |R_i| ≤ m, so "TARGET_k for every k" is exactly additive EFX₀ existence. By the perturbation lemma, for each fixed (n, m) that is additive EFX on positive instances ([PROMPT.md](../PROMPT.md), L1). Monotone EFX₀ fails at k = 8, so any argument that works for every k must use additivity, or at least cancelability. Structural theorems valid for all monotone valuations (Lianeas; triangle-free; trees) can at best reach small k unless they are specialised. The excess ladder collapses at m = n + ω(1), but no comparable collapse is known for the relevance ladder.

Computer-assisted proof is now the leading method on the agent side. Akrami et al. found their counterexample by SAT/SMT, "the first use of SAT/SMT solving for EFX". SPASS proved their three-agent, seven-good formula unsatisfiable, DRAT-trim checked the proof, and the soundness of the encoding is proved in Lean ([AMM+ v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18216)). AFM certify their (4, ≤ 9) result with an independent certifier ([AFM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08590)). No SAT search restricted to degree-bounded additive instances appears in the literature apart from the project's own. Several recent entries are AI-assisted or AI-generated: AFM, Bratby, and a strong-orientability characterisation "discovered by AI, with human intervention" ([Zeng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36498)). Their claims deserve the same independent checking the project applies to its own.

## Outside fields offer an exact dual and a value-free search pattern, but no proof

Most cross-field sources below were read at abstract, snippet or secondary level, and their statements are given at that depth. The best fit comes from matroid theory, and the derivation that follows is entirely the researchers' own *[derived]*. The open step in the project's serial-dictatorship programme is to fix an order and then complete its picks ([k4/sdc.md](../k4/sdc.md)). Fix an order π, its picks, an absorber o and the junk J (the unpicked goods). Every other agent has free slots, and the junk goods that can be matched into acceptable slots form a **transversal matroid M_T**. For each frozen agent i, let D_i be the relevant goods of i that could end up with the absorber, so |D_i| ≤ 3. The absorber must not contain a danger set: a subset T ⊆ D_i with at least two goods and v_i(T) > v_i(X_i).

The family of absorber-safe subsets of D_i is a matroid in every case but one. Write D_i = {x ≥ y ≥ z} and let h be the agent's holding value. The exception is when the minimal danger sets are {x, y} and {x, z}, which happens iff v(x) + v(z) > h ≥ v(y) + v(z). Then the maximal safe sets {x} and {y, z} differ in size, so they cannot be the bases of a matroid. It arises only at k = 4, for an agent that holds one good and has three relevant goods unheld. At k = 3 every frozen agent has |D_i| ≤ 2, so **the per-agent constraint is always a matroid**, which fits the k = 3 proof being a counting argument.

Suppose the D_i are pairwise disjoint and no agent is in the exceptional case. Then by the matroid union theorem a one-absorber completion exists iff, for every S ⊆ J,

  Σ_i (|S ∩ D_i| − r_i(S ∩ D_i)) ≤ r_T(S),

where r_i is the rank function of agent i's matroid on D_i and r_T that of M_T. Edmonds' algorithm tests this in polynomial time and returns a violating set S when the test fails.

The formula is the project's naive deficit with three corrections: a maximum over subsets, matching rank in place of a raw slot count, and per-agent ranks. Those corrections target the two ways the naive deficit fails on the 690 outcomes of the screen set. **72 outcomes with deficit ≤ 0 do not extend, because unwanted agents need their own slots; 38 with deficit > 0 do extend, because frozen agents share goods or one is unfrozen** ([k4/sdc.md](../k4/sdc.md)). The second mode is also where the derivation stops being exact. Overlapping D_i make the admissible family an intersection of matroids. And an agent that receives junk changes its own danger family, a coupling between M_T and the absorber constraint that the derivation ignores.

The project's Lemma K′ already gives an exact *primal* criterion for an owner completion of a fixed pre-allocation, and it handles that coupling: it is an "iff" quantified over the kept set K and over services ([k4/rulef.md](../k4/rulef.md)). **The matroid view would add the dual**: a polynomial test and a named violating set S. Such a set is the object open item 30 asks for, namely a description of the obstruction to completing a fixed order and of the reordering it forces ([LEDGER.md](../LEDGER.md)).

For the overlapping case, the matching tools offer sufficient conditions only. Meshulam's topological Hall theorem and the Aharoni–Berger theorem on a matroid intersected with a simplicial complex apply directly ([Wikipedia survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall-type_theorems_for_hypergraphs), secondary; the Aharoni–Berger statement is recalled, *[unverified]*). Independent transversals fit protection. Haxell's theorem says parts of size 2Δ suffice in a graph of maximum degree Δ, and this is tight ([Haxell–Mittal–Zhao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09515), abstract level). The project's data suggest why it applies. **For 63 of 63 hard exposed agents on the screen set, and 297 of 298 on 5,000 random n = 4 profiles, a single good meets every danger set** ([k4/sdc.md](../k4/sdc.md)). Protection then means choosing one conflict-free key placement per agent, an independent-transversal problem that Haxell's theorem solves whenever each agent has at least 2Δ options *[spec]*. Drisko-type rainbow-matching theorems give a similar sufficient condition, with slack of a factor of two. Like Haxell's, they ignore the cases where one placement protects several agents ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_matching), snippet level).

Haxell's alternating trees carry the most promising pattern for *choosing* the order. As stated, the hypergraph Hall condition is too strong: with acceptable bundles of five or six goods it needs a cover number τ ≥ (2r − 3)(|Y₀| − 1) + 1, while |Y₀| agents with at most four relevant goods have τ ≤ 4|Y₀| ([Wikipedia survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall-type_theorems_for_hypergraphs)). The proof is what transfers. In restricted Santa Claus it became an algorithm (Asadpour–Feige–Saberi), was made polynomial (Annamalai–Kalaitzis–Svensson) and was lifted to matroids ([Davies–Rothvoss–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07189)). It swaps a matched player's bundle while keeping the player matched. Crucially, it terminates by **a lexicographic signature of the alternating tree, not by agents' values**, which is exactly the kind of search P1 demands *[spec]*. P1 rules out only value-monotone potentials over partial EFX₀ allocations. Three families survive it: potentials over pre-allocations or bases (LB⁺ at k = 3 is one), Haxell-style signatures, and potentials over complete allocations as in Akrami et al.

The remaining imports fail, each for a specific reason:

- **Rainbow cycle number.** It bounds leftover goods through Pareto-dominating swaps ([CGMMM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01628)), so it cannot complete an allocation. With bounded relevance it would leave O(min_d(kn/d + R(d))) goods, which with Sivashankar's preprint bound R(d) < e·d is O(√(kn)) *[spec; our arithmetic]*.
- **Sperner, KKM and consensus halving.** These round a fractional division at boundary items ([Igarashi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.01348)). That gives EF1-type guarantees with no control over *which* good closes the envy, and EFX₀ needs exactly that control.
- **Etesami's fixed-point reformulation** proves existence only conditionally ([Etesami](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04915)).
- **Scarf's lemma.** It yields fractional outcomes, and finding one is PPAD-complete ([Kintali et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.1601), snippet level). Rounding them leaves k − 1 over-allocation ([Nguyen–Peivandi–Vohra](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jetheo:v:165:y:2016:i:c:p:209-241)).
- **Coalition stability.** Empty cores in multi-unit housing markets with additive preferences ([Konishi–Quint–Wako](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/mateco/v35y2001i1p1-15.html)) warn against any core-style argument.
- **Markets and welfare.** Market arguments deliver EF1 together with Pareto optimality ([Barman–Krishnamurthy–Vaish](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04731)). But EFX and Pareto optimality can be incompatible once zeros are allowed ([Plaut–Roughgarden](https://www.timroughgarden.org/papers/efx.pdf)), and maximum Nash welfare is EFX only for at most two distinct values ([Amanatidis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09838)).
- **Local lemma and discrepancy.** Both need slack EFX₀ lacks. The number of agents valuing a good is unbounded, so dependencies are unbounded, and an error of ±1 good is precisely what EFX₀ cannot absorb *[derived]*.

Computational logic contributes an architecture rather than a lemma. Tang and Lin proved Arrow-type theorems by an inductive lemma plus a finite base case checked by SAT ([Tang–Lin](https://cse.hkust.edu.hk/faculty/flin/papers/arrow_aij.pdf), snippet level). Heule's Schur-number-five proof shows the scale that DRAT certificates and cube-and-conquer reach ([Heule](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08076), snippet level). For the project the analogue is a discharging proof *[spec]*. The project already has the reducible configurations (peeling, the core reduction, the minimal-counterexample rows) and the counting identity Σ_g deg(g) = Σ_i |R_i|. It lacks an unavoidability lemma. Counterexample-guided synthesis of the order rule could also replace the manual "rule fails on instance X" loop. That is a 2QBF search for a rule that works on every core, with the existing SAT checker as verifier *[spec]*. No fair-division paper was found that uses discharging, kernels, Scarf's lemma or popular matchings for EFX.

## Conclusion

The literature changes how the project should see itself. The relevance ladder is not a variant of the agent ladder but its finite-type dual, and both now stop at four. The agent side's tools fail for two separable reasons: perturbation erases the zeros, and progress is measured by agents' values. The project has independently reached the agent side's own walls, P1 mirroring CGM's Section 5 and the failure of fixed-priority potentials at n = 4 mirroring CGM+21. The useful imports are therefore local devices rather than theorems: Mahara's count of few large bundles, the two-parking-spot dumps, the never-envied sink, and the fact that EFX₀ acceptability depends on at most k goods and one bit. The gaps are now located precisely. They are the window 5 ≤ β ≤ 2n − 3 for cores whose agents all hold four goods, the cell n = 5 with k = 4, the (4, 3) cell, and the monotone threshold between 3 and 8.

For closing k = 4 the implied programme has three parts. It needs an exact obstruction for a fixed order, for which matroid union supplies the dual. It needs a termination argument that does not track values, for which Haxell's trees are the one proven pattern in a neighbouring problem. And it needs a way to place goods with three or more valuers, for which the two-envier dump is the natural extension of the multigraph proofs. For arbitrary k the message is sharper. "All k" is the additive EFX conjecture, and monotone valuations already fail at k = 8, so no argument that ignores additivity can get there. The one unexplored statement for all k that falls short of the full conjecture is a charity bound that depends only on k.

## Ranked research directions

The ranking below is our judgement. Each direction is ranked by its expected contribution to closing TARGET₄, or to a statement for all k, discounted by risk; cost breaks ties. Costs are rough guesses, not measurements, and directions 1, 6 and 8 are cheap enough to run in parallel with anything else.

| Rank | Direction | What it would prove | Rough cost | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Matroid dual of fixed-order completion | an exact, polynomial-time obstruction certificate (disjoint regime) | days | derived |
| 2 | The (4, 3) cell via a two-envier dump | TARGET₄ when every good has ≤ 3 valuers | months | devices from the literature, K4.P3 evidence, spec |
| 3 | Termination that does not track values | the choice-of-order step, hence TARGET₄ | open-ended | spec |
| 4 | Monotone k = 3 and the monotone threshold | monotone TARGET, or a sparse monotone counterexample | weeks | derived |
| 5 | Charity bound depending only on k | a theorem for all k, short of the conjecture | open-ended | spec |
| 6 | Verify two free strengthenings; test H_t | two remarks on published theorems | days | derived readings |
| 7 | Discharging plus SAT; extend K4.MC7 to β = 5 | a finite reduction of TARGET₄; a narrower β window | weeks–months | spec, methods from the literature |
| 8 | Flat-agent and twin reductions | a mixed class; a smaller search | days | literature, spec |

**1. Dualize the fixed-order completion with matroid union.**

- *Would prove:* an exact Hall-type criterion for one-absorber completion of a fixed serial-dictatorship order, in Conjecture OC₁'s setting or at a Lemma K′ state. It is decidable in polynomial time, returns a violating set S of junk goods, and is exact whenever the frozen agents' danger domains are disjoint and no agent has minimal danger sets {xy, xz}.
- *Why it might work:* at k = 3 the per-agent constraint is always a matroid, the formula corrects both recorded failure modes of the naive deficit, and Lemma K′ already settles the primal side exactly ([k4/rulef.md](../k4/rulef.md)).
- *Main risk:* the 38 deficit-positive outcomes that extend are exactly the overlap and unfreezing cases where matroid structure breaks, so the dual may be exact only where the problem was already easy. The strict-containment clause and an absorber's own pick inside a danger set are also unmodelled, and the derivation has not been checked against `k4/sdc/sdclib.py`.
- *First step:* implement the matroid-union test on the screen set's R1 outcomes, compare it with SAT, and tabulate the disagreements by overlap, by the exceptional configuration, and by coupling.

**2. Prove TARGET₄ on the (4, 3) cell with a two-envier dump.**

- *Would prove:* TARGET₄ for every instance whose goods have at most three valuers each. This is the first open cell of the (k, p) table and a publishable intermediate between the multigraph theorem and TARGET₄, and it excludes every H_t.
- *Why it might work:* with p = 3 a held good has at most two enviers, and the literature already contains two two-parking-spot devices (2410.17002's |S_i ∩ S_j| ≥ 2; Lianeas's j₁, j₂). K4.P3 reports no failure of bounded LB₄ʳ in the cell, and champion edges drawn only from valuers would localise the analysis of stuck states.
- *Main risk:* p ≤ 2 enters the multigraph proof in several places, including the pair classes, the orientation invariant and the case analysis A–H ([multigraph_digest.md](../proofs/multigraph_digest.md)). Even monotone p = 2 with triangles is open, so the argument must be additive-specific. The independent EC 2026 proof, which might hold transferable ideas, is unavailable.
- *First step:* map line by line where p ≤ 2 enters 2606.18665, then test a two-envier dump rule, or LB₄ʳ with bounded rotations, as the proof target on the (4, 3) cores.

**3. Base the choice of order on a termination argument that does not track values.**

- *Would prove:* the step "obstruction → better order" in SD-C/OC₁, and hence TARGET₄. When the fixed-order test fails, its tight set S names a frozen agent to insert earlier. The loop then terminates by a Haxell-style lexicographic signature over successive tight sets, or by a potential over bases or over complete partitions.
- *Why it might work:* P1 forbids only value-monotone potentials over partial EFX₀ allocations. LB⁺ at k = 3 is already a potential over bases, Haxell's augmenting tree is a proven pattern in a neighbouring allocation problem, and partition-space potentials are the agent-side family that is still advancing.
- *Main risk:* this is the most speculative direction. Partition-space arguments have not gone beyond n = 3–4 on the agent side, the project's fixed-priority test already fails for some orders (instance Q), and Haxell's quantitative slack does not hold here.
- *First step:* once direction 1 yields certificates, prototype the re-insertion loop on the suite and measure whether any signature strictly increases.

**4. Settle monotone k = 3 and pin the monotone threshold.**

- *Would prove:* either EFX₀ for monotone valuations with at most three relevant goods per agent, or a monotone counterexample with k ≤ 7. The first would align the project with the structural literature, which is entirely monotone. The second would be a new non-existence result showing additivity is essential.
- *Why it might work:* under monotone valuations EFX₀ still depends only on each agent's weak order on the 2^k subsets of its relevant goods. The problem therefore stays finite-type, and the project's type-enumeration and CEGAR tooling applies *[derived]*. The proofs of peeling rules R1, R2 and the junk rule L3, as stated in the brief, appear to use only monotonicity *[derived, to check]*. Akrami et al.'s m ≤ 7 theorem and their SAT method give a base.
- *Main risk:* the six-ranking ordinal reduction L5 and LB⁺ use additivity (a < b + c fixes the order of pair sums). Monotone cores therefore have many more types, and the k = 3 proof may not transfer. A counterexample search at n = 3, m = 8, with each agent ignoring a good, needs a new encoding over 2^8 subsets per agent.
- *First step:* enumerate the monotone strict types of a three-good agent, adapt the k = 4 certifier, and rerun the n ≤ 5 certification.

**5. Seek a charity bound that depends only on k.**

- *Would prove:* for every k, an EFX₀ allocation leaving at most f(k) unallocated goods, each valued by someone and none envied, with f(3) = 0 by TARGET. This is the relevance analogue of the t − 2 bound for t valuation types, and the only route in sight to a theorem for all k that falls short of the full conjecture.
- *Why it might work:* types already give a charity bound independent of n, and relevance is likewise independent of n. BCFF's generalized champion edges free whole sets along cycles, and Mahara's n − 2 machinery holds for general monotone valuations.
- *Main risk:* those proofs run on perturbed instances that cannot see zeros. P1 shows a Pareto-maximal partial allocation can be stuck with a single leftover, so f(k) must come from an argument that does not track values. Rainbow-cycle bounds depend on n.
- *First step:* rewrite Mahara's n − 2 argument for instances with genuine zeros, and locate where the count n − 2 could be replaced by a function of k.

**6. Verify the two free strengthenings and test H_t.**

- *Would prove:* two short remarks on published theorems. One is Lianeas et al.'s Theorem 1 for every linear hypergraph with a vertex on no Berge triangle (monotone EFX₀, polynomial time). The other is Sgouritsa–Sotiriou's monotone EFX₀ for p ≤ 2 whenever n ≥ 4k + 1. It would also settle whether the Lianeas algorithm handles the project's hard family H_t.
- *Why it might work:* full readings found each hypothesis used at a single point.
- *Main risk:* the readings may be wrong, private goods keep most cores out of reach, and for additive valuations the multigraph theorem already covers p ≤ 2.
- *First step:* re-check every use of Observation 2.2 (Lemmas 3.2–3.5 and 4.5–4.8) and Sgouritsa–Sotiriou's treatment of zero-marginal incident edges in Claim 3.13, then implement the algorithm and run it on H_t.

**7. Assemble a discharging-plus-SAT architecture and extend K4.MC7 to β = 5.**

- *Would prove:* a reduction of TARGET₄ to a finite unavoidable set of configurations, each certified by SAT with DRAT/LRAT proofs or an independent certifier. Separately, TARGET₄ at β = 5, where K4.MC4 bounds a minimal counterexample by n ≤ 12; that would narrow the window 5 ≤ β ≤ 2n − 3 from below.
- *Why it might work:* the project already has the reducible configurations and the counting identity, and certified SAT is the field's leading method.
- *Main risk:* there is no unavoidability lemma. Profile counts grow like 288^n: one β = 4 core ran for an hour without finishing ([LEDGER.md](../LEDGER.md), item 16). And synthesized order rules may overfit small n, as rule F did on HH_t.
- *First step:* catalogue the existing k = 4 reductions as reducible configurations, then try a charge rule built on Σ_g deg(g) = Σ_i |R_i|.

**8. Turn flat agents and twins into reductions.**

- *Would prove:* EFX₀ when every agent either has at most three relevant goods or is flat, a class unbounded in k for the flat agents, together with reduction rules that shrink the k = 4 search.
- *Why it might work:* binary instances are EFX₀ for every k under maximum Nash welfare. Identical agents can be grouped (leximin++; the "leading agent" of [Ghosal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10632)), and two core agents with the same relevant set and ranking form such a group.
- *Main risk:* maximum Nash welfare is a global optimum while LB⁺ is a serial dictatorship, and combining two different global structures may be as hard as the general case. Flat agents are tied, so they also need the tie reduction K4.TIE.
- *First step:* run the k = 4 certifier on profiles whose four-good agents are flat, and test whether twins can be merged without losing completability.
