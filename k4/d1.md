## 3. Theorem D1-C (proof)

**Setting.** As in `k4/oc1_hall.md` §21. Y = SD(π) has no one-absorber completion (a *hard* start), D = {k}, and
every good is relevant to some agent (true in cores). P = (k = x₀, …, x_t = z) is a canonical chain, a = Y_{x_{t−1}},
Π = J ∪ B_z, P_k = Π ∩ R_k, and M is the family of inclusion-minimal improving subsets of P_k (nonempty by
Proposition 3′). For H₀ ∈ M, B′(H₀) is the state after P, with owner k. U is the set of agents off P that are
unfrozen at the picks (pickless agents included). y_z = Y_z.

**Theorem D1-C (K4.OCH.D1C).** If P has no HEAD, some H₀ ∈ M makes C(B′(H₀), k) hold, so B′(H₀) has an S\*
completion owned by k. In particular, sub-lemma D1-C of §21 holds: a failing CROWD-only chain is repaired by another
minimal H₀′ on the same chain.

Combined with Lemma D1-red, **Finding D1 reduces to the starts at which every canonical chain from k has HEAD**
(§5).

### 3.1 Tools

Write "h is *robust* for i in Q" when i holds one good y_i and v_i(y_i) + v_i(h) ≥ v_i(Q ∖ {h}).

**Fact R (k ≤ 4).** Let i hold one good y_i, and let Q be a set of at most three goods, each worth at most v_i(y_i)
to i. Then every h ∈ Q is robust for i in Q, except possibly the *strict minimum* of Q: the good worth less to i than
each of the two others (only when |Q| = 3).

*Proof.* If |Q| ≤ 2, then v_i(y_i) ≥ v_i(Q ∖ {h}). If Q = {h, p, q} and v_i(h) ≥ min(v_i(p), v_i(q)), then
v_i(y_i) + v_i(h) ≥ max + min = v_i(p) + v_i(q). ∎

**Fact K.** A set S does not threaten k at p_k if S ∩ R_k is not improving, since v_k(S ∖ h) ≤ v_k(S ∩ R_k) ≤ p_k.

**Tool T (a completion of the picks; Proposition 3′ with helpers).** Let o be unfrozen at the picks, W_o = J ∪ B_o,
and H ⊆ J such that
- (i) W_o ∖ H does not threaten k at p_k, and
- (ii) the goods of H have distinct helpers: unfrozen agents i_h ≠ o with a pick, valuing h, with h robust for i_h in
  W_o ∩ R_{i_h}.

Then the picks have an S\* completion with owner o. At a hard start this is impossible.

*Proof.* Lemma 2\* at the picks with owner o.
- (G1): every agent other than o holds at most one good. (G2): by (I2); pickless agents value no junk.
- (H1): a frozen q ≠ k values no junk (D), so W_o ∩ R_q ⊆ B_o is at most one good, and no subset of W_o ∖ H
  threatens q. For k this is (i). There are no multi-good holders.
- No holder is non-movable: nobody wants B_o (o is unfrozen), and |B_o| ≤ 1.
- By Lemma 4 the helpers are admissible, so they form a system of distinct admissible holders for H. ∎

For a helper i ≠ o with a pick, every good of W_o ∩ R_i is worth at most p_i to i: junk by (I2), Y_o because o is
unfrozen. And |W_o ∩ R_i| ≤ 3 because y_i ∈ R_i ∖ W_o. So Fact R applies to helpers. It also applies at B′ to a
holder u off P (its base is its pick, Π ∩ R_u is worth at most p_u by (I2) and (U)), and to the head z when it is a
holder at B′ (Π ∩ R_z is worth at most p_z < v_z(a)). For u off P with a pick, Q_u := Π ∩ R_u, and Rob(u) is the
set of goods robust for u in Q_u. This is the same at B′ and at the picks with owner z, since W_z = Π.

**Lemma O (options).** Let u be crowded at B′(H₀). Then u is z or an agent of U with a pick (Lemma D1-red 3; a
middle agent or a pickless agent values at most one good of Π ⊆ J ∪ {y_z}). Its only possible compensation good is
the at most one good of (Π ∩ R_u) ∖ H₀ (three goods at most, two of them in H₀). Call that good an *option* if it is
robust for u in Π ∩ R_u. A holder with an option is safe with it against every X_k ⊆ Π ∖ {e}.
- (a) If u ∈ U has no option, every good of H₀ ∩ Q_u is robust for u (Fact R: a non-robust leftover good is the strict
  minimum). If u has an option, Rob(u) contains all goods of Q_u but at most one.
- (b) If u = z and y_z ∉ H₀, then y_z is an option. Indeed H₀ ∩ R_z is two junk goods g₁, g₂, so Π ∩ R_z =
  {y_z, g₁, g₂}, and v_z(a) + v_z(y_z) > v_z(g₁) + v_z(g₂) because v_z(a) > p_z ≥ v_z(g_i).
- If the crowded holders of B′(H₀) have distinct options, C(B′(H₀), k) holds (Lemma D1-red 4, with H the set of
  options; an option is admissible).

From now on assume **no single good is improving** (otherwise H₀ = {g} crowds nobody and repairs by Lemma D1-red).

### 3.2 The head

**Lemma C3.** If z is crowded at B′(H₀) and y_z ∈ H₀, then U = ∅.

*Proof.* Take g ∈ H₀ ∩ R_z ∩ J. Suppose o ∈ U, and apply Tool T with owner o, H = {g} and helper z.
- z is unfrozen at the picks and values g. W_o ∩ R_z ⊆ R_z ∖ {a, y_z} has at most two goods, so g is robust for z.
- (W_o ∖ {g}) ∩ R_k ⊆ (P_k ∖ {y_z, g}) ∪ (B_o ∩ R_k). Since R_k ⊇ {Y_k, y_z, g}, this is at most one good: a good of
  P_k or Y_o (worth at most p_k, since nobody wants Y_o). It is not improving, and Fact K gives (i). ∎

**Lemma HC (the crowded head has an option).** If z is crowded at B′(H₀), then z has an option.

*Proof.* If y_z ∉ H₀, this is Lemma O(b). Let y_z ∈ H₀. By Lemma C3, U = ∅: every agent other than k and z is frozen
at the picks and values no junk. Take g ∈ H₀ ∩ R_z ∩ J; then R_z ⊇ {a, y_z, g}, and Q_z := Π ∩ R_z has at most one
good e outside {y_z, g}.
- *If e ∈ H₀.* Then H₀ = Q_z = P_k = {y_z, g, e} (|P_k| ≤ 3), and no pair of P_k is improving. Every junk good is
  relevant to some agent, and only k and z value junk, so J ⊆ (R_k ∪ R_z) ∖ {Y_k, a, y_z} = {g, e} and Π = P_k ⊆ R_k.
  Then v_k(Π ∖ h) is a pair, at most p_k, so Π does not threaten k, and Tool T with owner z and H = ∅ completes the
  picks. Contradiction.
- *Otherwise* (e absent, or e ∉ H₀). Suppose z has no option: e is absent, or v_z(a) + v_z(e) < v_z(y_z) + v_z(g).
  Let x = x_{t−1} (x = k when t = 1). Consider the allocation A: x takes {a} ∪ (J ∖ {g}), z takes {y_z, g}, and every
  other agent keeps its pick. It gives every agent other than x at most one junk good. It is EFX₀:
  - *z's bundle {y_z, g}.* For i ≠ z, v_i(y_z) ≤ p_i by (U), and v_i(g) ≤ p_i by (I2) when i values g. So removing
    one good leaves at most p_i ≤ v_i(A_i).
  - *x's bundle, seen by z.* A_x ∩ R_z ⊆ {a, e}. Without e, the remainder after removing a good is worth at most
    v_z(a) < v_z(H₀ ∩ R_z) = v_z(y_z) + v_z(g) (z is crowded). With e, it is worth at most
    v_z(a) + v_z(e) < v_z(y_z) + v_z(g).
  - *x's bundle, seen by k* (t ≥ 2). A_x ∩ R_k ⊆ {a} ∪ (P_k ∖ {y_z, g}), and if a ∈ R_k then P_k = {y_z, g}. So it
    is at most one good, worth at most p_k (v_k(a) ≤ p_k by (π)).
  - *x's bundle, seen by any other agent i.* i is frozen at the picks and values no junk, so A_x ∩ R_i ⊆ {a}. Only z
    wants a: z is a holder at B′, hence unfrozen there, so a has no second wanter (Lemma D1-red 2). So
    v_i(a) ≤ p_i.
  - Bundles of one good threaten nobody.

  So A is a one-absorber completion of the picks (absorber x). Contradiction. ∎

**Corollary U0.** If U = ∅, every H₀ ∈ M repairs: its only possible crowded holder is z (Lemma O), which has an
option (Lemma HC).

### 3.3 The holders off the chain

Assume U ≠ ∅. By Lemma C3, z is crowded only by sets H₀ ∌ y_z, and then its option is y_z (Lemma O(b)). For X ∈ M
let A_X be the set of crowded holders of B′(X) in U (whether u ∈ A_X depends only on X and u). Each crowded holder
has at most one candidate good, so by Lemma O, **X fails** (no distinct options) only if
- (F1) some u ∈ A_X has no option; or
- (F2) two holders of A_X have the same option; or
- (F3) z is crowded by X, and some u ∈ A_X has the option y_z.

In each case A_X ≠ ∅. For u ∈ A_X, |X ∩ Q_u| ≥ 2, so by Fact R, Rob(u) contains a good of X.

**Case A: y_z ∉ R_k** (so P_k ⊆ J, and every good of Rob(u) ∩ P_k can be kept out with u as helper for owner z).
Tool T with owner z applies whenever some goods of P_k with distinct helpers in U leave a non-improving rest.

- *A-a, A-b, A-c: M has one member X* (P_k = X = {b, c}; or X = P_k = {b, c, d} with no improving pair; or
  X = {b, c} ⊊ P_k = {b, c, d}). If u ∈ A_X, take h ∈ Rob(u) ∩ X. Then P_k ∖ {h} contains no member of M, and
  Tool T(z, {h}) applies. So A_X = ∅, and X repairs (only z can be crowded, with option y_z).
- *A-d: M = {{b, c}, {b, d}}.* If some u ∈ U had b ∈ Rob(u), Tool T(z, {b}) would apply ({c, d} is not improving).
  So for u ∈ A_X, b is the strict minimum of Q_u = X ∪ {e_u}, and e_u is an option. No holder is stuck, so a
  failing X fails by (F2) or (F3). Members of A_{bc} help with c, members of A_{bd} with d; two distinct ones would
  give Tool T(z, {c, d}). So if both fail, A_{bc} = A_{bd} = {u}. Then Q_u = P_k, u's option for {b, c} is d ≠ y_z,
  and neither (F2) nor (F3) can occur for {b, c}. So one of the two repairs.
- *A-e: M = all three pairs of P_k = {b, c, d}.* Tool T(z, {h, h′}) applies to any two distinct goods with distinct
  helpers. Suppose all three pairs fail, let 𝒜 = A_{bc} ∪ A_{bd} ∪ A_{cd}, and R(u) = Rob(u) ∩ P_k ≠ ∅ for u ∈ 𝒜.
  Two members u ≠ u′ have distinct representatives unless R(u) = R(u′) = {h}.
  - If 𝒜 = {u}: u lies in all three A_X, so Q_u = P_k. (F2) is impossible, and (F3) needs the option P_k ∖ X to be
    y_z ∉ P_k. So u has no option for each of the three pairs: each of b, c, d is the strict minimum of Q_u.
    Impossible.
  - Otherwise R(u) = {h} for every u ∈ 𝒜. Then Q_u ⊉ P_k (else Fact R gives two robust goods of P_k), so u lies in
    A_X only for X = Q_u ∩ P_k ∋ h. But A_{P_k ∖ {h}} ≠ ∅. Impossible.

  So some pair repairs.

**Case B: y_z ∈ R_k.** Let T_k = P_k ∖ {y_z} ⊆ J. For o ∈ U, W_o ∩ R_k = T_k ∪ (B_o ∩ R_k).
- *|T_k| = 1, P_k = {y_z, c}, M = {P_k}.* An o ∈ U with B_o ∩ R_k = ∅ (pickless, or Y_o ∉ R_k) gives Tool T(o, ∅),
  since {c} is not improving. So every o ∈ U has Y_o ∈ R_k, and |R_k| ≤ 4 leaves U = {o}. If o is crowded and
  c ∈ Rob(o), Tool T(z, {c}) applies. Otherwise c is the strict minimum of Q_o = {y_z, c, e}, and e is an option.
  Either way P_k repairs: z is not crowded (Lemma C3), and o is the only other candidate.
- *|T_k| = 2, P_k = {y_z, b, c}, R_k = {Y_k, y_z, b, c}.* Now W_o ∩ R_k = {b, c} for every o ∈ U. If {b, c} were not
  improving, Tool T(o, ∅) would apply. So {b, c} ∈ M, and Tool T(o, {h}) applies to any h ∈ {b, c} with a helper
  i ≠ o, i ∈ U ∪ {z}.
  - *M = {{b, c}}.* For u ∈ A_{bc}, some h ∈ Rob(u) ∩ {b, c}, and P_k ∖ {h} is not improving: Tool T(z, {h}). So
    A_{bc} = ∅, and {b, c} repairs.
  - *M = {{b, c}, {b, y_z}}* (or the same with c). If some u ∈ U had b ∈ Rob(u), Tool T(z, {b}) would apply
    ({y_z, c} is not improving). So every u ∈ A_X has Q_u = X ∪ {e_u}, b its strict minimum and e_u an option, and
    R_u = {y_u} ∪ X ∪ {e_u} is full. A failure is then (F2) or (F3), and each gives Tool T:
    - (F2) with u₁ ≠ u₂ ∈ A_X and common option e: owner u₁, helper u₂. W_{u₁} ∩ R_{u₂} = (X ∪ {e}) ∖ {y_z},
      because R_{u₂} is full. If X = {b, c} and e ≠ y_z, it is {b, c, e}, and c is robust (b is the strict minimum):
      T(u₁, {c}). If X = {b, c} and e = y_z, it is {b, c}: T(u₁, {b}). If X = {b, y_z}, it is {b, e}: T(u₁, {b}).
    - (F3) with X = {b, c}: z values b and c, so R_z = {a, y_z, b, c}. Owner u (the holder with option y_z), helper
      z: W_u ∩ R_z = {b, c}, so T(u, {b}).

    So neither member of M fails.
  - *M = all three pairs.* If u ∈ U is crowded by some X ∈ M, then U = {u}. For o ∈ U ∖ {u}: if X = {b, c}, then
    W_o ∩ R_u ⊇ {b, c} has at most three goods, so one of b, c is robust; if X = {b, y_z}, then W_o ∩ R_u ⊆
    R_u ∖ {y_u, y_z} has at most two goods, so b is robust. Either way Tool T(o, {h}).
    - If no u ∈ U is crowded by any X, then every X repairs.
    - Otherwise U = {u}. The only possible crowded holder of {b, y_z} and of {c, y_z} is u. If both fail, u has no
      option for either, so u values y_z, b and c, Q_u = P_k, and both c (the leftover of {b, y_z}) and b (the
      leftover of {c, y_z}) are the strict minimum of Q_u. Impossible.

This covers every shape: with no improving single good and |P_k| ≤ 3, M is one pair, the triple, two pairs (which
share a good) or three pairs; in Case B with |T_k| = 2, the shapes without {b, c} are excluded above. ∎

*Remarks.*
1. **The duality, in general.** Every contradiction above is the (B3) duality of Theorem F1₁: a holder crowded at B′
   is a robust helper at the picks, because crowding means that it values two of k's pool goods, and with |R_u| ≤ 4
   at most one of its pool goods is not robust. The hard start forbids helpers, so it forbids crowding, except in
   the configurations where another member of M avoids it.
2. **Where k ≤ 4 is used.** |P_k| ≤ 3 (the shapes), |Π ∩ R_u| ≤ 3 (Fact R and the single option), |R_z| ≤ 4 (Lemmas
   O(b), C3, HC) and |R_k| ≤ 4 (Lemma C3, Case B).
3. **The absorber completion of Lemma HC** is not an S\* completion: the absorber x_{t−1} is frozen. It is a
   one-absorber completion, which is what hardness forbids. This is the only place where the proof uses more than
   "no S\* completion at the picks".
4. **No null goods.** The assumption that every good is relevant to some agent is used only in Lemma HC's first case.
