## 3. Chains of frozen goods: the f = 1 lemmas along need paths

Written proofs, refereed in the PR #82 review. Notation of `k4/dl2.md` §4 and `k4/dl13.md` §1: P is min-frozen with
ω ≥ 1, needed set 𝒩, frozen agents F, junk J; W_o = B_o ∪ J; θ_x(Z) = max_{h ∈ Z} v_x(Z ∖ h); Z *threatens* x holding B if
θ_x(Z) > v_x(B); 𝒩_{−S} := ⋃_{i ∉ S} N_i; V := Val*(P), and def(P) = ω + 2 − V (Lemma H1) when P has a free agent.
For a frozen y, g_y is its good (B_y = {g_y}). The proofs use only these definitions, Lemma H1 and (M1), (M2) of
`k4/dl2.md` §4.

**Fact 0 (u counts needed goods).** At a min-frozen P, y ↦ g_y is a bijection F → 𝒩 ((V): every needed good is the
whole base of one agent, which is then frozen, and a frozen agent's base is a needed good). So for a free o and any Z,

  u_o(Z) = #{q ∈ 𝒩 : q ∉ N_o(Z) ∪ 𝒩_{−o}},

the needed goods that only o needs and that o's needs from Z leave out. (This is the form used in the proof of
`k4/dl13.md` Lemma 12.)

**Fact 1 (upgrades are harmless).** If an agent w moves from a base B to a base B′ with v_w(B′) ≥ v_w(B), then
N_w(B′) ⊆ N_w(B) (M2), and every set that does not threaten w holding B does not threaten w holding B′.

### 3.1 Need paths and the chain swap

**Definition.** A *need path* to a frozen agent x is a sequence of distinct agents z = a_{k+1}, a_k, …, a_1, a_0 = x
(k ≥ 0) with a_1, …, a_k frozen, z free, and a_{i+1} needing g_{a_i} for i = 0, …, k (each agent needs the good of the
next). k is the number of frozen intermediaries; k = 0 means that z needs g_x. The *chain swap* along it, with a
helper h (a free agent, h ≠ z) or none: z takes {g_{a_k}}, each a_i (1 ≤ i ≤ k) takes {g_{a_{i−1}}}, x takes a set A,
h takes a set B′_h, everybody else keeps its base. G := J ∪ B_z ∪ B_h (B_h := ∅ without a helper) is the set of goods
the move frees.

**Lemma NP (need paths exist).** Let P be min-frozen and x ∈ F. Either some free agent has a need path to x, or the
agents that have a *need walk* to x (a sequence ending at x in which each agent needs the good of the next and every
agent but the first is frozen) are all frozen and include a cycle of the frozen need digraph (y → y′ iff y needs
g_{y′}). In particular, at a T4-optimal P (acyclic frozen need digraph, `k4/dl13.md` §2.2) every frozen agent has a
need path from a free agent.

*Proof.* Let S be the set of agents with a need walk to x (x ∈ S). If S contains a free agent, a shortest need walk from
a free agent to x has distinct agents, and every agent in it but the first is frozen (a walk extends only through the
good of a frozen agent): a need path. Otherwise S ⊆ F. Every y ∈ S has g_y ∈ 𝒩, so some agent needs g_y, and that agent
lies in S. So every vertex of S has an in-neighbour in S in the frozen need digraph; walking backwards inside the
finite set S repeats a vertex, which closes a cycle. ∎

**Lemma 6⁺ (the chain swap stays in the min-frozen class).** Let z, a_k, …, a_1, x be a need path, h a free agent
other than z or none, and A ⊆ G ∩ R_x, B′_h ⊆ G ∩ R_h disjoint sets of at most two goods each with N_x(A) ⊆ 𝒩 and
N_h(B′_h) ⊆ 𝒩 (*admissible*). Then the chain swap P′ is min-frozen, NA(P′) = 𝒩, F(P′) = (F ∖ {x}) ∪ {z},
J(P′) = G ∖ (A ∪ B′_h) and ω(P′) = ω. If h gives up a good of B_h (or there is no helper), P → P′ is a (T3⁺) move with
W = {a_1, …, a_k}; for k = 0 it is the role swap of `k4/dl2.md` Lemma 6.

*Proof.* Needs in P′. z needs g_{a_k} in P, so v_z(g_{a_k}) > v_z(B_z) and N_z({g_{a_k}}) ⊆ N_z(B_z) ⊆ 𝒩 (M2). For
1 ≤ i ≤ k, a_i needs g_{a_{i−1}}, so N_{a_i}({g_{a_{i−1}}}) ⊆ N_{a_i} ⊆ 𝒩. x and h by hypothesis; the other agents keep
their bases and needs. So NA(P′) ⊆ 𝒩. Every good of 𝒩 is a one-good base of P′: g_{a_0}, …, g_{a_k} are the bases of
a_1, …, a_k, z, and every other good of 𝒩 is the base of a frozen agent of P outside the path, which keeps it. The new
bases A, B′_h lie in G, which misses 𝒩 (J by (V1); B_z and B_h are free bases, which miss 𝒩 by (V2) or because a free
one-good base is unneeded) and misses the unchanged bases. Every base lies in its owner's relevant set (an agent values
a good it needs) and has at most two goods. So P′ ∈ 𝒫 by (V), |F(P′)| = |NA(P′)| ≤ |𝒩| = f, hence NA(P′) = 𝒩 by
minimality, and F(P′) is the set of agents whose base is one good of 𝒩: (F ∖ {x}) ∪ {z}, since A and B′_h miss 𝒩.
J(P′) is the bookkeeping of the move, and ω(P′) = |F(P′)| − (2n − m) = ω. The (T3⁺) conditions: NA is kept; x is the
only agent going frozen → free and z the only one going free → frozen, and z needs its new good in P; the a_i are frozen
in both; the helper gives up a good; the bases of {a_1, …, a_k, z} in P′ are {g_{a_0}}, …, {g_{a_k}}, which are the bases
of {x, a_1, …, a_k} in P. ∎

**Lemma 8⁺ (the unfrozen agent as owner after a chain swap).** In the setting of Lemma 6⁺, x is free in P′; its
bundles are the Z with A ⊆ Z ⊆ G ∖ B′_h; such a Z is safe in P′ iff it threatens no agent outside
{x, z, h, a_1, …, a_k} holding its base, nor z holding {g_{a_k}}, nor a_i holding {g_{a_{i−1}}}, nor h holding B′_h; and

  u′_x(Z) = #{q ∈ 𝒩 : q ∉ N_x(Z) ∪ 𝒩′},   𝒩′ := N_z({g_{a_k}}) ∪ ⋃_{i=1}^{k} N_{a_i}({g_{a_{i−1}}}) ∪ N_h(B′_h) ∪ 𝒩_{−{x,z,h,a_1,…,a_k}}.

So Val_{P′}(x) = max (|Z| + u′_x(Z)) over these Z is computed from P's data, and def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |Z| − u′_x(Z) for
each safe Z. By Fact 1, a set that threatens none of z, a_1, …, a_k holding their bases in P threatens none of them in P′.

*Proof.* W′_x = A ∪ J(P′) = G ∖ B′_h. Safety concerns the agents other than x with their P′ bases. 𝒩′ is the union of
the P′ needs of the agents other than x (the moved ones as listed, the others unchanged), and Fact 0 in P′ (𝒩 is the
needed set of P′, and x is free there) gives u′_x. Lemma H1 in P′. The last sentence: z and the a_i move to bases they
value more (they need them). ∎

### 3.2 The structural repairs along need paths

**Lemma 9⁺ (the owner at the free end).** Let o be free, o = a_{k+1}, a_k, …, a_1, a_0 = x a need path (so o needs
g_{a_k}), X a bundle of o in P and Z ⊆ W_o with
- (i) Z threatens x holding {g_x};
- (ii) Z threatens no agent outside {o, x} holding its base;
- (iii) θ_o(Z) ≤ v_o(g_{a_k}).

Then Z ∩ R_x contains a set A admissible for x, and for each such A the chain swap along the path (no helper, x takes
A) gives

  def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |Z| − (u_o(X) − e) − ι,

where e is the number of goods counted in u_o(X) that lie in N_o({g_{a_k}}), and ι = 1 if g_{a_k} ∈ N_o(X) and no agent
other than o needs g_{a_k}, ι = 0 otherwise. If v_o(X) < v_o(g_{a_k}), then e = 0, and ι = 1 iff nobody but o needs
g_{a_k}. For k = 0 this is `k4/dl13.md` Lemma 9 (written there with agents instead of goods).

*Proof.* *A exists:* the argument of `k4/dl13.md` Lemma 9, which uses only (i) and Z ⊆ W_o (so Z misses 𝒩): by (i) some
h ∈ Z has v_x(Z ∖ h) > v_x(g_x); every good of R_x ∖ 𝒩 is worth less than g_x to x (N_x({g_x}) ⊆ 𝒩); so
Q := (Z ∖ h) ∩ R_x, or its two best goods if |Q| = 3, is admissible. *The bound:* Lemma 6⁺ with z = o and no helper
(G = W_o) makes P′ min-frozen; Z is a bundle of x in P′ (A ⊆ Z ⊆ W_o). It is safe in P′ (Lemma 8⁺): the unmoved agents
by (ii); o holding {g_{a_k}} by (iii); each a_i by (ii) and Fact 1. In Lemma 8⁺'s count, v_x(Z) ≥ θ_x(Z) > v_x(g_x) by
(i), so N_x(Z) ⊆ N_x({g_x}) ⊆ 𝒩_{−o} (M2; x ≠ o); N_{a_i}({g_{a_{i−1}}}) ⊆ N_{a_i} ⊆ 𝒩_{−o}; and
𝒩_{−{x,o,a_1,…,a_k}} ⊆ 𝒩_{−o}. So N_x(Z) ∪ 𝒩′ ⊆ 𝒩_{−o} ∪ N_o({g_{a_k}}). A good counted in u_o(X) misses 𝒩_{−o}
(Fact 0), so it is counted in u′_x(Z) unless it lies in N_o({g_{a_k}}): u′_x(Z) ≥ u_o(X) − e. If ι = 1, g_{a_k} is not
counted in u_o(X) (it lies in N_o(X)) but is counted in u′_x(Z): it misses 𝒩_{−o} (only o needs it) and
N_o({g_{a_k}}) (o's own base). So u′_x(Z) ≥ u_o(X) − e + ι, and Lemma 8⁺ gives the bound. If v_o(X) < v_o(g_{a_k}),
then N_o({g_{a_k}}) ⊆ N_o(X) (M2), which counted goods miss, so e = 0; and g_{a_k} ∈ R_o ∖ X (X misses 𝒩) is worth
more than X, so g_{a_k} ∈ N_o(X). ∎

The corollaries below are stated for any free agent o and any bundle X of o that is safe in P, with the absolute bound
on def(P′) in terms of val_o(X) := |X| + u_o(X); for a best owner o and an optimal X, val_o(X) = V and the bound reads
def(P′) ≤ def(P) − (gain). The absolute form is what DL on the key graph needs (see *Key form* after Corollary 8.2⁺).

**Corollary 9.1⁺ (the S1 repair through a need path).** Let o be free, X a safe bundle of o, c ∈ J ∖ X,
Y := X ∪ {c}, and suppose Y threatens exactly one agent other than o, a frozen x (the *single frozen blocker*), and o is
the free end of a need path o, a_k, …, a_1, x with θ_o(Y) ≤ v_o(g_{a_k}) (*θ-ok along the path*). Then the chain swap
(o takes g_{a_k}, the a_i pass the frozen goods along, x takes any admissible A ⊆ Y, which exists) gives
def(P′) ≤ ω + 1 − val_o(X) − ι, with ι = 1 iff nobody but o needs g_{a_k}; for a best owner o and an optimal X,
def(P′) ≤ def(P) − 1 − ι. For k = 0 this is `k4/dl13.md` Corollary 9.1.

*Proof.* Lemma 9⁺ with Z = Y: (i), (ii) are the single block, (iii) the hypothesis. θ_o(Y) ≥ v_o(Y ∖ c) = v_o(X), so
v_o(X) ≤ v_o(g_{a_k}), and the inequality is strict in a strict profile (g_{a_k} ∉ X; v_o(X) = 0 if X misses R_o). So
e = 0, and def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |X| − 1 − u_o(X) − ι = ω + 1 − val_o(X) − ι; with def(P) = ω + 2 − V (Lemma H1) this is
def(P) − 1 − ι when val_o(X) = V. ∎

`k4/dl13.md` Lemma 10 (the θ-dichotomy) is stated for any good g that o needs, so it applies with g := g_{a_k}: when
θ-ok fails along a path, either at most two goods of o in Y are worth more than g_{a_k} together (and then another agent
needs g_{a_k}), or o is big-top on g_{a_k} with its three lower goods in Y. Different need paths from o to x end in
different goods g_{a_k}, and θ-ok may hold along one and fail along another.

**Lemma 11⁺ (an owner that does not move).** In the setting of Lemma 6⁺, let o ∉ {x, z, h, a_1, …, a_k} be free, X a
bundle of o in P with X ∩ (A ∪ B′_h) = ∅, and Y ⊇ X a bundle of o in P′ that is safe in P′. Let e* be the number of
goods counted in u_o(X) that lie in N_x(A) ∪ N_h(B′_h), and κ the number of goods of 𝒩 not counted in u_o(X) that miss
N_o(Y) ∪ 𝒩′_{−o}, where 𝒩′_{−o} := N_x(A) ∪ N_z({g_{a_k}}) ∪ ⋃_i N_{a_i}({g_{a_{i−1}}}) ∪ N_h(B′_h) ∪
𝒩_{−{o,x,z,h,a_1,…,a_k}}. Then def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |Y| − (u_o(X) − e*) − κ. For k = 0 it contains `k4/dl13.md`
Lemma 11 (there κ ≤ 1).

*Proof.* o keeps its base and is free in P′ (F(P′) = (F ∖ {x}) ∪ {z} and o ∉ F ∪ {z}); J ∖ (A ∪ B′_h) ⊆ J(P′), so X
and Y are bundles of o in P′. By Fact 0 in P′, u′_o(Y) = #{q ∈ 𝒩 : q ∉ N_o(Y) ∪ 𝒩′_{−o}}. Let q be counted in u_o(X)
and not lie in N_x(A) ∪ N_h(B′_h). Then q ∉ N_o(Y) ⊆ N_o(X) (M1); q ∉ N_z({g_{a_k}}) ∪ ⋃_i N_{a_i}({g_{a_{i−1}}}), a set
inside 𝒩_{−o} (the inclusions in the proof of Lemma 6⁺); and q ∉ 𝒩_{−{o,x,z,h,a_1,…,a_k}} ⊆ 𝒩_{−o}. So q is counted
in u′_o(Y), and so are the κ further goods: u′_o(Y) ≥ u_o(X) − e* + κ. Lemma H1 in P′. ∎

**Corollary 11.1⁺ (the blocker swap through a need path).** Let o be free, X a safe bundle of o, c ∈ J ∖ X,
Y := X ∪ {c}, and suppose Y threatens exactly one agent other than o, a free z, which is the free end of a need path
z, a_k, …, a_1, x with θ_z(Y) ≤ v_z(g_{a_k}). Let A ⊆ (J ∪ B_z) ∖ Y be admissible for x with θ_x(Y) ≤ v_x(A), and assume
no good counted in u_o(X) lies in N_x(A) (automatic if v_x(A) ≥ v_x(g_x)). Then the chain swap (no helper, x takes A)
gives def(P′) ≤ ω + 1 − val_o(X), i.e. def(P′) ≤ def(P) − 1 for a best owner and an optimal X. For k = 0 this is
`k4/dl13.md` Corollary 11.1.

*Proof.* Lemma 11⁺ with no helper and Y: Y ∖ B_o ⊆ J ∖ A ⊆ J(P′), so Y is a bundle of o in P′. Y is safe in P′: the
unmoved agents other than o are not threatened by Y in P (z is the only blocker); z holding {g_{a_k}} and x holding A by
hypothesis; each a_i by Fact 1, since Y did not threaten it in P. e* = 0 by hypothesis (if v_x(A) ≥ v_x(g_x), then
N_x(A) ⊆ N_x({g_x}) ⊆ 𝒩_{−o}, which counted goods miss). So def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |X| − 1 − u_o(X). ∎

**Corollary 11.2⁺ (the κ swap: an owner that keeps its bundle).** Let o be free, X a safe bundle of o, and x a frozen
agent whose good g = g_x is needed in P by exactly one agent a_1 other than o, with g ∉ N_o(X) (o does not need g given

pair with v_x(A) > v_x(g). Then the chain swap (no helper, x takes A) gives def(P′) ≤ ω + 1 − val_o(X), i.e.
def(P′) ≤ def(P) − 1 for a best owner and an optimal X.

*Proof.* A is admissible: N_x(A) ⊆ N_x({g}) ⊆ 𝒩 (M2). Lemma 6⁺ applies. X is a bundle of o in P′ (X ∖ B_o ⊆ J ∖ A ⊆
J(P′)), and it is safe in P′: X is safe in P, so it threatens no unmoved agent, and z and the a_i only upgrade (Fact 1);
nor x holding A, since θ_x(X) ≤ v_x(g) < v_x(A) (X does not threaten x holding g in P). Lemma 11⁺ with Y = X:
e* = 0, since N_x(A) ⊆ N_x({g}) ⊆ 𝒩_{−o}, which counted goods miss.
κ ≥ 1: g is not counted in u_o(X) (a_1 ≠ o needs it), but it is counted in u′_o(X): g ∉ N_o(X) by hypothesis;
g ∉ N_x(A) (v_x(A) > v_x(g)); a_1 holds g in P′ (z does if k = 0) and nobody needs its own base; z (if k ≥ 1) and a_i

miss g. So def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |X| − u_o(X) − 1. ∎

At k = 0 this is `k4/dl13.md` Lemma 11 with no helper, Y = X and κ = 1. It is a repair at part of the θ-b case of

**Corollary 8.2⁺ (the Lemma 7 swap through a need path).** Let x be frozen and big-top on its top g_x (four goods,
v(g_x) > v(b_x) + v(c_x)), L_x = R_x ∖ {g_x}, and let a_1 be the only agent that needs g_x (if a_1 is free, the path is
a_1, x with k = 0). Let z, a_k, …, a_1, x be a need path, h a free agent other than z or none, with L_x ⊆ G, A ⊆ L_x
admissible (e.g. {b_x, c_x}), and B′_h ⊆ G ∖ L_x admissible with g_x ∉ N_h(B′_h). If some Z ⊇ L_x is a bundle of x in P′
that is safe in P′, then def(P′) ≤ ω + 1 − |Z|; in particular def(P′) < def(P) as soon as |Z| + 1 > V. The move is a
(T3⁺) move (Lemma 6⁺) when there is no helper or the helper gives up a good of B_h, which is automatic when B_h meets
L_x; otherwise the bound holds but the move is a role swap with a growing helper, not a (T3⁺) move (1,015 of the moves
the PR #82 review found are of that kind; `k4/f2_lemmas.py`'s C3⁺ test admits only helpers that give up a good). For
k = 0 this is `k4/dl13.md` Corollary 8.2.

*Proof.* {b_x, c_x} is admissible: the goods of R_x worth more than b_x + c_x are g_x only (big-top; d_x < c_x), and
g_x ∈ 𝒩; L_x ⊆ G misses 𝒩. v_x(Z) ≥ v_x(L_x) > v_x(g_x) (balance), so g_x ∉ N_x(Z). And g_x ∉ 𝒩′ of Lemma 8⁺: in P only
a_1 needs g_x, so no unmoved agent does; for k ≥ 1 neither does z in P′ (N_z({g_{a_k}}) ⊆ N_z(B_z) ∌ g_x) nor a_i with
i ≥ 2 (N_{a_i}({g_{a_{i−1}}}) ⊆ N_{a_i} ∌ g_x); a_1 holds g_x in P′ (z does if k = 0), and nobody needs its own base;
g_x ∉ N_h(B′_h) by hypothesis. So g_x is counted in u′_x(Z), and Lemma 8⁺ gives def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |Z| − 1. With
def(P) = ω + 2 − V, def(P′) < def(P) when |Z| + 1 > V. ∎

**Key form.** Every bound above is absolute: def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − (a value computed at P). For a key κ (needed set, frozen
agents and their goods) write def*(κ) for its least deficit and V*(κ) := ω + 2 − def*(κ), the largest Val* over the
min-frozen states of κ (ω is the same for all of them). (T1) and (T2) moves stay inside κ, so the corollaries may be
applied at *any* state P₁ of κ: if their configuration holds at P₁ with val_o(X) + ι ≥ V*(κ) (Corollary 9.1⁺; ι := 0
for 11.1⁺ and 11.2⁺), or with |Z| + 1 > V*(κ) (Corollary 8.2⁺), then the move from P₁ reaches a state P′ with
def(P′) < def*(κ), so κ has a (T3ᵖ)-edge (a (T3) edge if k = 0) to a key whose least deficit is smaller. At a state that
attains def*(κ), with o a best owner and X optimal, val_o(X) = V*(κ) and the condition holds. This is the form that
`EFX.C4min.dlKeyAt_of_keyMin_key` (formal/k4-key-frame, PR #79) takes: at a key-minimal P, some state P₁ of the same key
has a move to a state whose key has a smaller def*.

### 3.3 A (T4) move followed by a (T3⁺) move is a (T3⁺) move

**Lemma CL (closure).** Let P → P* be a (T4) move (the changed agents are frozen in P and in P*, they permute their
one-good bases, and NA(P*) = NA(P)) and P* → P′ a (T3⁺) move. Then P → P′ is a (T3⁺) move (with W = ∅ it may be a
(T3) move).

*Proof.* Let 𝒩 = NA(P) = NA(P*) = NA(P′). A (T4) move changes only frozen agents and keeps every agent's frozen
status, so P and P* have the same frozen set F, the same free agents with the same bases (hence the same needs), and the
same junk. Let x, z, h be the agents of the (T3⁺) move P* → P′ (h possibly absent); F(P′) = (F ∖ {x}) ∪ {z}, since
every agent outside x and z keeps its frozen status in a (T3⁺) move (the changed agents frozen in both stay frozen, the
helper stays free, and an unchanged agent keeps its base and NA is kept). In P → P′: x is frozen in P and free in P′, so
its base changed; z is free in P and frozen in P′, so its base changed, and z needs its new good in P, because z has
the same base and needs in P as in P*; every other changed agent frozen in P is frozen in P′ (it lies in F ∖ {x}); a
changed agent free in both is free in P* with the same base, so it changed in P* → P′ and is h, which gives up a good
of B_h(P*) = B_h(P). The bases: in P the agents of F hold the goods of 𝒩 one each, and in P′ the agents of
(F ∖ {x}) ∪ {z} do; the agents of F ∖ {x} whose base did not change hold the same goods in both; so the changed agents
frozen in both, W, together with x hold in P exactly the goods that W together with z holds in P′. ∎

**Proposition N (reduction to T4-optimal states, up to a (T4) key edge).** Let P be min-frozen with def(P) > 0 such
that no (T4) move lowers the deficit. Then P = P* or P → P* is a (T4) move, for a T4-optimal min-frozen P* with
def(P*) = def(P) and the same free agents, free bases, junk and needed set; every (T3⁺) move P* → P′ with
def(P′) < def(P*) gives a (T3⁺) move P → P′ with def(P′) < def(P); and exactly one of the following holds:
- (i) def*(κ(P*)) < def(P). If P is at the T3 stage (def(P) = def*(κ(P))), then κ(P) → κ(P*) is a (T4) edge of the key
  graph to a key with smaller def*.
- (ii) def*(κ(P*)) = def(P*), and P* is at the T3 stage. Every improving (T3⁺) move from P* gives one from P.

So for DL on the key graph with (T3⁺) ∪ (T4) edges, the T3 stage needs to be shown only at T4-optimal states, where
every frozen agent has a need path from a free agent (Lemma NP). For single-step DL_{R_C} this reduction holds only in
case (ii): in case (i) P* is not key-minimal, a (T1) or (T2) move lowers def(P*), and the composite with P → P* is not
one R_C move. Case (i) occurs: 19 of the 273 non-T4-optimal T3-stage states of the data (`results/k4_f2/propn.log`;
smallest n = 4, m = 8, sets [[0,2,6,7],[1,5,6,7],[2,3,4,5],[3,4,6,7]], values [[3,2,4,8],[1,4,8,6],[1,4,8,6],
[4,5,8,6]], P = ({6},{1,5},{2,4},{7}) with def 1, P* = ({7},{1,5},{2,4},{6}) T4-optimal with def 1 and
def*(κ(P*)) = 0).

*Proof.* `k4/dl13.md` Corollary 12.1 (K4.DL13.ROT, PROVED) reaches a T4-optimal P* from P by
finitely many frozen rotations, def(P*) ≤ def(P), each rotation a Pareto reassignment (`k4/dl13.md` Lemma 12: same
needed set, frozen set and junk, free agents untouched). Their composite is a permutation of the frozen goods among the
frozen agents in which every agent ends with a good it values at least as much as its own; if it is not the identity,
it is a (T4) move (every changed agent is frozen in P and in P*, NA kept). No (T4) move lowers def(P), so
def(P*) = def(P). Lemma CL lifts the (T3⁺) moves. For the dichotomy: def*(κ(P*)) ≤ def(P*) = def(P). If it is smaller,
(i) holds, and def(P) = def*(κ(P)) at the T3 stage. Otherwise P* is key-minimal with def(P*) > 0; a (T4) move from P*
to some P″ with def(P″) < def(P*) would compose with P → P* to a permutation of the frozen goods among the frozen agents
with NA kept, a (T4) move from P (or P″ = P, impossible as def(P″) < def(P)) lowering def(P); so no (T4) move lowers
def(P*), and P* is at the T3 stage. ∎

### 3.5 Why chains suffice: the reduction to the f = 1 lemmas

In each of `k4/dl13.md`'s structural repairs (Corollaries 9.1, 11.1, 8.2) the hypothesis "a free agent z needs the
frozen good g_x" serves twice: it makes the swap valid (Lemma 6) and it protects z holding g_x. Along a need path every
intermediary takes a good it needs: the needed set stays (Lemma 6⁺), the intermediary's needs shrink and every set that
did not threaten it still does not (Fact 1), so it costs nothing in the safety of the new owner's bundle, and its new
needs lie inside 𝒩_{−o}, so it costs nothing in the u-terms. Hence each repair holds with "z needs g_x" replaced by "z is
the free end of a need path to x" (Corollaries 9.1⁺, 11.1⁺, 11.2⁺, 8.2⁺). By Lemma NP such a path exists for every
frozen agent of a T4-optimal state, to which Proposition N reduces the T3 stage for DL on the key graph (up to a (T4) key
edge). For a frozen blocker whose needers are all frozen (the case named in `k4/dl13.md` §6 item 4) what is proved is
conditional: *if the owner o itself is the free end of a need path to the blocker and θ-ok holds along that path,*
Corollary 9.1⁺ repairs it by a (T3⁺) move (not a (T3) move). Lemma NP gives only *some* free end, not necessarily the
owner. That the owner can be taken to be a free end is a statement about the data only (§1, §7), and even at the
case-B states with such an owner, 109 states (19 of main, 55 of n5, 35 of rc) have no instance of Corollary 9.1⁺ and are
certified otherwise (§4). This is what the n = 5 failures of DL_RT4 need (§2).

At f ≥ 2 the other frozen agents enter the bounds only through goods counted in u_o(X) that the move makes needed again:
e (goods in N_o({g_{a_k}}), Lemma 9⁺) and e* (goods in N_x(A) ∪ N_h(B′_h), Lemma 11⁺). Both vanish under the hypotheses
of the corollaries (e because v_o(X) < v_o(g_{a_k}); e* because v_x(A) ≥ v_x(g_x), or by hypothesis), and the goods
the chain passes along can only add (ι, κ). At k = 0, Corollary 11.2⁺ is `k4/dl13.md` Lemma 11 with Y = X and κ = 1, a
repair at part of the θ-b case of `k4/dl13.md` §6 item 2 (see after Corollary 11.2⁺; PR #85 treats θ-b).

## 5. From Theorem Z′'s state: Lemmas C⁺ and C′⁺ (f ≥ 2)

PR #80 (`k4/sx.md` §6, proof/k4-sx) starts from a maximum Q of (r′, Λ′) at a key κ = (𝒩, φ) with def*(κ) > 0 and
proves Lemma F⁺ (the threat forest; free-valid owners exist), Lemma A⁺ (the owner swap along a need chain) and Lemma B⁺
(the path move along a need chain), cited here as k4/sx.md, PR #80. A⁺ or B⁺ applies at some maximum of 193 of the 289
keys with def* > 0 of its three f ≥ 2 inputs; this section takes the other 96 keys (49 of the n = 4 catalogues and
hunts, 40 of the T1-stuck profiles, 7 of compute/k4-rt4-n5c) and their 174 maxima where neither applies. The
coordinator's referee of PR #80 classified the repairs from P_Q there into two shapes, a C shape (another leaf owns a
full bundle of ω + 2 goods) and an ι shape (x or another leaf owns ω + 1 goods and a newly counted good pays for the
dropped one). Lemmas C⁺ and C′⁺ below give written proofs of both shapes at any f, in the language of §3.

**Notation.** Q is a configuration at κ: free agents y hold disjoint pairs Q_y ⊆ M ∖ 𝒩 with admissible part
H_y = Q_y ∩ U_y (U_y = R_y ∖ 𝒩), the pool L has ω goods, X_y = Q_y ∪ L. P_Q is its state: frozen w on {φ(w)}, free y on
H_y. P_Q is a min-frozen state of κ (the proof of `k4/c4min.md` Lemma 1(a) with C = ∅, K4.C4MIN.CFG; directly:
NA(P_Q) ⊆ 𝒩, because an admissible H_y leaves no good of U_y worth more than itself and each frozen w on φ(w) needs
only goods of 𝒩, as it does in a min-frozen state of key κ; every good of 𝒩 is the one-good base of an agent and the
free bases miss 𝒩, so P_Q ∈ 𝒫 by (V); and |F(P_Q)| ≤ |𝒩| = f forces NA(P_Q) = 𝒩 by minimality, as in the proof of
Lemma 6⁺. `k4/f2_cc.py` looks every P_Q up among the min-frozen states.) Its junk is J = L ∪ ⋃_y (Q_y ∖ H_y), so
each X_y is a bundle of y in P_Q, with
|X_y| = ω + 2. The lemmas hold at any min-frozen P (any f); at P = P_Q they give def ≤ 0, i.e. an edge of the key graph
to a key with def* ≤ 0 (Lemma 0 of k4/sx.md, PR #80, is the same count).

**Fact 2 (θ is monotone in the valued part).** If Y ∩ R_w ⊆ Z ∩ R_w and |Y| ≤ |Z|, then θ_w(Y) ≤ θ_w(Z).

*Proof.* If Y ⊄ R_w, then θ_w(Y) = v_w(Y ∩ R_w) (drop a good w does not value), and |Y ∩ R_w| < |Z|, so some h ∈ Z lies
outside Y ∩ R_w; then Y ∩ R_w ⊆ Z ∖ h and θ_w(Y) ≤ v_w(Z ∖ h) ≤ θ_w(Z). If Y ⊆ R_w, then Y ⊆ Z; for Y = Z there is
nothing to show, and otherwise some h ∈ Z ∖ Y gives θ_w(Y) ≤ v_w(Y) ≤ v_w(Z ∖ h) ≤ θ_w(Z). ∎

**Fact 3 (a good that counts after a chain swap).** In the setting of Lemma 6⁺ without a helper (path
z, a_k, …, a_1, x, x taking A), let o be x or a free agent of P off the path, and Y a bundle of o in P′. A good q ∈ 𝒩 is
counted in u′_o(Y) as soon as
- (1) q ∉ N_o(Y) (v_o(Y) > v_o(q), or q ∉ R_o);
- (2) every agent that needs q in P is o or the agent that holds q in P′ (no *third needer*);
- (3) o = x, or q ∉ N_x(A).

*Proof.* By Fact 0 in P′ (𝒩 is its needed set, o is free there), q is counted iff q ∉ N_o(Y) and no agent i ≠ o needs
q in P′. The holder of q does not need its own base. An unmoved agent keeps its needs, which miss q by (2). z and the
a_i take goods they need, so their needs only shrink (Fact 1), and by (2) they did not need q in P unless they hold it
in P′. If x ≠ o, x does not need q in P′ by (3). ∎

**Lemma C⁺ (another free agent owns a full bundle; k4/sx.md Lemma C, PR #80, at any f).** Let P be min-frozen, τ the
free end of a need path τ, a_k, …, a_1, x (k ≥ 0), X_τ and X_o bundles in P of τ and of a free agent o off the path,
a pair P_x ⊆ X_τ (|P_x| = 2) with A := P_x ∩ R_x admissible for x, and Y := (X_o ∪ X_τ) ∖ P_x. Assume
- (a) θ_x(Y) ≤ v_x(A);
- (b) θ_τ(Y) ≤ v_τ(φ(a_k)) (φ(a_k) := the good of a_k; φ(x) for k = 0);
- (c) Y threatens no agent outside {o, τ, x} holding its base in P.

Then the chain swap P′ (τ takes φ(a_k), a_i takes φ(a_{i−1}), x takes A) satisfies def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |Y|; P → P′ is a
(T3) move without helper for k = 0, and a (T3⁺) path swap with W = {a_1, …, a_k} otherwise. At P = P_Q with
X_y = Q_y ∪ L, Y = Q_o ∪ (X_τ ∖ P_x) has ω + 2 goods and def(P′) ≤ 0. Sufficient conditions: (a) holds if P_x is robust
for x (v_x(P_x) ≥ v_x(U_x ∖ P_x)); (c) holds under **(H*)**: X_o threatens nobody outside {o, x}, no agent outside
{x, o, τ} values a good of X_τ ∖ (X_o ∪ P_x), and |Y| ≤ |X_o| (at P_Q: |Y| = |X_o|).

*Proof.* A ⊆ X_τ ⊆ J ∪ B_τ, so Lemma 6⁺ (no helper) makes P′ min-frozen with needed set 𝒩, frozen set
(F ∖ {x}) ∪ {τ} and junk (J ∪ B_τ) ∖ A, and gives the move. Y is a bundle of o in P′: B_o ⊆ X_o misses P_x (P_x lies in
B_τ ∪ J), and Y ∖ B_o ⊆ (J ∪ B_τ) ∖ P_x ⊆ (J ∪ B_τ) ∖ A. Y is safe in P′: x holding A by (a); τ holding φ(a_k) by (b);
a_i holding φ(a_{i−1}) by (c) and Fact 1; every other agent keeps its base, (c). Lemma H1 (with u′ ≥ 0) gives the bound.
At P_Q, Q_o ∩ X_τ = ∅ and |X_τ ∖ P_x| = ω. *(a) from robustness:* Y misses 𝒩, so Y ∩ R_x ⊆ U_x ∖ P_x and
θ_x(Y) ≤ v_x(U_x ∖ P_x) ≤ v_x(P_x) = v_x(A). *(c) from (H*):* for w ∉ {o, τ, x}, Y ∖ X_o ⊆ X_τ ∖ (X_o ∪ P_x), so
Y ∩ R_w ⊆ X_o ∩ R_w, and Fact 2 gives θ_w(Y) ≤ θ_w(X_o) ≤ v_w(B_w). ∎

At f = 1 this is Lemma C of k4/sx.md, PR #80, whose θ-b(τ₁) hypothesis is one way to get (b) and whose (H) is (c). At
f ≥ 2, (c) also concerns the frozen agents other than x: a leaf whose bundle threatens a second frozen agent cannot be
the owner o under (H*). On the data the owner is always a leaf of Lemma F⁺, but (H*) fails at more than half of the
C⁺ maxima, where (c) holds for another reason; so (c) is the hypothesis to keep.

**Lemma C′⁺ (an owner of ω + 1 goods paid by a newly counted good; k4/sx.md Lemma C′, PR #80, and the ι of Lemma 9⁺,
at any f).** Let P, τ, the need path, A ⊆ (J ∪ B_τ) ∩ R_x admissible and P′ be as in Lemma C⁺. Let o be x or a free
agent of P off the path, and Y a bundle of o in P′ (A ⊆ Y ⊆ J ∪ B_τ if o = x; B_o ⊆ Y ⊆ B_o ∪ ((J ∪ B_τ) ∖ A)
otherwise) such that
- (a) o = x or θ_x(Y) ≤ v_x(A); (b) θ_τ(Y) ≤ v_τ(φ(a_k)); (c) Y threatens no agent outside {o, τ, x} holding its base
  in P;
- some q ∈ 𝒩 satisfies (1)–(3) of Fact 3.

Then def(P′) ≤ ω + 1 − |Y|; at |Y| = ω + 1, def(P′) ≤ 0.

*Proof.* Lemma 6⁺ as in Lemma C⁺; Y is safe in P′ by (a)–(c) and Fact 1; Fact 3 gives u′_o(Y) ≥ 1; Lemma H1. ∎

Four kinds of q occur, all at k = 0 except where noted:
- **q = φ(x), o = x** (Lemma 9⁺'s ι, with (i) weakened to v_x(Y) > v_x(φ(x)), which is (1)): τ is the only agent
  that needs φ(x). The coordinator's example: n = 4, m = 8, sets [[0,2,4,6],[0,2,5,6],[1,3,4,7],[1,3,5,7]], every
  agent values its goods 2, 3, 8, 4; κ: agents 0 and 1 frozen on 4 and 5 (def* = 1); at the maximum
  Q = {2: {0,7}, 3: {1,3}}, L = {2,6}, the only leaf (agent 2) threatens both frozen agents, so A⁺ fails. τ = 2
  takes 4, x = 0 takes A ⊆ {0,2,6} and owns Y = {0,2,6}: θ₁(Y) = 7 < 8, v₀(Y) = 9 > 8, and only agent 2 needed good 4, so q = 4 counts.
- **q = φ(x), o another free agent** (Lemma C′ of k4/sx.md, PR #80): o and τ are the only needers of φ(x) (at f = 1
  these are its two terminals τ₂, τ₁), v_o(Y) > v_o(φ(x)), and v_x(A) > v_x(φ(x)).
- **q = φ(w) of a frozen w off the move, o a free agent** (new at f ≥ 2): o is the only agent that needs φ(w) in P,

  k = 4 core with f ≥ 2 and ω ≥ 1, every key κ with def*(κ) > 0 has a maximum Q of (r′, Λ′) at which Lemma A⁺ (any
