## 1. Setting and data

Notation of `k4/dl2.md` §4. A strict profile of a connected k = 4 core whose fewest frozen agents is f ≥ 1, with
ω ≥ 1; P ∈ 𝒫 min-frozen with needed set 𝒩 = NA(P), frozen agents F, junk J; W_o = B_o ∪ J; θ_x(Z) =
max_{h ∈ Z} v_x(Z ∖ h); Z *threatens* x holding B if θ_x(Z) > v_x(B); a bundle of a free o is a Z with
B_o ⊆ Z ⊆ W_o; u_o(Z) counts the frozen x ≠ o whose good misses N_o(Z) ∪ 𝒩₋ₒ; Val_P(o) = max(|Z| + u_o(Z)) over the
safe bundles; V := Val*(P) = max over the free o. **Lemma H1**: def(P) = ω + 2 − V. So def(P) > 0 iff V ≤ ω + 1.
𝒩_{−S} := ⋃_{i ∉ S} N_i. A *needer* of g ∈ 𝒩 is an agent with g ∈ N_i. For a best owner o, an optimal X and
c ∈ J ∖ X, the *blockers* of c are the agents w ≠ o that X ∪ {c} threatens (holding B_w). A set B ⊆ R_i of at most
two goods with N_i(B) ⊆ 𝒩 is *admissible for* i (a base i may take without enlarging the needed set; Lemma 1(c)).

**Remark (free agents exist).** At f ≥ 1, f ≤ n − 1: serial dictatorship (agents in any order take their favourite
remaining relevant good, or nothing) is in 𝒫, since an agent needs only goods taken before its turn, which are
one-good bases; and the last agent's good is needed by nobody. So a min-frozen P has a free agent, whose base is a
safe bundle, and def(P) is finite.

**Definition.** P (min-frozen, def(P) > 0) is *T1-stuck* if no (T1) move (a free y re-bases to some B′ ⊆ B_y ∪ J
admissible for y, B′ ≠ B_y; Lemma 1(c)) gives def(P′) < def(P).

## 2. The deficit lemmas

### 2.1 Role swaps (T3)

Throughout, P is min-frozen with ω ≥ 1; x ∈ F with B_x = {g}; z a free agent with g ∈ N_z; H = ∅ or H = {h} with h
free, h ≠ z; G := J ∪ B_z ∪ B_h; A ⊆ G admissible for x and B′_h ⊆ G admissible for h (§1), disjoint. If H = ∅, then
B_h := ∅, B′_h := ∅, N_h(B′_h) := ∅, and every index set {x, z, h} (as in 𝒩_{−{x,z,h}}) reads {x, z}. P′ is the role
swap: z takes {g}, x takes A, h takes B′_h. By Lemma 6 (`k4/dl2.md`), P′ is min-frozen, NA(P′) = 𝒩 and
F(P′) = (F ∖ {x}) ∪ {z}; its junk is J′ = G ∖ (A ∪ B′_h), and ω(P′) = ω. A (T3) move is the case where the helper
gives up a good of B_h.

**Lemma 8 (the unfrozen agent as owner).** x is free in P′ and its bundles in P′ are the Z with
A ⊆ Z ⊆ G ∖ B′_h. Such a Z is safe in P′ iff it threatens no agent w ∉ {x, z, h} holding B_w, does not threaten z
holding {g}, and (if H ≠ ∅) does not threaten h holding B′_h. For it, u′_x(Z) = ū(Z) + ι(Z), where

  ū(Z) := #{w ∈ F ∖ {x} : B_w ∩ (N_x(Z) ∪ N_z({g}) ∪ N_h(B′_h) ∪ 𝒩_{−{x,z,h}}) = ∅},
  ι(Z) := 1 if v_x(Z) > v_x(g) and g ∉ N_h(B′_h) ∪ 𝒩_{−{x,z,h}}, and 0 otherwise.

Hence Val_{P′}(x) = max{|Z| + ū(Z) + ι(Z)} over those Z, and def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |Z| − ū(Z) − ι(Z) for each of them.

*Proof.* J′ = (J ∪ {g} ∪ B_z ∪ B_h) ∖ ({g} ∪ A ∪ B′_h) = G ∖ (A ∪ B′_h), so W′_x = A ∪ J′ = G ∖ B′_h. Safety in P′
concerns the agents other than x with their P′ bases: B_w for w ∉ {x, z, h}, {g} for z, B′_h for h. In P′,
𝒩′_{−x} = N_z({g}) ∪ N_h(B′_h) ∪ 𝒩_{−{x,z,h}} (the other agents keep their bases and needs). A frozen agent of P′
other than x is either some w ∈ F ∖ {x}, with B′_w = B_w (w ∉ {z, h}, which are free in P), counted in u′_x(Z) iff
B_w misses N_x(Z) ∪ 𝒩′_{−x}, i.e. iff it is counted in ū(Z); or z, with base {g}, counted iff g ∉ N_x(Z) ∪ 𝒩′_{−x}.
g ∉ N_z({g}). g ∈ R_x ∖ Z (g ∈ 𝒩, and G misses 𝒩: J by (V1), free bases by (V2) or because a free one-good base is
unneeded), so g ∉ N_x(Z) iff v_x(Z) ≥ v_x(g), i.e. v_x(Z) > v_x(g) by strictness. So z is counted iff ι(Z) = 1.
Lemma H1 in P′ (x is free there). ∎

`k4/dl13_lemmas.py` checks the equality Val_{P′}(x) = max(|Z| + ū(Z) + ι(Z)) against the exact owner table of P′ at
every swap allowed by Lemma 6 (every x, needer z, helper or none, A, B′_h): at T1-stuck states, 256,972 swaps (239,872
at the 13,971 records of §1's runs, `coverage.log`; 17,100 at the 7,586 of compute/k4-dl13's failing n = 4 profiles,
`coverage_n4_failures.log`); at every def > 0 state, T1-stuck or not, of the two samples of §4, 134,153 + 9,973 swaps
(`lemmas_gap_n3.log`, `lemmas_random.log`): no violation.

Lemma 7 of `k4/dl2.md` is the case "ι(Z) = 1" read in P′; the next corollary pulls it back to P.

**Corollary 8.2 (the Lemma 7 swap).** Let x be big-top with top g and lower goods L_x = R_x ∖ {g}, and let no agent
other than z need g (in P). Let L_x ⊆ G, A ⊆ L_x admissible for x (e.g. A = {b_x, c_x}), B′_h ⊆ G ∖ L_x admissible for h
with g ∉ N_h(B′_h) (automatic if g ∉ R_h). If some Z ⊇ L_x is a bundle of x in P′ that is safe in P′, then
def(P′) ≤ ω + 1 − |Z| − ū(Z); in particular def(P′) < def(P) as soon as |Z| + 1 > V.

*Proof.* v_x(Z) ≥ v_x(L_x) > v_x(g) (balance), and g ∉ 𝒩_{−{x,z,h}} (z is the only needer), so ι(Z) = 1 in
Lemma 8. {b_x, c_x} is admissible for x: its needs are the goods worth more than b + c, i.e. g only (big-top; d < b + c),
and g ∈ 𝒩; L_x ⊆ G misses 𝒩. ∎

**Lemma 9 (the owner swap from a needer).** Let o be free with g ∈ N_o (so z := o, H = ∅, G = W_o), X a bundle of o
in P, and Z ⊆ W_o such that
- (i) Z threatens x holding {g};
- (ii) Z threatens no agent w ∉ {o, x} holding B_w;
- (iii) θ_o(Z) ≤ v_o(g).

Then Z contains a set A admissible for x, and for each such A the swap (o takes {g}, x takes A) satisfies

  def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |Z| − (u_o(X) − e) − ι,

where e is the number of agents counted in u_o(X) that are x or whose good lies in N_o({g}), and ι = 1 if nobody
outside {o, x} needs g, 0 otherwise. e = 0 when v_o(X) < v_o(g); e ≤ 1 (only x) when g is o's top.

*Proof.* *A exists.* By (i) some h ∈ Z has v_x(Z ∖ h) > v_x(g); let Q = (Z ∖ h) ∩ R_x. Z ⊆ W_o misses 𝒩, and every
good of R_x ∖ 𝒩 is worth less than g to x (it is not g, and not in N_x({g}) ⊆ 𝒩). If |Q| ≤ 2, A := Q has
v_x(A) > v_x(g) > every good of R_x ∖ (𝒩 ∪ A), so N_x(A) ⊆ 𝒩. Otherwise |Q| = 3, R_x = Q ∪ {g}, and A := the two
best goods of Q has N_x(A) ⊆ {g} (the third good is worth less than each of them). *The bound.* Lemma 6 applies
(o needs g), and Lemma 8 with this Z: it is a bundle of x in P′ (A ⊆ Z ⊆ W_o = G), safe in P′ by (ii) and (iii).
By (i), v_x(Z) ≥ θ_x(Z) > v_x(g), so N_x(Z) ⊆ N_x({g}) ⊆ 𝒩_{−o} (M2) and ι(Z) = ι. Let w be counted in u_o(X),
w ≠ x, with B_w ∩ N_o({g}) = ∅. B_w misses 𝒩_{−o}, which contains N_x(Z) and 𝒩_{−{o,x}}, and misses N_o({g});
so w is counted in ū(Z) (H = ∅, z = o). Hence ū(Z) ≥ u_o(X) − e. *The special cases.* If v_o(X) < v_o(g): g ∈ R_o ∖ X
(g ∉ W_o), so g ∈ N_o(X) and x is not counted; and N_o({g}) ⊆ N_o(X) (M2), which no counted good meets. If g is o's
top, N_o({g}) = ∅. ∎

**Corollary 9.1 (the S1 repair).** Let o be a best owner with an optimal X, c ∈ J ∖ X, and suppose X ∪ {c} threatens
exactly one agent other than o, a frozen x whose good g o needs (the *S1 shape*), and θ_o(X ∪ {c}) ≤ v_o(g)
(*θ-ok*). Then the swap (o takes {g}, x takes any A ⊆ X ∪ {c} admissible for x, which exists) gives
def(P′) ≤ def(P) − 1 − ι.

*Proof.* Lemma 9 with Z = X ∪ {c}: (i), (ii) are the S1 shape, (iii) is θ-ok. θ_o(X ∪ {c}) ≥ v_o(X) (drop c), so
v_o(X) ≤ v_o(g), and the inequality is strict (strict profile; v_o(X) = 0 < v_o(g) if X misses R_o). So e = 0, and
def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |X| − 1 − u_o(X) − ι = def(P) − 1 − ι (Lemma H1, X optimal). ∎

**Corollary 9.2 (Lemma 9 at a best owner).** If o is a best owner that needs g, X optimal with e = 0, and Z ⊆ W_o
satisfies (i)–(iii) with |Z| + ι > |X|, the swap (o takes {g}, x takes an A ⊆ Z admissible for x) lowers the deficit. (Z need
not contain X: for a big-top o, dropping one lower good of o is what (iii) can require; see Lemma 10.)

*Proof.* Lemma 9 gives def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |Z| − u_o(X) − ι ≤ ω + 2 − |X| − 1 − u_o(X), and |X| + u_o(X) = V for an
optimal X of a best owner, so def(P′) ≤ def(P) − 1 by Lemma H1. ∎

**Lemma 10 (how the S1 repair fails: the θ-dichotomy).** Let o be free with g ∈ N_o, and Z ⊆ W_o with
θ_o(Z) > v_o(g). Then exactly one of:
- **(θ-a)** some set Q of at most two goods of Z ∩ R_o has v_o(Q) > v_o(g). Then Q is a re-base of o admissible for o
  inside W_o that does not need g, and **some agent other than o needs g**;
- **(θ-b)** |R_o| = 4, g is o's top, o is big-top (v_o(g) > v_o(b_o) + v_o(c_o)), and o's three lower goods lie in Z.

*Proof.* (θ-a): v_o(Q) > v_o(g) > v_o(B_o) (o needs g), so N_o(Q) ⊆ N_o(B_o) ⊆ 𝒩 (M2), Q ⊆ (B_o ∪ J) ∩ R_o and
Q ≠ B_o: Q is admissible, and g ∉ N_o(Q). By Lemma 1(c) the re-base gives a min-frozen P₁ with NA(P₁) = 𝒩 ∋ g; as
NA(P₁) = 𝒩_{−o} ∪ N_o(Q), g ∈ 𝒩_{−o}. Not (θ-a): θ_o(Z) = v_o(Z ∖ h) for some h, and Q′ := (Z ∖ h) ∩ R_o has
v_o(Q′) ≥ θ_o(Z) > v_o(g) but at least three goods; g ∉ Z and |R_o| ≤ 4, so R_o = Q′ ∪ {g}, and every pair of Q′ is
worth less than g: g is o's top and beats the best pair. ∎

So at an S1 shape the S1 repair fails only (θ-a) when o has a pair in X ∪ {c} worth more than g, and then g has a
second needer; or (θ-b) when o is a big-top agent on g with all its lower goods in X ∪ {c}. In (θ-b), Corollary 9.2
can drop a lower good of o from Z, at the price of one good, which a second junk good blocked by x alone, or ι = 1,
pays back.

**Lemma 11 (an owner that does not move).** Let o ∉ {x, z} ∪ H be free, X a bundle of o in P with
X ∩ (A ∪ B′_h) = ∅, and Y ⊇ X a bundle of o in P′ that is safe in P′. Let e* be the number of agents counted in
u_o(X) that lie in {x, z} ∪ H or whose good lies in N_x(A) ∪ N_z({g}) ∪ N_h(B′_h), and κ := 1 if
g ∉ N_o(Y) ∪ N_x(A) ∪ N_h(B′_h) ∪ 𝒩_{−{o,x,z,h}}, else 0. Then def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |Y| − (u_o(X) − e*) − κ.
Here x is never counted in u_o(X) (z ≠ o needs g), and agents with a good in N_z({g}) never are (N_z({g}) ⊆ N_z ⊆ 𝒩_{−o}).

*Proof.* Lemma 2* (`k4/dl2.md`, the move keeps the needed set) gives u′_o(Y) ≥ u_o(X) − e* from the frozen agents of
P outside the move. In addition z ∈ F(P′) ∖ F(P) has base {g} and is counted in u′_o(Y) iff
g ∉ N_o(Y) ∪ 𝒩′_{−o} = N_o(Y) ∪ N_x(A) ∪ N_z({g}) ∪ N_h(B′_h) ∪ 𝒩_{−{o,x,z,h}}, and g ∉ N_z({g}): that is κ. Lemma H1
in P′. ∎

κ = 1 is Lemma 7's mechanism at an owner that does not move: o's bundle now satisfies o, x's new base satisfies x,
and z is the only needer left.

**Corollary 11.1 (the blocker swap).** Let o be a best owner, X optimal, c ∈ J ∖ X, and suppose X ∪ {c} threatens
exactly one agent other than o, a free z that needs the good g of a frozen x, with θ_z(X ∪ {c}) ≤ v_z(g). Let
A ⊆ (J ∪ B_z) ∖ (X ∪ {c}) be admissible for x with θ_x(X ∪ {c}) ≤ v_x(A), and assume no agent counted in u_o(X)
has its good in N_x(A) (automatic if f = 1 or v_x(A) ≥ v_x(g)). Then the swap (z takes {g}, x takes A) gives
def(P′) ≤ def(P) − 1.

*Proof.* Lemma 11 with H = ∅ and Y = X ∪ {c}: c ∈ J ∖ A ⊆ J′; Y threatens nobody in P′ (the agents outside
{o, x, z} as in P; z holding g and x holding A by hypothesis); e* = 0 (x, z are not counted, N_z({g}) as noted, and the
hypothesis on N_x(A); at f = 1 nobody is counted, since x is not; if v_x(A) ≥ v_x(g), N_x(A) ⊆ N_x({g}) ⊆ 𝒩_{−o}).
So def(P′) ≤ ω + 2 − |X| − 1 − u_o(X) = def(P) − 1. ∎

### 2.2 Frozen rotations (T4): DL₁₃ fails, and the normalization that repairs it

**DL₁₃ is false at n = 4** (found by compute/k4-dl13 and merged in PR #74: `results/k4_dl13/n4_FAILURES.md`; three
implementations agree; K4.DL2.T13 REFUTED; since this instance's only improvements are (T4) moves, it refutes DL_T as
well, K4.DL2.T REFUTED). Its smallest instance: core (m = 6, idx 5) of `results/k4_certs_4_n4_1.json.gz`, agents 0: (0:2, 2:3,
4:4, 5:8), 1: (1:2, 3:4, 5:3), 2 and 3: (3:3, 4:4, 5:2); P = ({4}, {1}, {3}, {5}) has f = 3, 𝒩 = {3, 4, 5}, J = {0, 2},
ω = 1, def(P) = 1. The only free agent, 1, owns {0, 1} or {1, 2} but not {0, 1, 2}, which threatens agent 0 (holding 4:
θ₀ = 2 + 3 = 5 > 4); agent 0's good 4 is needed only by the frozen agents 2 and 3, so no role swap can free agent 0.
The repair exchanges the goods of the frozen agents 0 and 3, each of which needs the other's: in
P′ = ({5}, {1}, {3}, {4}) both are still frozen, 𝒩 is unchanged, agent 0 holds 5 (worth 8 to it) and {0, 1, 2} is
safe, def(P′) = 0. This file's tool reproduces all 3,062 failing states of those runs from their lists
(`results/k4_dl13/n4_failures_*.tsv`; `k4/dl13_t4_runs.sh`, `results/k4_dl13_stuck/t4_failures_*.log`): every one is T1-stuck with no (T3) repair, and
**none of them is T4-optimal** (defined below); at every def > 0 state of those 1,131 profiles a frozen rotation
lowers the deficit.

The coordinator's extension is **R_134** = (T1) ∪ (T3) ∪ (T4), with **(T4)**: the frozen agents' one-good bases are
permuted among the frozen agents, all stay frozen, the needed set is unchanged, every other base is unchanged
(Conjecture **DL₁₃₄**, K4.DL2.T134: DL_R for R_134 at f ≥ 1; REFUTED by #74 at f = 1 by `dl13-n4m9-rot`, §2.3). The improving (T4)
moves of the failures are of the following kind.

The *frozen need digraph* of P has the frozen agents as vertices and an edge x → y when x needs B_y. A *frozen
rotation* along a cycle x₁ → x₂ → … → x_k → x₁ of it gives each x_i the good of x_{i+1}; more generally, a *Pareto
reassignment* is a permutation π of F with v_x(B_{π(x)}) ≥ v_x(B_x) for every x ∈ F; every agent that moves then
strictly prefers its new good (distinct goods have distinct values), hence needs it, so π is a product of frozen
rotations. P is **T4-optimal** if its frozen need digraph is acyclic, i.e. no Pareto reassignment other than the
identity exists. At f = 1 every P is T4-optimal.

**Lemma 12 (frozen rotations never raise the deficit).** Let P be min-frozen with ω ≥ 1 and π a Pareto reassignment,
P′ the result (x ∈ F takes B_{π(x)}). Then P′ is min-frozen, NA(P′) = NA(P), F(P′) = F, J(P′) = J, the free agents keep
their bases and needs; every bundle safe in P is safe in P′, and u′_o(Z) ≥ u_o(Z) for every free o and every Z. Hence
Val_{P′}(o) ≥ Val_P(o) for every free o and **def(P′) ≤ def(P)**, with strict inequality iff some free o has a bundle
Z, safe in P′, with |Z| + u′_o(Z) > Val*(P); in particular when a best owner o, an optimal X and some c ∈ J ∖ X have
every blocker of c among the moved agents and X ∪ {c} threatens none of them holding its new good (the failures above),
or when u′_o(X) > u_o(X).

*Proof.* For x ∈ F, v_x(B_{π(x)}) ≥ v_x(B_x) > 0, so B_{π(x)} ⊆ R_x and N_x(B_{π(x)}) ⊆ N_x(B_x) (M2). So
NA(P′) ⊆ 𝒩, every good of NA(P′) is still a one-good base (the goods of 𝒩 are permuted among F), J is unchanged and
misses 𝒩, and the free agents' pairs miss 𝒩: P′ ∈ 𝒫, and |F(P′)| = |NA(P′)| ≤ f forces NA(P′) = 𝒩 by minimality;
F(P′) is then the set of agents holding one good of 𝒩, i.e. F. Safety: the frozen agents' base values weakly rise and
the others' bases are unchanged, so θ_w(Z) ≤ v_w(B_w) ≤ v_w(B′_w). For u: w ↦ B_w is a bijection F → 𝒩 in P and in P′,
so u_o(Z) = #{h ∈ 𝒩 : h ∉ N_o(Z) ∪ 𝒩_{−o}} and u′_o(Z) = #{h ∈ 𝒩 : h ∉ N_o(Z) ∪ 𝒩′_{−o}}, and
𝒩′_{−o} ⊆ 𝒩_{−o} (frozen agents' needs shrink, free agents' needs are unchanged). The free agents and ω are those of P;
Lemma H1. For the strict case, X ∪ {c} is then a safe bundle with |X ∪ {c}| + u′_o(X ∪ {c}) ≥ |X| + 1 + u_o(X). ∎

**Corollary 12.1 (normalization).** From every min-frozen P, finitely many frozen rotations reach a T4-optimal P* with
def(P*) ≤ def(P) (each rotation raises Σ_{x ∈ F} v_x(B_x)).

**Conjecture DL₁₃^opt.** At f ≥ 1, every *T4-optimal* min-frozen P with def(P) > 0 has a (T1) or (T3) neighbour with a
smaller deficit.

**Proposition 12.2.** DL₁₃^opt, with Theorem Z at f = 0, implies TARGET₄. *Proof.* Take the relation R^opt(P, P′):
"P′ arises from P by frozen rotations followed by at most one (T1) or (T3) move" at f ≥ 1, and any pair at f = 0. If
def(P) > 0 at f ≥ 1, rotate to P* (Corollary 12.1). If def(P*) < def(P), P* is an R^opt-neighbour with a smaller
deficit; otherwise P* is T4-optimal with def(P*) = def(P) > 0, and DL₁₃^opt gives P″ with def(P″) < def(P) and
R^opt(P, P″). At f = 0 Theorem Z (K4.C4MIN.Z) gives DL for the full relation (`k4/dl2.md` §3). So DL_{R^opt} holds, and
`EFX.C4min.target4_of_defLocal` (K4.STRAT.DL2.LEAN, any relation) gives TARGET₄. ∎ (Writing R^opt in Lean is for the
formal workstream; the descent there takes any R.)

(**DL₁₃^opt is refuted at f = 1**, §2.3, so Proposition 12.2 is now an implication from a false statement; Lemma 12 and

### 2.3 Rotations are needed at f = 1 too: the target R_T4 and the T3 stage

a key: at f = 1 `k4/c4min_reduce.md` §2 (Theorem Z′, K4.C4MIN.RED.Z, PROVED) shows that at every key some configuration
has a free owner whose bundle threatens no free agent, so its only blockers are frozen. What is missing is the
transfer from that configuration to a deficit-minimal P of the key: a configuration maximizing Theorem Z's potential
need not minimize the deficit, and the frozen agents it exposes need not be those of P.

**Remark (DL on the key graph).** That transfer is not needed for TARGET₄. Write κ(P) for the key of P (needed set,
frozen agents, their goods), def*(κ) for the least deficit of a min-frozen P with key κ, and N(κ) for the keys reached
by one (T3) or (T4) move from some state of κ. Let R_key(P, P′) hold iff κ(P′) ∈ N(κ(P)) ∪ {κ(P)} (and every pair at
f = 0). Then R_T4 ⊆ R_key, so DL_{R_T4} implies DL_{R_key}, which implies TARGET₄ (K4.STRAT.DL2.LEAN takes any
relation; f = 0 is Theorem Z), and DL_{R_key} is equivalent to **DL on the key graph**: *every key κ with def*(κ) > 0
has a neighbour κ′ ∈ N(κ) with def*(κ′) < def*(κ)*. (If def(P) > def*(κ(P)), a state of the same key is better;
otherwise a better state lies in a neighbouring key.) In this form the role swap may start from any state of the key,
e.g. from the configuration of Theorem Z′, and only the least deficits of the keys matter. On the data it holds
wherever DL_{R_T4} does (a (T3) repair at a T3-stage state is a better neighbouring key).

## 3. The structure of T1-stuck states

**Proposition T.** Let P be min-frozen with f ≥ 1, ω ≥ 1, def(P) > 0.
- **(a) Criterion.** P is T1-stuck iff for every free y and every B′ ≠ B_y inside B_y ∪ J admissible for y, with P′
  the re-base: (i) every Z with B′ ⊆ Z ⊆ W_y that is safe for y in P has |Z| + u_y(Z) ≤ V; and (ii) every free
  o ≠ y has Val_{P′}(o) ≤ V.
- **(b) Owners are blocked.** For every best owner o and optimal X, C := J ∖ X ≠ ∅, and every c ∈ C has a blocker.
- **(c) A single free blocker cannot escape.** Let P be T1-stuck, and let c ∈ C have exactly one blocker y, free.
  Then every B′ ≠ B_y inside (B_y ∪ J) ∖ (X ∪ {c}), admissible for y, with v_y(B′) ≥ v_y(B_y) (or, more generally,
  such that no agent counted in u_o(X) has its good in N_y(B′)) is still threatened: θ_y(X ∪ {c}) > v_y(B′). In
  particular Corollary 5's unblocking fails.
- **(d) Needed goods stay needed** (at every min-frozen P). If a free agent z is the only needer of some g ∈ 𝒩, then
  g ∈ N_z(B′) for every B′ ⊆ B_z ∪ J admissible for z (in particular v_z(B′) < v_z(g)).

*Proof.* (a) By Lemma 1 the min-frozen P′ at distance 1 are the re-bases of a free y inside (B_y ∪ J) ∩ R_y, and the
(T1) moves are those with N_y(B′) ⊆ 𝒩; the free agents of P′ are those of P. Lemma H1 gives
def(P′) = ω + 2 − max_o Val_{P′}(o), and Lemma 3 gives Val_{P′}(y) as the maximum in (i). (b) |W_o| = |B_o| + |J| and
|J| = S + ω ≥ (2 − |B_o|) + ω, so |W_o| ≥ ω + 2 > V ≥ |X|: C ≠ ∅. If X ∪ {c} were safe, then (u_o is monotone by
(M1)) |X ∪ {c}| + u_o(X ∪ {c}) ≥ V + 1, against optimality. (c) Otherwise Lemma 2 with Y = X ∪ {c} (c ∉ B′, so
c ∈ J(P′); Y is safe in P′: the agents outside {o, y} are not blockers of c, and y holding B′ is not threatened) and
e = 0 gives def(P′) ≤ def(P) − 1; the re-base is a (T1) move (B′ is admissible for y), against T1-stuckness.
(d) The re-base to B′ is in 𝒫 with NA(P′) = 𝒩_{−z} ∪ N_z(B′) ⊆ 𝒩 (Lemma 1(c)), so NA(P′) = 𝒩 by minimality;
g ∉ 𝒩_{−z}, hence g ∈ N_z(B′). ∎

**Where the frozen agents and their needers sit (EVIDENCE, `results/k4_dl13_stuck/stats.log`, the runs of §1).** At
f = 1 let x be the frozen agent, on g. Then g is the top of x and of every needer y of g (as in `k4/c4min_f1.md`
Lemma 1 for configurations: 𝒩 = {g}, so a top a_y ≠ g is not needed, v_y(a_y) ≤ v_y(B_y) < v_y(g)). The 13,171
T1-stuck states with f = 1 fall into two regimes:
- **several needers of g** (10,405 states). No owner can unfreeze x then (every u-term is 0), so the deficit counts
  bundle sizes only. At 10,352 of them (99.5%; at n = 3 all 9,743) some best owner o has the **S1 shape** (o needs g;
  a junk good c that x alone blocks). At 1,434 of these every S1 triple θ-fails, of kind (θ-b) at 1,426 (a big-top
  best owner o whose three lower goods fill X ∪ {c}), (θ-a) at 3 and both at 5; (θ-a) occurs at some triple of 44
  states, all with three needers of g (n = 4);
- **one needer z of g** (2,766 states). Then x is big-top on its top at 2,753 (99.5%), and its three lower goods lie
  two in J and one in the base of a best owner at 1,702, all three in J at 694, one in J and two in best owners'

  since z is the only needer, that owner is z (an S1 shape, 405 states) or an owner that needs nothing. The repair is

## 6. What remains

The existence step is open. In the architecture of the target R_T4 (§2.3: normalize the free part by (T1), (T2),
the frozen part by (T4), then repair by (T3)), a proof needs, at a T3-stage state P with f ≥ 1:
1. **SX: a frozen obstruction.** Proposition T(b), (c) say every best owner is blocked and a single free blocker cannot
   escape; they do not produce a frozen blocker, and without the T3-stage hypothesis there need not be one
   (`dl13-n3m6`, §5). At the T3 stage the data say there always is one (Conjecture SX, 11,262 states). The route is
   Theorem Z′ (`k4/c4min_reduce.md` §2, f = 1; Theorem F for robust frozen agents at f ≥ 2): some configuration of the
   key has an owner that threatens no free agent. The missing step is to carry this to a deficit-minimal P of the key;
   for TARGET₄ it suffices to show DL on the key graph (§2.3, Remark), where the swap may start from that
   configuration's state, provided the swap goes below the key's least deficit.
2. **From a frozen obstruction to a role swap.** Where the obstruction has the S1 shape and is θ-ok, Corollary 9.1
   repairs it; where the frozen agent is big-top with one needer and its lower goods are within reach of one helper,
   Corollary 8.2 does. Open at the T3 stage: the θ-b case (1,242 states, 1,199 of them at f = 1 with several needers;
   S1 shape, o big-top on g with its lower goods in X ∪ {c}; smallest `dl13-n3m7-theta`), where Corollary 9.2 and Lemma 11 certify a repair
   at most states (the other needer takes g, x takes c, o keeps its base and absorbs the needer's old base; or x owns
   X ∪ {c, c′} minus a lower good of o) but no structural condition guarantees one; and the T3-stage states without an
   S1 shape that C2 and C3 miss (77 at f ≥ 2, 22 at f = 1 with several needers).
3. **The one-needer regime**: why x is big-top and why its lower goods are within reach of one helper. On the data
   C1, C2 or C3 applies at all but 3 of the 2,766 one-needer T1-stuck states (all on one core, n = 3, m = 6; `dl13-n3m6`
   is one; none at the T3 stage). `dl13-n4m9-rot` (§2.3) shows that the helper may itself need a good of a third free
   agent; there the deficit is lowered inside the key ((T2)), so the state is not at the T3 stage.
4. **f ≥ 2**: the same, per frozen agent; the u-terms of the other frozen agents enter through e and e* (Lemmas 9, 11),
   and a frozen blocker may have only frozen needers (at 97 T4-optimal T1-stuck states with f ≥ 2 every single frozen
   blocker of a best owner is of that kind), so its role swap must go through another frozen agent's needer.
