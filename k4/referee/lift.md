## 2. Lemma LIFT-red

Re-derived:
1. Values rise along both chains.
2. Every good of J₁ ∪ J₂ ∪ H₀¹ is unwanted at B₂:
   - J by (I2);
   - y_z because z is a holder of Y;
   - a because z is a holder of B₁;
   - and z itself now holds t with v_z(t) > v_z(a) > p_z.
3. The other bundles are single goods, plus the pair H₀¹, possibly moved one step along P₂.
   - P₂ is canonical, so it passes single bundles, except the pair if it goes through k.
   - That pair cannot reach z, because z's new bundle is a single good.
   - A pair of unwanted goods threatens nobody.
4. So only X can threaten, Lemma M applies at B₂, and "X to h is EFX₀ ⟺ X threatens no i ≠ h at its B₂ value".

Correct.

**The junk-free condition on P₁ is needed.** If a middle agent takes junk, its bundle {y, g} can threaten a second
wanter of y. With middle junk allowed, my check finds 14 pairs where some other bundle threatens, all on random
starts; the earlier version of the lemma omitted the condition.
- Example: values [[37,33,11,27,0,0,0], [18,0,0,37,23,0,35], [31,37,27,0,8,0,0], [0,25,0,19,0,23,13]], picks
  (2, 3, 0, 1);
- P₁ = (2, 1, 0) with H₀¹ = {2, 4}, P₂ = (3, 0) with H₀² = {3, 5}.

## 3. Lemma LIFT-1

Re-derived:
1. Every good of X is unwanted.
2. Bundles other than h's threaten nobody. This includes z's {t, g}: t is unwanted by (L1), and g is junk.
3. (L3) protects every i ∉ {h, z}.
4. The three-case count for z, using |R_z ∖ {t}| ≤ 3.
5. h is not threatened.
6. The S\* shape holds.

Correct.

**Simplification.** Step 4 bounds each leftover good of X by v_z(t) "since t is z's favourite good". That property is
introduced only parenthetically ("Let t be z's good at B₂ (its favourite good of R_z)"), and Lemma LIFT-red does not
supply it. But it is not needed:
- X ⊆ J ∪ {y_z, a}, and v_z(junk) ≤ p_z < v_z(a) < v_z(t), by (I2) and the gains of both chains;
- so every good of X is worth less than t to z anyway.

LIFT-1 therefore holds for z's single good t at B₂, favourite or not. (L2) is still needed, for |A| = 2. My mutation
that drops "favourite": 2,623 constructions, 0 failures.

**The remarks.**
- A second sharer k′ off both chains values both goods of H₀¹: each good of J ∪ {y_z} is worth at most its pick, so an
  improving pair must consist of goods it values. So R_{k′} = {y_{k′}} ∪ H₀¹ ∪ {at most one ℓ}. H₀¹ ∩ X = ∅ and
  y_{k′} ∉ X, so (L3) holds. Lemma PL(c) is not needed for this; |R| ≤ 4 suffices.
- For P₁'s head k off P₂: X ∩ R_k ⊆ {y_k, ℓ}, and y_k ∈ X only if P₁ = (k, z), with a = y_k released or taken by h.
  Correct.

## 4. Lemma LIFT (multi §8)

1. **Moves.** k holds T, so the junk of B⁻ in I⁻ is the junk of B in I. Values and the move conditions are agent by
   agent, so an official move of I⁻ is one of I that leaves k unchanged.
2. **Completions.**
   - Pairs (i, j) with i, j ≠ k are as in I⁻.
   - Toward T: removing a good leaves one good of T, worth ≤ v_i(B_i) ≤ v_i(X_i).
   - (k, j) is the hypothesis.
   - k takes no junk.

   Correct.
3. **The hypothesis at B₁ after a canonical chain taking a junk pair, or the pool pair {junk, Y_z}.** It follows from
   (I2) and (U); my check never saw it fail.
4. **The "fails only if" remark.** With S ∩ R_k ⊆ {Y_k, ℓ}:
   - a threat needs {Y_k, ℓ} worth more than T with a third, worthless good to remove, or k wanting ℓ inside a
     multi-good bundle;
   - Y_k alone never threatens, since p_k < v_k(T).

   Correct, as a necessary condition.
