# The (k, p) = (4, 3) cell: four relevant goods, at most three valuers per good (proof/k4-p3)

Workstream `proof/k4-p3`, branch `claude/k4-p3`. Target: **TARGET₄ for p ≤ 3**, every additive instance in which every
agent positively values at most four goods and every good is positively valued by at most three agents has a complete
EFX₀ allocation. The class is closed under the peeling reductions (L2, L3 and their k = 4 forms only delete agents and
goods), so it suffices to treat k = 4 cores in which every good has at most three valuers, the *(4, 3) cores*. Every
p ≤ 2 instance is settled by the multigraph theorem (arXiv 2606.18665, ledger T3), so this is the first open cell of the
(k, p) table (`proofs/pq_bounded.md`). Notation: `proofs/lemmas.md`, `k4/lb4.md` (bases, needs, frozen agents, slots,
owner), `proofs/multigraph_digest.md` ("digest §k").

## 1. Where "at most two valuers per good" enters the multigraph proofs

Sources: `proofs/multigraph_digest.md` (a full reading of arXiv 2606.18665 by an earlier session; I have read the digest,
not the paper) and `research_notes/EFX literature from the agent side/structural_side.md` (notes on arXiv 2410.17002 and
2512.21644; I have read the notes, not the papers). Everything below about the papers is therefore second-hand, cited
to those files. "(F1)–(F3)" are the digest's three free facts of a multigraph: (F1) a held good is seen by at most one
other agent; (F2) goods split into pair classes E_{i,j} with a fixed two-way cut; (F3) pre-dumping allocations are
orientations, so envy is local to one pair.

**2410.17002** (bipartite multigraphs, monotone; notes, structural side): Lemma 2.10, "in an EFX orientation of a
multigraph, if j envies i then X_i ⊆ E(i, j); any vertex is envied by at most one neighbor", is exactly (F1) + (F3), and
the dump (Algorithm 5 / Lemma 4.10) gives A_i(X), i's free incident goods, to i's unique envier; bipartiteness is used
to argue that the envier has received nothing valuable to another T-vertex. The authors' own diagnosis of the general
case is two adjacent envied vertices with no allocated good between them, with the suggested fix |S_i ∩ S_j| ≥ 2 (two
safe parking spots).

**2512.21644** (triangle-free multigraphs, monotone; notes): Phases One and Two are stated to apply to any multigraph
(each agent takes one incident unit bundle, the allocation is EFX, nobody prefers a free incident unit bundle, the
envy graph is a union of stars); triangle-freeness is used only in Phase Three, which dumps the leftover unit bundles
B⁽¹⁾_i on the agent who envies i, through three facts: two envied adjacent agents have different enviers; the agents
envied by a recipient are pairwise non-adjacent; every other agent is non-adjacent to the recipient or to all of them.

**Summary of the map.** In all three proofs p ≤ 2 enters in two places: (a) the *unique envier* (Lemma 2.10, Obs. 2.12),
which names the dump recipient and makes the envy graph a forest of stars; (b) *per-pair accounting* of what a viewer
sees in a recipient's bundle (one piece per pair class). With p = 3, (a) becomes "at most two enviers" and (b) becomes
"at most one good per class, but a viewer meets up to three classes in one recipient". The orientation phase itself is
not the obstacle for one-good units (Greedy survives), but its *shape* is: fact 2 shows that the stars-plus-unenvied-
recipients shape cannot be the output in the cell. What a two-envier dump must keep is the budget form of (b): every
viewer i of a recipient s must have v_i(X_s ∩ R_i) ≤ v_i(X_i) (X_s holds a good worthless to i), and recipients may be
envied provided their enviers are compensated in the same step (§3).

## 2. Facts about the cell

### 2.1 P1 fails on GM4-D
GM4-D (from the screen set, `k4/sdc.md`): R_0 = {0, 2, 4, 5} with values (10, 7, 2, 6), R_1 = {0, 3, 4, 6} (8, 4, 1, 6),
R_2 = {1, 2, 5, 6} (1, 4, 8, 6), R_3 = {1, 3, 5, 6} (3, 6, 10, 2). Goods 5 (valuers 0, 2, 3) and 6 (valuers 1, 2, 3) have
three valuers, the others two. The partial allocation X_0 = {2, 4}, X_1 = {0}, X_2 = {5}, X_3 = {1, 3} with good 6 free
is EFX₀ with values (9, 8, 8, 9) and is Pareto-maximal among partial EFX₀ allocations, and no partial EFX₀ allocation with
the same values allocates more. The four other Pareto-maximal value vectors are attained by complete allocations, but
each lowers some agent below (9, 8, 8, 9). Good 6 cannot be added: to 0 gives agent 2 the view {2, 6} (10 > 8); to 3
gives agent 1 the view {3, 6} (10 > 8); to 1 or 2 makes a strongly envied two-good bundle out of an envied top.
The two candidate recipients of the unique-envier rule, agent 0 (envier of 1) and agent 3 (envier of 2), are each
blocked by the *other* valuer of 6: a two-envier configuration in which both parking spots fail.

### 2.4 Two sinks on the gadget chains

**Proposition H₂.** For every t ≥ 1, on H_t and on H³_t (variant A), the picks of LB₄'s Phase 1 with index insertion
extend, without any upgrade or rotation, to the EFX₀ allocation
- ℓ: g_1, u, u′, every c_{1,i}, and every b_{j,i} with j ≥ 2;
- y_1: its pick e_1 (g_2, or z if t = 1), every b_{1,i}, and every c_{j,i} with j ≥ 2;
- every other agent: exactly its pick (x_{j,i}: a_{j,i}; y_j: e_j).

It has exactly two bundles of more than two goods, and it is a two-owner completion (owners ℓ and y_1, no slot goods).

*Proof.* Only X_ℓ and X_{y_1} have two or more goods (for t = 1, X_{y_1} = {z, b_{1,i}}), so only they can be strongly
envied; we show that every other agent values each of them at most as much as its own bundle, which is stronger.
- X_ℓ: x_{1,i} sees g_1 and c_{1,i}, worth 3 + 4 = 7 < 8 = v(a_{1,i}); x_{j,i} (j ≥ 2) sees b_{j,i}, 6 < 8; a y sees
  nothing (its goods are a's and e_j, and e_j ≠ g_1).
- X_{y_1}: x_{1,i} sees b_{1,i}, 6 < 8; x_{2,i} sees g_2 = e_1 and c_{2,i}, 3 + 4 = 7 < 8; x_{j,i} (j ≥ 3) sees
  c_{j,i}, 4 < 8; ℓ sees nothing if t ≥ 2, and z (6 < 8 + 5 + 4) if t = 1; the other y's see nothing.
∎ Checked by the raw definition with two independent checkers for t ≤ 12 (`k4/p3/h2_explicit.py`,
`results/k4_p3x/h2_explicit.log`), and found first by SAT (`k4/p3/owners_ht.py`; one bundle is impossible there by
Propositions H and H₃ with q = 0).

So **the unbounded rotations of Propositions H and H₃ are an artefact of the single owner**: the second sink takes the
b's where the first takes the c's, and every frozen x sees one private good per sink. In counting terms: with one owner
a frozen x holding {a} with b, c junk needs one of them in a slot; with two owners it needs none. Only a *flat* frozen
top-holder (c + d > a, every pair of its lower goods beats its top) still needs a slot when all three lower goods go to
the sinks. Since an agent of a k = 4 core has at most two private goods, such an agent's third lower good is shared,
and one slot good then protects every frozen agent that shares it.

## 3. Naive dump rules and why they fail

`k4/p3/dump_rules.py`: picks of a serial-dictatorship order fixed; the free goods placed by SAT under a rule. Over the
354 suite pick vectors that extend:
- *env* (a free good g goes to a valuer of g or to an envier of a valuer of g): loses 47; smallest n = 2, m = 5
  (`c4x-n2m5-frozen`: both agents hold their tops, nobody envies, and one agent's private good must be dumped on the
  other agent, an unenvied non-valuer);
- *unenv* (a valuer, or an unenvied agent): loses 16; smallest n = 3, m = 5 (`induct-gps-q4-a`): agent 0's private
  good must go to agent 2, which is envied by agent 1, while agent 1 is compensated by a free good of its own in the same
  completion;
- *both*: loses 2 (the same n = 3 instance is the smallest);
- *envonly* (only enviers of valuers): loses almost everything; valuers must be able to keep free goods (slots).
So a dump rule cannot be a static list of recipients computed from the pick state: the slots and the compensation of
enviers must be decided together with it. This is what LB's slots-and-owner completion does, and the two-owner form
below keeps that structure.

## 5. Two-owner completions: soundness, self-protection, the threat graph

Setting: a valid pre-allocation P (`k4/lb4.md` §1) with pick bases only (Phase 1, no upgrade: policy 0), and two free
agents o₁ ≠ o₂. A *two-owner completion* places the junk into the slots of the free agents other than o₁, o₂ (at most
one good each) and into X_{o₁} = {Y_{o₁}} ∪ J₁, X_{o₂} = {Y_{o₂}} ∪ J₂; frozen agents keep exactly their picks. (OC₄) is
required for each owner.

**Lemma S₂ (soundness).** A two-owner completion of a valid P satisfying (OC₄) for both owners is EFX₀.
*Proof.* The proof of Theorem 1′₄ (`k4/lb4.md` §1, quoted there: "Fix i and j ≠ i with |X_j| ≥ 2. If j = o, (OC₄) is
the claim. Otherwise |X_j| = 2 and j is free, …") treats one viewer i and one bundle X_j at a time; a bundle with three
or more goods is an owner's and is handled by that owner's (OC₄), and the clause "if i = o with needs N_o^X" applies to
each owner. Nothing else refers to the owner. ∎

**Lemma P₂ (self-protection with two owners).** Let o₁, o₂ have bases of at most one good. Fill the slots of the free
agents x ∉ {o₁, o₂} with a pick base one at a time, each taking its ≻-best good of R_x among the junk not yet placed (any
junk good if none of its own is left). Then no such x is threatened by X_{o₁} or by X_{o₂}, whatever else is placed.
*Proof.* Lemma 2₄'s proof (`k4/lb4.md` §2), per owner: goods of N_x are frozen agents' picks, so X_{o_c} ∩ R_x consists of
goods ranked below Y_x and contains at most one good of B_{o_c}; the case analysis (Y_x = b_x: X_{o_c} ∩ R_x is at most
one good; Y_x = a_x: x holds an envy-free pair, or X_{o_c} ∩ R_x ⊆ B_{o_c}) is the same for each owner, and later
placements only remove goods from the owners' bundles. ∎

So, after the slot filling of Lemma P₂, only the frozen agents and the two owners constrain how the remaining junk J′
is split between the owners. For a frozen x, let D_x be its lower goods (ranked below its pick) that lie in J′ or are
an owner's pick. By the classification in `k4/lb4.md` §2 ("What changes at k = 4"), a frozen top-holder's minimal
threatening sets among its lower goods b > c > d are: {b, c, d} only (a > b + c); {b, c}; {b, c}, {b, d}; or every pair
(*flat*, a < c + d); a frozen holder of b_x is threatened only by {c, d}, and then only if c + d > b; a holder of c_x or
d_x never. Hence:

**Lemma C₂ (two-owner criterion).** Let 𝒯 be the hypergraph on J′ ∪ {Y_{o₁}, Y_{o₂}} whose edges are the minimal
threatening sets T ⊆ D_x (v_x(T) > v_x(Y_x)) of the frozen agents x; its pairs form the graph T. If J′ has a
2-colouring, extended by colour c on Y_{o_c}, in which no edge of 𝒯 is monochromatic, and each owner o_c values the goods
of colour 3 − c (with Y_{o_{3−c}}) at most as much as its own bundle, then the two-owner completion with
X_{o_c} = {Y_{o_c}} ∪ (colour c) satisfies (OC₄), so it is EFX₀ (Lemmas S₂, P₂).
*Proof.* A frozen x holds exactly its pick, its needs are frozen agents' picks (alone), so X_{o_c} ∩ R_x ⊆ D_x. That set
contains no minimal threatening set, so it is not threatening: v_x(X_{o_c} ∩ R_x) ≤ v_x(Y_x) ≤ v_x(X_x). Free agents
other than the owners are covered by Lemma P₂, and the owners by the last condition. ∎

By the classification, the edges of 𝒯 are: for a non-flat frozen top-holder, the pairs {b, c} (and {b, d}) or, when
a > b + c, the triple {b, c, d}; for a flat one, the three pairs (a triangle, so a flat frozen agent with three goods in
D_x has no proper colouring and needs one of them in a slot); for a frozen holder of b_x, the pair {c, d} if
c + d > b. Triples never create odd cycles.

With one owner the same frozen agents need a *hitting set* of their threatening sets placed in slots (one good per
non-flat frozen agent, two per flat one, `k4/lb4.md` §2); with two owners they need only a proper 2-colouring of 𝒯, and a
slot only to break odd cycles of T. This is the precise sense in which two sinks are "two parking spots" in the cell.

**Where the odd cycles are (EVIDENCE).** `k4/p3/threat_graph.py` builds the pair graph T for the Phase 1 state (index
insertion, no upgrade, owners' picks not included) and tests for a two-owner completion with no rotation (SAT):

| set | states with ≥ 2 free agents: T bipartite / T with an odd cycle | two owners fail (≥ 2 free) | states with 1 free agent (two owners fail) |
|---|---|---|---|
| suite, p ≤ 3 | 85 / 0 | 2 | 41 (21) |
| screen, p ≤ 3 | 86 / 0 | 2 | 42 (22) |
| certified (4, 3) cores, n = 4–6, one strict type per agent (300) | 289 / 5 | 0 | 6 (1) |
| random (4, 3) cores, n = 6 (150) | 149 / 1 | 0 | 0 |
| random (4, 3) cores, n = 7 (100) | 100 / 0 | 0 | 0 |

(Fixed sampler; `results/k4_p3x/threat_graph.log`.) Every odd cycle found is the triangle of one flat frozen agent; and whenever there are at least two free agents, two
owners succeed except on `ls-alt-rule-n4m7` and `lb4-last-block-leader-index-n4m8`, where T is bipartite and the
failure comes from the owners' picks (in `ls-alt-rule-n4m7` each free agent holds a lower good of a frozen agent and a
junk good valued by both frozen agents is threatening with both picks: it has no colour). Multi-agent odd cycles are
possible in principle: x₁ = {a₁, g, h, s}, x₂ = {a₂, h, k, s}, x₃ = {a₃, k, g, s′} with values (8, 6, 4, 3), s and s′
taken first by two leaders and the a's needed by later agents, give the triangle g h k through three frozen agents; one
slot good breaks it, and slots are plentiful there.

**Lemma F₂ (flat agents that need a slot are leaders).** In any run of Phase 1 (any insertion rule), let x be a frozen
agent holding its top whose lower goods are all junk. Then x was processed in an insertion step, so every block contains
at most one such agent; and every block containing a frozen agent contains a terminal (free agent with a slot).
*Proof.* At x's turn its top was available (x took it), and its lower goods are junk, never picked, so R_x ⊆ G; by (I3)
of `proofs/lb_last_step.md` §1 (valid at k = 4, `k4/lb4.md` §2) x was processed in an insertion step, and each block has
exactly one. The terminal is the end of a need chain from any frozen agent of the block, which stays in the block by (B2)
((A4) of `proofs/lb_last_step.md` §4, whose proof uses only (B2) and the definition of frozen). ∎

So, as in LB⁺'s Theorem A, the agents that cannot be protected by colouring alone, the flat frozen agents with three
junk lower goods, are block leaders with a terminal in their block. The step that does not yet go through: a terminal
whose slot takes a good to break a flat leader's triangle no longer follows Lemma P₂'s rule, so it may lose its own
protection and must itself be added to 𝒯 (the conflict already noted at the end of `k4/lb4.md` §2 for one owner).

**Claims X and X′ (refuted, `attempts/k4-p3-uncoupled-owners.md`).** "Any two free agents whose picks no frozen agent
values are a valid pair of owners, with no rotation" fails at n = 4, m = 8 (the owners value each other's picks, and the
frozen agents' threats push each owner's own junk into the other's bundle; every good there has at most two valuers).
Adding "and neither owner values the other's pick" (X′) fails at n = 4, m = 9: a flat frozen leader whose three lower
goods are junk, in a block whose only terminal is an owner, which is the bad case of Lemma F₂'s count. In both
examples the Phase 1 bases have no EFX₀ extension at all (brute force, `k4/p3/claim_x_cex.py`), so a rotation is
needed. Each claim held on all other states tested (1,714 of 1,715 and 7,110 of 7,111 states with such a pair).

**Target: Theorem A₂ (to prove).** After Phase 1 with no upgrades, if at least two free agents exist, some pair of
free agents is a valid pair of owners, except in an explicit bad case (owners' picks coupled through a junk good, or
more flat frozen leaders than slots), and in the bad case one rotation (Theorem B's move) leads to a state with a valid
owner or owner pair. Ingredients that carry over from LB⁺: (A3)'s argument shows that a flat frozen agent whose three
lower goods are all junk was processed in an insertion step (every good of R_x was available at its turn), so there is
at most one per block, and each block has a terminal τ(x) with a slot (`proofs/lb_last_step.md` (A3), (A4)); this is
LB⁺'s count with "exposed" replaced by "flat with three junk lower goods". What does not carry over yet: the owners'
picks (both may be lower goods of frozen agents), and the states with a single free agent (21 of the 41 suite states
with one free agent fail with two owners, as they must, and need a rotation).

## 6. OC₁^max in the cell (coordinator's task, burst 4)

**The conjecture** (LEDGER K4.SDC.OC1MAX and `k4/sdc.md` §31 on `claude/kind-lamport-y8fdme`, read there): for every
serial-dictatorship order whose picks form a maximum matching of the agent / relevant-good graph, the picks have a
*one-absorber completion* (an EFX₀ allocation keeping every pick, in which at most one agent receives two or more junk
goods), or one *improving move* gives bases that have one. Moves (§29 there): an addition (an agent adds own junk goods
to its base), a chain (x₀ takes goods of junk ∪ B_{x_t} worth more than its base, each x_i takes B_{x_{i−1}} plus own
junk and gains, no empty base handed on) or a ring. With Lemma MAXSD (some order's picks are a maximum matching,

p ≤ 3, by climbs at n = 6, 7, and prove it for p ≤ 3 (or p ≤ 2).

short, one-move algorithmic proof of the k ≤ 4 case and as the first theorem of the OC programme, not as an existence
result (PROMPT.md §5 rule 5).

**Toward a proof of OC₁^max(2)** (plan, nothing proved). With p ≤ 2, a pick has at most one other valuer, so a frozen
agent has a *unique* needer, processed after it in its block; the frozen agents with their needers form a forest whose
roots are free agents (the need chains of LB⁺, `proofs/lb_last_step.md` (A4), become unique). A junk good has at most
two valuers, both ranking it below their picks. A free agent can take one junk good safely (Theorem 1′₄'s two-good
argument), a frozen one only if its needer's final bundle is worth at least the frozen pick to the needer. The 11 n = 4
failures need a good with a third valuer exactly where a junk pair threatens two dangerous agents at once (the
coordinator's observation); with p ≤ 2 each junk good threatens at most its two valuers. A proof would follow LB⁺'s
Theorem A/B pattern on this forest: the absorber is a root, an exposed frozen agent is protected by its own root's
slot, and the one move is the chain along the unique need path. The certificate's witness kinds (n = 5: 126,102 at
the picks, 223,541 chains, 3,287 additions, 7 rings) say that chains are what is needed.

**Lemma HEAD₂ (proved).** If every good has at most two valuers, the obstacle HEAD of Lemma D-red
(`k4/oc1max_multi.md` §2 on `proof/k4-oc1max-multi`, refereed CORRECT there) never occurs: after a canonical chain
P = (k = x₀, …, x_t = z) with an improving H₀, the head z is unfrozen at B′.
*Proof.* B′_z = {g} with g = Y_{x_{t−1}}. The valuers of g include x_{t−1} (its pick) and z (which wants it), so by
p ≤ 2 they are exactly these two. z does not want its own base. x_{t−1} does not want g: if t = 1, x₀ = k holds H₀
with v_k(H₀) > p_k = v_k(g); if t ≥ 2, x_{t−1} holds Y_{x_{t−2}}, which it wants at the picks, so
v(Y_{x_{t−2}}) > p_{x_{t−1}} = v(g). So nobody wants B′_z, and z is not frozen at B′; HEAD needs z frozen. ∎
So at p ≤ 2 Lemma D-red's obstacles are CROWD and DEMAND only, and Hall's D1-red (|D| = 1, `k4/oc1_hall.md` §21) has
CROWD only.

**Theorem P2-D1 (proof by assembly; two of its ingredients are written proofs not yet refereed).** Let every agent
value at most four goods and every good be valued by at most two agents, with strict values, and let every good be
relevant to some agent (true in cores). For every serial-dictatorship order whose picks form a maximum matching, reduce
to the perfect start I′ of Lemma RED. If I′ has at most one dangerous agent, then the picks have a one-absorber EFX₀
completion, or one canonical chain from the dangerous agent, with an inclusion-minimal improving set, gives bases with
one (owned by that agent). So **OC₁^max(2) reduces to perfect starts with at least two dangerous agents.**

*Proof.*
1. *Lemma RED* (`k4/sdc.md` §31 on `claude/kind-lamport-y8fdme`; written proof, **not refereed**): completions of I′ at
   its picks or after a move of I′ extend to completions of the original instance, and moves of I′ are moves of it.
   I′ keeps the agents T not reachable from pickless agents, the pickless agents as null agents, and the goods
   {Y_t : t ∈ T} ∪ J. It inherits p ≤ 2 (it is a restriction), strictness, and the facts (π), (I2), (U) of serial
   dictatorship (its picks and order are restrictions of the original ones). Every good of I′ is relevant to an agent
   of I′: Y_t to t, and a junk good to some agent, who lies in T because by Lemma RED (i) (Lemma MAX) no reachable
   or pickless agent values junk.
2. If I′ has a one-absorber completion at its picks, we are done. Otherwise the start is hard. The last agent of the
   order is unfrozen (nobody comes after it to want its pick), so by *Proposition T0* (`k4/oc1max_multi.md` §2,
   refereed CORRECT) some dangerous agent exists: D ≠ ∅. With the hypothesis, D = {k}.
3. *Lemma D1-chain* (`k4/oc1_hall.md` §21, refereed CORRECT): k has a canonical chain P = (k, …, z), and z holds a pick.
   *Proposition 3′* (refereed CORRECT): the pool J ∪ B_z is improving for k, so the family M of inclusion-minimal
   improving sets is nonempty.
4. *Lemma HEAD₂* (above): P has no HEAD, since every good has at most two valuers.
5. *Theorem D1-C* (`k4/d1.md` §3 on `proof/k4-d1`, PR #113; written proof, **not refereed**): at a hard start with
   D = {k} in which every good is relevant to some agent, a canonical chain without HEAD has some H₀ ∈ M for which
   B′(H₀) has an S\* completion owned by k. An S\* completion is a one-absorber completion, and P with H₀ is one
   official move (a chain). ∎

So for multigraph instances the first theorem of the OC programme holds as soon as Lemma RED and Theorem D1-C are
refereed, in the case of at most one dangerous agent; the case |D| ≥ 2 is what remains (210 hard starts of that kind in
`proof/k4-oc1max-multi`'s data have p ≤ 2; census below).

**DEMAND does occur at p ≤ 2** (so "every danger set is private, hence no DEMAND" is not the key step). The D-red
census of `proof/k4-oc1max-multi` (`k4/oc1max_multi/dred.py`, run unchanged) on the 210 of its 8,383 collected hard

**The p ≤ 2 structure of a canonical chain (burst 9).** At a perfect start with strict values in which every good has
at most two valuers, a pick Y_i has at most one other valuer, hence at most one wanter w(i). So the canonical chain
from a frozen agent k is unique, P(k) = (k, w(k), w(w(k)), …, z), and it is the earliest-wanter chain E(k) of
`proof/k4-oc1max-multi` §12 (Lemma SD-E, refereed CORRECT there, applies to it). The relation "j wants Y_i" is a forest
whose roots are the unfrozen agents; P(k) is the path from k to its root. Write E_J for the unfrozen pick holders that
J threatens at their pick value (Theorem U's set).

**Lemma UNF₂ (proved).** After P(k) = (k = x₀, …, x_t = z) with any improving H₀ ⊆ (J ∪ {Y_z}) ∩ R_k, every x_i with
i ≥ 1 is unfrozen. *Proof.* B′_{x_i} = {Y_{x_{i−1}}}, whose only valuers are x_{i−1} and x_i. If i = 1, x₀ = k holds
H₀ with v_k(H₀) > p_k = v_k(Y_k). If i ≥ 2, x_{i−1} holds Y_{x_{i−2}}, which it wants at the picks, so
v(Y_{x_{i−2}}) > p_{x_{i−1}} = v(Y_{x_{i−1}}). So nobody wants B′_{x_i}. ∎ (Lemma HEAD₂ is the case i = t.) Hence the
agents frozen at B′, other than k, are agents off P(k) whose wanter still wants their pick. A truly dangerous d off
P(k) is unfrozen at B′ iff w(d) lies on P(k) and prefers its new bundle to Y_d; say P(k) *covers* d then.

**Lemma CR₂ (proved).** Let k ∈ D_T, H₀ ⊆ J ∩ R_k improving, and let a holder u ≠ k at B′ be crowded by H₀
(|H₀ ∩ R_u| ≥ 2 and v_u(H₀ ∩ R_u) > v_u(B′_u)). Then u ∈ D_T (frozen at the picks; on P(k) or covered by it), or u ∈ E_J, or
(iii) H₀ = J is three junk goods, all valued by u.
*Proof.* S = H₀ ∩ R_u ⊆ J and v_u(S) > v_u(B′_u) ≥ p_u.
- If J ⊄ R_u, removing from J a good outside R_u leaves at least v_u(S): J threatens u at p_u.
- If J ⊆ R_u, then |J| ≤ 3, since Y_u ∈ R_u and |R_u| ≤ 4.
  - |S| = 2 and |J| = 3: the two most valuable goods of J are worth at least v_u(S), so J threatens u.
  - |J| = 2: J = S = H₀ ⊆ R_k, and J threatens k only if its better good is worth more than p_k, which (I2) excludes.
    So k ∉ D_T, a contradiction.
  - |S| = 3 = |J|: case (iii).

So outside (iii), J threatens u at p_u. If u is frozen at the picks, it values junk, so u ∈ D_T. Otherwise u ∈ E_J. ∎

**Proposition KEEP₂ (proved from Lemma 2\* of `k4/oc1_hall.md` §6 and Lemma D-red, both refereed CORRECT).** Let every
agent value at most four goods and every good at most two agents, with strict values. Let Y be a perfect start (hard
or not), k ∈ D_T, P(k) = (k, …, z), and H₀ ⊆ J ∩ R_k with v_k(H₀) > p_k. Suppose:
- **(C)** every d ∈ D_T ∖ {k} is unfrozen at B′ (it lies on P(k), or P(k) covers it);
- **(N)** no holder other than z is crowded by H₀.

Then B′ has an S\* completion owned by k in which z receives Y_z.

*Proof.* Apply Lemma 2\* at B′ with owner k and H = {Y_z}. Then W_k ∖ H = J.
- (G1) and (G2) hold by Lemma D-red 1.
- (H1): by UNF₂ and D-red 2, the agents frozen at B′, other than k, are frozen at the picks and lie off P(k).
  - Those in F ∖ D value no junk.
  - Those in D ∖ D_T are threatened by no subset of J at any value at least their pick value.
  - D_T ∖ {k} is unfrozen at B′ by (C).
- *The system Y_z ↦ z.* z is unfrozen at B′ (UNF₂), so it is a holder. R_z contains Y_z and a = Y_{x_{t−1}}, so it
  contains at most two junk goods, each worth less than p_z (I2). So v_z(J ∩ R_z) < 2p_z < v_z(a) + v_z(Y_z), and J
  does not threaten z at v_z(B′_z) + v_z(Y_z). So z is admissible for Y_z, whether or not it is exposed.
- *Every non-movable exposed holder is in the system.* Non-movable means wanting a good of H₀ (impossible by D-red 1)
  or being crowded by H₀, and by (N) only z can be crowded. ∎

**Corollary U₂.** If D_T = {k} (any number of dangerous agents), (C) is void. So P(k) with H₀ repairs as soon as H₀
crowds no holder other than z, and by CR₂ the only holders H₀ can crowd are agents of E_J ∖ {z}, plus case (iii). This
is `proof/k4-oc1max-multi`'s Theorem U at p ≤ 2 with its hypothesis "E_J ⊆ {z}" weakened to "H₀ crowds no agent of
E_J ∖ {z}". At p ≤ 2 an uncrowded junk-exposed agent serves itself (DT's moves), and the head is never frozen
(HEAD₂). The chain exists, and an improving H₀ ⊆ J exists, as in Theorem U's step 0.

**Forest chains (burst 10).** The CAN₂ failures show which chains a p ≤ 2 proof needs: the repairing chains leave the
want forest only at roots. A *forest chain* is an official chain in which a receiver x_i that adds junk (H_i ≠ ∅)
takes the base of an agent x_{i−1} that is unfrozen at the picks. So a frozen agent's pick passes, exactly, to the
agent that wants it. At p ≤ 2 a forest chain is a run of need paths of the forest; between two paths, an agent
takes a root's pick, with or without junk. Canonical chains are the forest chains that are one need path. The chain
1 → 2 → 3 → 0 of the smallest CAN₂ failure is a forest chain: 3 adds junk 4 to the pick of root 2.

**The jump walk.** At p ≤ 2 a root z (an agent unfrozen at the picks) has at most one other valuer c(z), and c(z)
ranks Y_z below its own pick. The jump walk from an agent d₁ follows P(d₁) to its root z₁, jumps to d₂ = c(z₁),
follows P(d₂) to z₂, jumps to d₃ = c(z₂), and so on while the trees are new.

A *jump chain* JC(d₁, k) uses the first k segments:
- every agent of P(d_i) other than d_i takes its child's pick (it wants it);
- d_{i+1} takes Y_{z_i} plus one junk good g_{i+1}, with v(Y_{z_i}) + v(g_{i+1}) > p_{d_{i+1}}; the g_i are distinct,
  and G = {g₂, …, g_k};
- d₁ takes an improving H₀ ⊆ ((J ∖ G) ∪ {Y_{z_k}}) ∩ R_{d₁}.

Write B′ for the bases after it and W = (J ∖ G) ∪ {Y_{z_k}}. The chain is an official chain from x₀ = d₁ to x_t = z_k
and a forest chain. With k = 1 (d₁ frozen) it is the canonical chain P(d₁). The cross chain of the CAN₂ failures is
k = 2: d₁ and d₂ are the two dangerous agents, each in the other's root's jump.

**Proposition FK₂ (proved from Lemma 2\* of `k4/oc1_hall.md` §6, refereed CORRECT).** Let every agent value at most
four goods and every good at most two agents, with strict values, and let Y be a perfect start. Take a jump chain
JC(d₁, k). Suppose no entry d_i (i ≥ 2) is threatened by W at v(B′_{d_i}) (automatic unless d_k is a root), and one of
the following holds:
- **(A)** no agent frozen at B′ is threatened by W at its pick value, and no single-good holder other than d₁ is
  crowded by H₀;
- **(B)** Y_{z_k} ∉ H₀, and P(d_k) has at least two agents. No agent frozen at B′ is threatened by J ∖ G at its pick
  value, and no single-good holder other than d₁ and z_k is crowded by H₀.

Then B′ has an S\* completion owned by d₁, in which z_k receives Y_{z_k} in case (B).

*Proof.* We apply Lemma 2\* at B′ with owner o = d₁. Its exposed-set W_o = B_o ∪ J′ is (J ∖ G) ∪ {Y_{z_k}} = W.
- *Values.* Every agent's base value at B′ is at least its pick value, and strictly greater for the movers.
- *Who is frozen at B′.* Every agent u of a segment other than its start holds its child's pick, whose only valuers
  are the child and u (p ≤ 2). The child now holds H₀, {Y_{z_{i−1}}, g_i} or its own child's pick, and each of these
  is worth more to it than its pick. So u is unfrozen. An agent off the chain holds its pick, and that pick's other
  valuer gained nothing worse, so it is frozen at B′ only if it was frozen at Y.
  - Hence N₁ is the unfrozen single-good holders other than d₁, and Q is the entries d₂, …, d_k plus the agents frozen
    at B′.
- *(G1).* A single good threatens nobody. The bundle {Y_{z_i}, g_{i+1}} of an entry leaves one good when a good is
  removed.
  - The valuers of Y_{z_i} are z_i and d_{i+1}, and z_i holds something worth more than p_{z_i}.
  - Every valuer of g_{i+1} ranks it below its pick (I2).
- *(G2).* The goods of J are below every valuer's pick (I2), and values only rose. The valuers of Y_{z_k} are z_k, which
  now holds something it prefers, and c(z_k), which ranks Y_{z_k} below its own pick.
- *(H1) for an entry d_i.* R_{d_i} holds Y_{d_i}, Y_{z_{i−1}}, g_i and at most one more good r. Y_{d_i} is held by
  w(d_i) if d_i is frozen, and by d_{i+1} if d_i is a root other than d_k. So W ∩ R_{d_i} ⊆ {r}, and r is junk or
  Y_{z_k}, both below p_{d_i}. Hence d_i is not threatened; when d_k is a root, this is the hypothesis.
- *(H1) for frozen agents.* This is the first condition of (A) or (B).
- *Non-movable holders.* No holder wants a good of B_o = H₀ ⊆ J ∪ {Y_{z_k}}, as in (G2). So the non-movable exposed
  holders are among the crowded ones.
- *(A).* H = ∅ satisfies (H1), and no holder is non-movable. Lemma 2\* gives the completion.
- *(B).* Take H = {Y_{z_k}}, which is junk at B′, so W ∖ H = J ∖ G.
  - z_k is an unfrozen single-good holder. Its base a = Y_{x_{t−1}} is worth more than p_{z_k}.
  - At most two goods of J ∖ G lie in R_{z_k}, each below p_{z_k}, so they are worth less than v(a) + p_{z_k}. Hence
    z_k is admissible for Y_{z_k}, whether or not it is exposed.
  - The system Y_{z_k} ↦ z_k contains every non-movable exposed holder, by the crowding condition of (B).
  - Lemma 2\* gives the completion. ∎

Two special cases:
- **Proposition KEEP₂ is case (B) with k = 1.** Its condition "every other truly dangerous agent is unfrozen" is (B)'s
  first condition, since a frozen agent threatened by J is truly dangerous. Its hypothesis k ∈ D_T is not needed.
- **The cross chain repairs whenever (A) or (B) holds.**

So the open case of OC₁^max(2) is, on this data, one shape. A chain from one truly dangerous agent leaves another
frozen, and that agent's junk must be kept out of the owner's bundle by holders' slots, Lemma 2\* with H ≠ ∅. The

danger sets of the truly dangerous agents it leaves frozen", together with FK₂.

So the slot lemma to prove is, in its main case: after a jump chain from one truly dangerous agent, some agent of the
chain is unexposed, and it can take one good of each frozen truly dangerous agent's danger set. Given such a holder,

whose leftover danger sets need one good each.

    at 96.

    at the picks, where every truly dangerous agent's danger must be kept out using the other roots' slots, with
    owner k after P(k). The chain turns k's demand into ownership and adds the t agents of P(k) after k as holders.

**Lemma MON₂ (owner transfer along a canonical chain; proved).** Let p ≤ 2, values be strict, and Y be a perfect start.
Let k be frozen, P(k) = (k = x₀, …, x_t = z), and H₀ ⊆ (J ∪ {Y_z}) ∩ R_k improving, with B′ the bases after P(k).
Compare Lemma 2\*'s data at (Y, owner z) and at (B′, owner k).
1. The sets W coincide: W_z = {Y_z} ∪ J and W_k = H₀ ∪ J′ = J ∪ {Y_z}.
2. Q(B′) ⊆ Q(Y) ∖ P(k). The agents frozen at B′ are frozen at Y and lie off P(k), by UNF₂.
3. N₁(B′) ⊇ N₁(Y) ∪ {x₁, …, x_t}. Here N₁(Y) is the roots other than z. They keep their bases, and their bases'
   other valuers only gained, so they stay unfrozen. The x_i hold one good each and are unfrozen (UNF₂).
4. For every H, an agent of N₁(Y) is exposed, or admissible for a good h, at (B′, k) iff it is at (Y, z). Its base
   and the set W ∖ H are the same.
5. At (Y, z) no holder is non-movable: nobody wants Y_z, and a danger set inside the single good Y_z is impossible.
   At (B′, k) the non-movable holders are exactly the crowded ones (nobody wants a good of H₀ ⊆ J ∪ {Y_z}).

So a hard start, at which Lemma 2\* fails for owner z (its (G1), (G2) hold at the picks), fails because every H ⊆ J
with (H1) at Y has a Hall deficiency among the roots other than z. P(k) repairs with owner k iff, for some H,
removing the demands of P(k) and of the agents it unfreezes, and adding the holders x₁, …, x_t and the unfrozen agents,
removes that deficiency without creating an exposed crowded holder that cannot be matched. The slot lemma has to show
that some k achieves this.

**Lemma EXP₂ (proved).** In the setting of MON₂, let 1 ≤ i < t, and suppose x_i is not truly dangerous, does not value
Y_z, and J ⊄ R_{x_i}. Then x_i is not threatened by W at v(B′_{x_i}), so it is unexposed for every kept-out set H.
*Proof.* x_i values no good of W other than junk, so v_{x_i}(W ∖ h) ≤ v_{x_i}(J ∩ R_{x_i}) for every h. Take
g ∈ J ∖ R_{x_i}. Then v(J ∩ R_{x_i}) = v(J ∖ g) ≤ p_{x_i}, because x_i is frozen at the picks (i < t) and is not truly
dangerous. Finally p_{x_i} < v(Y_{x_{i−1}}) = v(B′_{x_i}). ∎

(If J ⊆ R_{x_i}, then |J| ≤ 2. Then Proposition 5 of `k4/oc1_hall.md` §9 already completes the picks whenever there
is a second root.)

*Check* (`k4/p3/exp2.py`, `results/k4_p3x/fk2/exp2.log`): every frozen k and every improving H₀ at the 22,891 hard
starts (the random ones and the CAN₂ failures), 104,134 (chain, H₀) tests in all.
- MON₂ 2–3 hold in every test (asserted).
- EXP₂ holds in all 36,147 of its cases.
- Of the middle chain agents outside EXP₂'s hypotheses, 12,731 are unexposed anyway and 3,503 are exposed.
- The head z is the one chain agent that W can expose through Y_z: in 4,116 tests with t = 1, z is exposed.

So after P(k) the chain supplies about t unexposed holders. The slot lemma must show that they, together with the
roots' slots that already failed at Y, cover the danger goods of the truly dangerous agents P(k) leaves frozen.

*A sharper target (EVIDENCE, random profiles).* On the 676 n = 5 multigraph hypergraphs × 10 random profiles, every
