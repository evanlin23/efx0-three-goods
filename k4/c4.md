# Theorem C₄ for LB₄ʳ: what is proved, the gap, and a counterexample

Workstream `proof/k4-c4`, ledger open item 18, rows K4.C4.*. Target: Theorem C₄ for LB₄ʳ (`k4/lb4.md` §5): for every
strict profile of every k = 4 core and every insertion sequence τ, LB₄ʳ(τ) returns an allocation. With Theorem 1′₄
(`k4/lb4.md` §1), K4.TIE and K4.CORE this would give K4.D and TARGET₄. Notation as in `proofs/lb_last_step.md` (LB⁺,
k = 3) and `k4/lb4.md`.

**Status.** Theorem C₄ as stated is **false** (§7), and nothing here proves K4.D. What is here:
- **Refutation (§7, a written proof, not yet reviewed, with computations for small cases):** on the connected k = 4
  cores H_t (n = 4t + 1, m = 10t + 3) LB₄ʳ with index insertion needs at least ⌈2t/3⌉ nested rotations, under all
  three upgrade policies (Proposition H, a counting proof). So it fails on H_5 (n = 21, m = 53) with three rotations,
  and no fixed number of rotations makes a fixed insertion order work. The computation matches the bound for t ≤ 4
  (1, 2, 2, 3 rotations; t = 4 with the owner's needs from its base). H_t satisfies K4.D (explicit allocation, checked),
  and LB₄'s search over insertion sequences solves H_2 and H_3 without any rotation: what fails is the fixed order,
  not K4.D.
- **Proved (§2–§4c, written proofs, not yet reviewed), for every run of Phase 1 and every n:** LB⁺'s Theorems A and
  B carry over to k = 4 except at 4-good agents. With envy-free upgrades (LB₄ʳ's second policy), the owner r fails
  only in LB⁺'s bad case or when some 4-good agent is *exposed* (threatened by the junk together with r's pick)
  (Theorem A₄); LB⁺'s rotation, and more generally every rotation along a need chain to r, leaves the set
  W = J ∪ {Y_r} unchanged and exposes no agent except r (Theorem B₄, Lemma R). When exactly one 4-good agent w is
  exposed: if w is frozen, rotating w to r (w takes its goods in W) makes w a valid owner unless two explicit
  conditions fail (Theorem B₄ʷ); if w is free, it protects itself with its own slot and r stays valid unless w takes
  the last terminal of an exposed leader (Theorem A₄ᵀ). With any number of exposed agents, r is valid when the goods
  they need kept out fit into the slots (Theorem A₄⁺).
- **Checked exhaustively (§5):** every claim of these theorems on every run of Phase 1 (every insertion sequence) of
  every strict profile of every core with n ≤ 3 and n = 4 with at most two 4-good agents: 0 violations
  (`k4/c4check.c -X`, a copy of `k4/lb4.c` with the checks added; `results/k4_c4_check.log`). The theorems prove
  LB₄ʳ's success on 78% (n = 3), 94.5% (n = 4, one 4-good agent) and 89% (n = 4, two) of the runs that need an owner.
- **The gap (§6), precisely:** the runs where none of these theorems applies: A₄⁺'s count fails and either two or
  more 4-good agents are exposed, or one is and the exceptions of Theorems B₄ʷ and A₄ᵀ hold, or LB⁺'s bad case holds
  with a 4-good r exposed after the rotation. At k = 4 these break every counting step of LB⁺'s Theorem A: an exposed
  4-good agent need not lead its block, can need two goods kept out, and can use up the terminal of its block, so that
  earlier blocks can have a deficit too; §7 turns that into unboundedly many rotations.
- **Evidence (§6.2):** with at most one 4-good agent, envy-free upgrades, every owner and **one** rotation never fail
  on n ≤ 4 (conjecture C₄¹, which would give TARGET₄ for every instance where at most one agent values four goods);
  §2–§4c prove LB₄ʳ's success on 94.5% of the n = 4 runs of that kind that need an owner. LB₄ʳ's search is far larger
  than needed on the certified cores (no upgrades, every owner and two rotations suffice for n ≤ 4 mixed).

Nothing here changes K4.D, K4.T or K4.LB4 (LB₄ searches insertion sequences; §7 does not refute it).

## 1. Setting and what is reused

A k = 4 core: agents with |R_i| ∈ {3, 4}, strictly balanced, strict types (K4.TIE: strict profiles suffice). Phase
1(τ) of `k4/lb4.md` §2 (serial dictatorship; P-steps for agents that lost a good, in any order; insertion steps
choose any agent, τ) satisfies the invariants (I1), (I2), (I3), (B1), (B2) of `proofs/lb_last_step.md` §1 verbatim
(`k4/lb4.md` §2). Nothing below uses LB₄'s P-step key or τ: every statement is for every run of Phase 1.

Pre-allocations, validity (V1), (V2), frozen agents F, terminals T, slots cap, S, ω, completions and (OC₄) are those
of `k4/lb4.md` §1, and Theorem 1′₄ (every completion of a valid pre-allocation that satisfies (OC₄), with only the
owner's bundle above two goods and frozen agents holding exactly their base, is EFX₀; machine-checked in PR #31) is
used as is. We write *threatened(x, L, H)* for "max_{h ∈ L} v_x(L ∖ {h}) > v_x(H)": agent x holding H strongly envies
the bundle L. It is **monotone**: if L′ ⊆ L and v_x(H′) ≥ v_x(H), threatened(x, L′, H′) implies threatened(x, L, H)
(take the h ∈ L′ achieving the maximum for L′; v_x(L′ ∖ h) ≤ v_x(L ∖ h)).

**Envy-free upgrades (LB₄ʳ's second policy, `lb4.c -u2`).** Repeat: some agent k ∉ U with base {Y_k}, Y_k ∉ NA,
N_k ≠ ∅, and a junk good g ∈ R_k with v_k(Y_k) + v_k(g) ≥ v_k(R_k ∖ {Y_k, g}) is upgraded: base {Y_k, g}, k ∈ U, g
leaves J. (`lb4.c` also requires the pair to shrink N_k; an envy-free pair does, since with strict types every good
outside it is worth less than it.) At the fixpoint:
- **(UT₂)** no k ∉ U has Y_k ∉ NA, N_k ≠ ∅ and a junk g ∈ R_k with {Y_k, g} envy-free.
- Every u ∈ U holds an envy-free base: it is never threatened by any bundle, and its needs are empty.
- For a 3-good agent the rule is LB⁺'s (b takes c when b ∉ NA and c is junk: b + c > a). A 4-good agent upgrades
  only from b with c when a + d < b + c (a pair {b, d} or {c, d} is never envy-free).
- The result is valid, as in `proofs/lb_last_step.md` §3: junk goods are in no N_i by (I2); an upgraded base is not
  in NA when it is formed, and NA only shrinks.

Let **r** be the last-processed agent not in U, B* its block, k* the leader of B*, and **W := B_r ∪ J** (B_r = {Y_r},
or ∅ if r has no pick). As in LB⁺:
- **(A1)** r ∈ T: an agent needing Y_r is not upgraded (upgraded agents need nothing) and was processed after r (I1).
- **(A2)** every agent after r is upgraded, and r ∈ B*: a later block's leader holds its top and has no needs, so it
  is not upgraded.
- **(A4)** for x ∈ F, a *need chain* x = x₀, x₁, …, x_t (x_{i+1} ∉ U, Y_{x_i} ∈ N_{x_{i+1}}, x₀ … x_{t−1} ∈ F,
  x_t ∈ T) exists, lies in x's block and is strictly increasing in processing order (B2); τ(x) denotes its end, a
  terminal of x's block with cap ≥ 1 (τ(x) = x for x ∈ T).
- W ∩ NA = ∅: J by (V1), Y_r by (A1).

## 2. Exposure

An agent x ∉ U, x ≠ r is **exposed** (x ∈ E_r) if threatened(x, W, B_x): it strongly envies the largest bundle the
owner r can get. Since every bundle r can get is B_r ∪ (J ∖ C) ⊆ W, by monotonicity an agent that is not exposed is
safe against every completion with owner r, whatever it receives.

**Lemma E (who can be exposed).** Let x ∈ E_r and L_x := R_x ∩ W. Then x has a pick, every good of L_x is ranked below
Y_x, and |L_x| ≥ 2. Moreover:
- (i) if |R_x| = 3, then Y_x = a_x, L_x = {b_x, c_x}, and x is the leader of its block;
- (ii) if |R_x| = 4, then Y_x ∈ {a_x, b_x}; if Y_x = b_x, then L_x = {c_x, d_x}, W ⊄ R_x and v_x(c_x) + v_x(d_x) >
  v_x(b_x).

*Proof.* If x has no pick, R_x = N_x ⊆ NA, so L_x = ∅ and x is not threatened. Goods ranked above Y_x are in
N_x ⊆ NA, hence not in W; Y_x ∉ W (it is x's pick, x ≠ r, and not junk). So L_x consists of goods ranked below Y_x.
If |L_x| ≤ 1, then v_x(W ∖ h) ≤ v_x(L_x) ≤ v_x(Y_x) for every h. So |L_x| ≥ 2.
(i) Two goods below Y_x force Y_x = a_x and L_x = {b_x, c_x}. At x's turn a_x was available (x took it), every junk
good was (junk is never picked), and so was Y_r, since r is processed after x by (A2). So R_x ⊆ G at x's turn, and x
was processed in an insertion step (I3).
(ii) Two goods below Y_x force Y_x ∈ {a_x, b_x}. If Y_x = b_x, then L_x = {c_x, d_x}; max_h v_x(W ∖ h) equals
v_x(L_x) − min_{h ∈ W} v_x(h), which is v_x(c_x) < v_x(b_x) if W ⊆ R_x and v_x(c_x) + v_x(d_x) otherwise. ∎

So a 3-good exposed agent is exactly LB⁺'s exposed agent: a block leader holding its top with {b_x, c_x} ⊆ W, and
π_x := R_x ∩ J = {b_x, c_x} ∩ J is nonempty. A 4-good exposed agent need not lead its block (it may have lost one of
its lower goods, or its top), and may need two goods kept out of X_r (a flat agent, a < c + d, with b, c, d ∈ W).
Examples: `attempts/k4-c4-exposure-counting.md`.

## 3. Theorem A₄: the owner r

**Theorem A₄.** Let P be the state after any run of Phase 1 and envy-free upgrades, with ω ≥ 1. If no 4-good agent is
exposed, then r is a valid owner (some completion with owner r, the owner's needs from its base, satisfies (OC₄)),
except possibly in **LB⁺'s bad case**:
- (1) E_r ∩ B* = {k*} with k* ≠ r and k* ∈ F;
- (2) every need chain from k* ends at r;
- (3) the sets π_x (x ∈ E_r) are pairwise disjoint.

In the bad case k* has three goods and {b_k*, c_k*} ⊆ W.

*Proof.* By Lemma E(i), E_r consists of 3-good block leaders holding their tops, at most one per block, with
π_x ≠ ∅ (at most one of b_x, c_x is Y_r). This is all that `proofs/lb_last_step.md` Theorem A uses about E_r, and
(A1), (A2), (A4) hold. Its proof gives, outside the bad case, a set H ⊆ J with |H| ≤ S − cap(r) that meets every π_x:
the terminals τ(x), x ∈ E_r ∖ B*, lie in distinct blocks other than B*, and one more terminal or a shared good covers
k* when k* ∈ E_r (verbatim).

Completion: since |J| = S + ω > S − cap(r), choose C ⊇ H with |C| = S − cap(r), split C over the slots of
T ∖ {r} in any way, and let X_r = B_r ∪ (J ∖ C). Only X_r has more than two goods, frozen agents hold their base.
(OC₄): an upgraded agent is never threatened; an agent x ∉ E_r is not threatened by W with its base, hence not by
X_r ⊆ W with its bundle (monotonicity); an agent x ∈ E_r has X_r ∩ R_x ⊆ {b_x, c_x} minus a good of H, at most one
good, worth less than a_x = Y_x. Theorem 1′₄ applies. ∎

LB₄ʳ finds this completion: its owner step tries r first, and its exact test (`k4/lb4.md` Lemma 3₄; the owner's
needs from its bundle only add slots) accepts this C, since no agent with a slot is threatened by X_r with its base
alone.

## 4. Theorem B₄: LB⁺'s rotation

Assume the bad case. Fix a need chain k* = x₀, x₁, …, x_t = r (t ≥ 1) and let P′ be LB⁺'s rotation: Y′_{x_i} =
Y_{x_{i−1}} for 1 ≤ i ≤ t; k* takes the base O := {b_k*, c_k*} and joins U; the good Y_r (if any) is released. So
J′ = W ∖ O.

**Theorem B₄.** (a) P′ is a valid pre-allocation, NA′ ⊆ NA, and k*'s base O is envy-free.
(b) **W′ := O ∪ J′ = W.** Every agent exposed w.r.t. the owner k* in P′ (x ∉ U′, x ≠ k*, threatened(x, W′, B′_x))
lies in E_r ∖ {k*}, or is r; and r can be exposed only if |R_r| = 4.
(c) If r is not exposed in P′ (in particular if |R_r| = 3), then ω′ ≤ 0 and the completion without owner is EFX₀,
or k* is a valid owner of P′.

*Proof.* (a) As `proofs/lb_last_step.md` Theorem B(a)–(c): O ⊆ W by Lemma E(i); each x_i (i ≥ 1) takes a good it
needed, so its needs shrink; k* is 3-good and balanced, so b + c > a and O is envy-free with no needs; nobody else
changes. J′ ⊆ W and W ∩ NA = ∅, so J′ ∩ NA′ = ∅ (V1); the bases of U′ avoid NA ⊇ NA′ (V2).
(b) W′ = O ∪ (W ∖ O) = W. Let x ∉ U′, x ≠ k*, be exposed in P′.
- x off the chain: B′_x = B_x, so x is threatened by the same W with the same base: x ∈ E_r (x ≠ r as r is on the
  chain).
- x = x_i, 1 ≤ i ≤ t − 1: v_x(B′_x) > v_x(B_x) and W is the same, so by monotonicity x is exposed in P as well:
  x ∈ E_r. As no 4-good agent is exposed in P, x is 3-good, hence a leader by Lemma E(i). But x_i is processed after
  k* in k*'s block (A4), so it is not the leader: impossible.
- x = r and |R_r| = 3: LB⁺'s case (e) with (UT₂) in place of (UT). Y_r ≠ a_r since r needs Y_{x_{t−1}}. If Y_r is c_r
  or none, b_r was picked by another agent (I1) and is still a pick in P′ (picks other than Y_r only move along the
  chain), so b_r ∉ W. If Y_r = b_r, then r ∉ U, b_r ∉ NA (A1), N_r = {a_r} ≠ ∅ and {b_r, c_r} is envy-free, so by
  (UT₂) c_r ∉ J, and c_r ∉ W. In every case R_r ∩ W holds at most one good below r's new pick, so r is not exposed.
(c) By (b), E′ ⊆ E_r ∖ {k*}, and all of them are 3-good leaders with {b_x, c_x} ⊆ W = O ∪ J′. None has
{b_x, c_x} ⊆ O: that would make π_x = π_k* ≠ ∅, against (3). So each x ∈ E′ has a good of {b_x, c_x} in J′; let H′
be one per x, |H′| ≤ |E_r| − 1 = |E_r ∖ B*| by (1). The terminals τ(x), x ∈ E_r ∖ B*, are distinct, outside B*, and
still terminals of P′ with cap′ ≥ 1 (LB⁺'s (d): Y_x ∉ NA ⊇ NA′). So |H′| ≤ S′ = S′ − cap′(k*). If ω′ ≤ 0, fill the
slots. Otherwise take C′ ⊇ H′ with |C′| = S′ and X_k* = O ∪ (J′ ∖ C′) ⊆ W′; (OC₄) holds as in Theorem A₄ (non-exposed
agents by monotonicity, exposed ones keep a good of {b_x, c_x} out). ∎

(b) is stronger than LB⁺'s (e) even at k = 3, and its proof is shorter: the rotation only permutes goods inside W and
moves chain agents up, so exposure can only appear at r. When r has 4 goods, the choice of the chain matters (r may
end on its top or on its b; `attempts/k4-c4-lbplus-rotation.md`); LB₄ʳ tries every chain.

**Corollary C₄⁰.** For every run of Phase 1 in which, after envy-free upgrades, no 4-good agent is exposed w.r.t. r,
and, in LB⁺'s bad case, r is not a 4-good agent exposed after the rotation, LB₄ʳ returns an allocation: its
envy-free-upgrade branch reaches either the completion of Theorem A₄ (owner r) or LB⁺'s rotation followed by owner
k* or no owner (Theorem B₄), both inside its search (`k4/lb4.md` §5: every chain and every subset O are tried, and
owner k* first). If the first branch (need-shrinking upgrades) already succeeds, LB₄ʳ returns that allocation.

At k = 3 (no 4-good agent) the hypotheses always hold, so Corollary C₄⁰ contains LB⁺'s Theorem C for LB₄ʳ's search.

## 4a. Rotating an exposed 4-good agent: Lemma R and Theorem B₄ʷ

The proof of Theorem B₄(b) uses nothing about k* except that it is frozen and that O ⊆ W. So it holds for every
rotation that ends at r.

**Lemma R (rotations to r keep W).** Let k ∈ F, k = x₀, x₁, …, x_t = r a need chain, O ⊆ R_k ∩ W, and P′ the
rotation (x_i takes Y_{x_{i−1}}; k takes the base O, joins U′ and gets the value-based needs
N′_k = {g ∈ R_k ∖ O : v_k(g) > v_k(O)}; Y_r is released). Then
- (a) J′ = W ∖ O, so O ∪ J′ = W;
- (b) if v_k(O) > v_k(Y_k), then N′_k ⊆ N_k, NA′ ⊆ NA and P′ is valid;
- (c) every x ∉ U′, x ≠ k, threatened by W with its base in P′ is exposed in P (x ∈ E_r), except possibly r; a chain
  agent x_i (1 ≤ i < t) is exposed in P′ only if it is a 4-good agent exposed in P; and a 3-good r is not exposed in P′.

*Proof.* (a) J′ = (J ∪ {Y_r}) ∖ O. (b) A good worth more than v_k(O) > v_k(Y_k) is ranked above Y_k, so it is in N_k;
the chain agents' needs shrink and nobody else changes, so NA′ ⊆ NA; then (V1) holds as J′ ⊆ W and W ∩ NA = ∅, and
(V2) as O ⊆ W. (c) As in Theorem B₄(b): an off-chain agent keeps its base and faces the same W; a chain agent x_i
has a better base and the same W, so by monotonicity it is exposed in P, and a 3-good exposed agent is a leader
(Lemma E(i)) while x_i is processed after k in k's block; a 3-good r is covered by the (UT₂) argument of B₄(b). ∎

**Theorem B₄ʷ (the exposed 4-good agent takes its goods in W).** Let w be a frozen exposed 4-good agent, and suppose
no other 4-good agent is exposed w.r.t. r. Let w = x₀, …, x_t = r be a need chain (so w ∈ B*), O := R_w ∩ W, and P′
the rotation. Then P′ is valid, and every agent exposed in P′ is a 3-good agent of E_r ∖ {w} or a 4-good r. If
moreover r is not a 4-good agent exposed in P′, and
- (i) no x ∈ E_r ∖ {w} has {b_x, c_x} ⊆ O, and
- (ii) k* ∉ E_r, or k* = w, or k* ∈ T, or some need chain from k* ends at a terminal other than r, or r is a
  terminal of P′,

then w is a valid owner of P′ (with its needs from its base; its base may have three goods).

*Proof.* w is exposed, so v_w(O) = v_w(W) ≥ max_h v_w(W ∖ h) > v_w(Y_w), and Lemma R(b) gives validity and
NA′ ⊆ NA. By Lemma R(c), an exposed agent of P′ other than r is in E_r ∖ {w} and is not a chain agent (the only 4-good
agent exposed in P was w = x₀); all of E_r ∖ {w} is 3-good, hence leaders of distinct blocks.
Counting, as in Theorem B₄(c). Each x ∈ E′ has {b_x, c_x} ⊆ W = O ∪ J′, and by (i) a good of it in J′; let H′ hold one
per x. The terminals τ(x), x ∈ E′ ∖ B*, are distinct, outside B* (the chain lies in B*), and keep their picks, so they
are terminals of P′ (NA′ ⊆ NA). E′ ∩ B* ⊆ {k*}, the leader of B*. If k* ∈ E′, then k* ≠ w and (ii) gives one more
terminal of P′ in B*: k* itself (it precedes w, so it is not on the chain), the end t′ ≠ r of a need chain from k*
(a terminal of P, not on w's chain, since chain agents other than r are frozen), or r. So |H′| ≤ S′, and w ∈ U′ has
no slot. Take C′ ⊇ H′ with |C′| = min(|J′|, S′), fill the slots, and X_w = O ∪ (J′ ∖ C′) ⊆ W. Upgraded agents are
envy-free; an agent that is not exposed in P′ is safe by monotonicity; an agent x ∈ E′ sees at most one good of
{b_x, c_x} in X_w. Only X_w has more than two goods and frozen agents hold their base, so Theorem 1′₄ applies. ∎

LB₄ʳ contains this path: after the owner step fails, its rotation search tries the frozen agent w, every need chain,
every O ⊆ R_w ∩ (J ∪ B_r), and then owner w (the only candidate when |O| = 3). The exhaustive check (§5) finds no
violation. What it leaves open for the exposed frozen 4-good agent: runs with no need chain from w to r (w not in the
last block, or its chains end at other terminals), and runs where (i) or (ii) fails.

## 4b. A free exposed 4-good agent: Theorem A₄ᵀ

A free exposed 4-good agent w can protect itself with its own slot (`k4/lb4.md` Lemma 2₄: w takes its best junk good
g_w; this needs |B_r| ≤ 1, which holds). The only cost is that w's slot is then not available to the other exposed
agents of its block. Let ℓ_w be the leader of w's block.

**Theorem A₄ᵀ.** Suppose w is the only exposed 4-good agent and w ∈ T. Let g_w be w's ≻-best good in J (it exists:
|L_w| ≥ 2 and at most one good of L_w is Y_r). Then r is a valid owner, unless
- **(Tc)** w ∉ B*, ℓ_w ∈ E_r ∩ F, every need chain from ℓ_w ends at w, and g_w ∉ R_{ℓ_w}; or
- **(Tb)** k* ∈ E_r ∩ F, every need chain from k* ends at r or (if w ∈ B*) at w, and the sets π_x of the 3-good
  exposed agents x other than ℓ_w (other than ℓ_w only when ℓ_w is as in (Tc) except that g_w ∈ R_{ℓ_w}) are pairwise
  disjoint.

*Proof.* Put g_w into w's slot first; by Lemma 2₄ (applied to w alone, before any other slot is filled) w is then not
threatened by X_r, whatever else is placed. It remains to place, in the slots of T ∖ {r, w}, a set H ⊆ J that meets
π_x for every 3-good x ∈ E_r (these are leaders, one per block, Lemma E(i)); then every exposed agent is safe and the
completion is valid exactly as in Theorem A₄. Choose terminals as in LB⁺'s Theorem A, avoiding w:
- x ∈ E_r ∖ B*, x not in w's block: τ(x) is in x's block, so τ(x) ∉ {r, w}.
- x = ℓ_w with w ∉ B*: if ℓ_w ∈ T, τ(ℓ_w) = ℓ_w; if a need chain from ℓ_w ends at a terminal other than w, use it;
  otherwise, not being in (Tc), g_w ∈ R_{ℓ_w} ∩ J = π_{ℓ_w}, and g_w (already in w's slot) serves ℓ_w: call ℓ_w
  *served*.
- B*: E_r ∩ B* ⊆ {k*} among 3-good agents. If k* ∈ E_r, then k* ∈ T (it serves itself), or a need chain from k* ends
  at a terminal of B* other than r and w, or (not being in (Tb)) two sets π_x, π_y of 3-good exposed agents that are
  not served meet, and one good of H serves both.
The chosen terminals are distinct (distinct blocks, and the extra one in B* is none of them), none is r or w, and
their number is at least |H| (one good per exposed 3-good agent that is not served, one less when two π-sets meet,
as in LB⁺'s count). So |H| ≤ S − cap(r) − cap(w); fill the remaining slots and give X_r the rest. ∎

In words: a free exposed 4-good agent breaks the owner r only by *taking the last terminal of an exposed leader*
((Tc), in an earlier block, or (Tb), in the last block). Both are rare (§5).

## 4c. Counting all slots at once: Theorem A₄⁺

Theorems A₄ and A₄ᵀ serve the exposed agents along LB⁺'s terminals, one per block. A cruder count over all slots
needs no assumption on the number of exposed 4-good agents. For a frozen exposed agent x let **ρ(x)** be the least
size of a set D ⊆ R_x ∩ J such that x is not threatened by W ∖ D with its base. By Lemma E, ρ(x) = 1 if |R_x| = 3,
and ρ(x) ∈ {1, 2} if |R_x| = 4: L_x has at most three goods, at most one of them is Y_r, and a single good of R_x
below Y_x is no threat. Write E_T := E_r ∩ T (free exposed agents) and E_F := E_r ∩ F.

**Theorem A₄⁺.** If |E_T| + Σ_{x ∈ E_F} ρ(x) ≤ S − cap(r), then r is a valid owner.

*Proof.* Every x ∈ E_T has a pick (Lemma E), so it holds {Y_x} with the needs of a pick, and cap(x) = 1. Fill the
slots of E_T first, one agent at a time, each taking its ≻-best good of R_x among the junk not yet placed. By
`k4/lb4.md` Lemma 2₄ (|B_r| ≤ 1) no agent of E_T is then threatened by X_r, whatever is placed afterwards: its proof
is per agent and uses only that x took its ≻-best junk good not yet placed, and later placements only shrink X_r.
For each x ∈ E_F fix a set D_x of size ρ(x) as above. The goods of ∪_x D_x that are not placed yet number at most
Σ ρ(x) ≤ S − cap(r) − |E_T|, the number of places left in the slots of T ∖ ({r} ∪ E_T); place them there. Fill the
remaining places with any junk, so that |C| = S − cap(r) < |J| (ω ≥ 1), and let X_r = B_r ∪ (J ∖ C) ⊆ W. (OC₄):
upgraded agents are never threatened; an agent outside E_r is safe by monotonicity, as in Theorem A₄; an agent
x ∈ E_F holds its base and X_r ⊆ W ∖ D_x, so it is safe by monotonicity and the choice of D_x; an agent of E_T is safe
by Lemma 2₄. Only X_r has more than two goods and frozen agents hold their bases, so Theorem 1′₄ applies. ∎

At k = 3 it says that r can fail only if the exposed leaders outnumber the slots of T ∖ {r}; Theorem A₄ adds the
structure of that case (LB⁺'s bad case). At k = 4 it is the only statement here that allows two or more exposed
4-good agents. It fails badly on the cores of §7: in H_t each gadget has three frozen exposed 4-good agents (its x's,
ρ = 1) and a single slot (that of y_j), and Proposition H is this count made robust under rotations.

## 6. The gap: exposed 4-good agents

By Theorems A₄ and B₄, Theorem C₄ reduces to runs of two kinds:
- **(G1)** some 4-good agent is exposed w.r.t. r;
- **(G2)** LB⁺'s bad case in which r has 4 goods and is exposed after LB⁺'s rotation.

Theorems B₄ʷ, A₄ᵀ and A₄⁺ remove part of (G1). What is left, on a run with ω ≥ 1 where A₄⁺'s count fails: two or
more exposed 4-good agents; one frozen exposed 4-good agent with no need chain to r, or with (i) or (ii) failing, or
with a 4-good r exposed after its rotation; one free exposed 4-good agent with (Tc) or (Tb); and (G2). On the n = 4
cores with one 4-good agent this is 5.5% of the runs that need an owner, and LB₄ʳ solves all of them (§6.2); §7 shows
that on larger cores the left-over part cannot be closed with a bounded number of rotations.

### 6.1 Why LB⁺'s counting breaks there (each point has a smallest example in `attempts/`)

1. *Exposed agents need not lead their blocks* (n = 3, m = 5): a 4-good agent that lost its c to the leader takes its
   top and is exposed by {b, d} (c + d < a < b + d). So a block can hold several exposed agents but only one terminal.
2. *A 4-good exposed agent can need two goods kept out* (n = 2, m = 5): a flat leader (a < c + d) with b, c, d ∈ W, one
   of them Y_r, needs both others out. Owner r's deficit (the least number of extra slots that would make r valid)
   is then 2, with no other agent: at k = 3 the deficit is at most 1 (Theorem A).
3. *A free exposed 4-good agent uses up its own slot* (n = 3, m = 6): it protects itself with its best junk good
   (`k4/lb4.md` Lemma 2₄), and that slot is then missing for the leader of its block. The deficit then sits in an
   *earlier* block (the leader and the 4-good agent in block 0, r alone in block 1), which never happens at k = 3.
4. *In (G2)* the 4-good r moves up and can be threatened by k*'s new base O = {b_k*, c_k*} itself (n = 3, m = 6): r
   held its c, takes its b, and O is r's c and d, worth more than its b; no slot helps r, since O stays in X_k*
   (`attempts/k4-c4-lbplus-rotation.md`; a rotation of the other 4-good agent repairs it). With at most one 4-good
   agent (n ≤ 4) this never makes k* fail (§5).

### 6.2 What LB₄ʳ does there (evidence)

LB₄ʳ's search is far larger than needed. Every row: every strict profile, every insertion sequence (`-i1`), 0
failures unless stated (`results/k4_c4_variants.log`):

| search | cores | failures |
|---|---|---|
| no upgrades, every owner, ≤ 2 nested rotations (`-u0 -o0 -r2 -w1 -c1`) | n ≤ 3; n = 4 with 1–3 four-good agents | 0 |
| no upgrades, owner r only, then ≤ 3 nested rotations (`-u0 -o2 -r3 -w1 -c1`) | n ≤ 3; n = 4 with 1–3 four-good agents | 0 |
| no upgrades, every owner, one rotation (`-u0 -o0 -r1 -w1 -c1`) | n ≤ 3 | 6 cores with n = 3 |

| all three policies, every owner, one rotation (`-u3 -o0 -r1 -w1 -c1`) | two 4-good agents, n ≤ 3 | 2 cores with n = 3 |
| envy-free upgrades, every owner, ≤ 2 rotations (`-u2 -o0 -r2 -w1 -c1`) | two 4-good agents, n ≤ 3 | 0 |

So with one 4-good agent, one rotation suffices on these cores; with two, two are needed (and with the owner's needs
from its base and chains ending only at non-upgraded agents, `-u2 -o0 -r2 -w0 -c0`, one n = 3 core with two 4-good
agents still fails). For cores with no 4-good agent Corollary C₄⁰ proves the `-u2 -o2 -r1` row. When r fails because a
4-good agent w is exposed (one 4-good agent, samples of n ≤ 4), the repair is almost always w itself: w is a valid
owner when it is free, and when it is frozen a rotation of w (along a need chain to r or to another terminal, w taking
some of its junk goods) makes w a valid owner or removes the need for one; in the few remaining cases LB⁺'s rotation
of k*, or another owner, works.

**Conjecture C₄¹ (at most one 4-good agent).** For every core with at most one 4-good agent, every strict profile and
every run of Phase 1: after envy-free upgrades, some free agent is a valid owner with its needs from its base, or one
rotation (any frozen k, any need chain, any O ⊆ R_k ∩ (J ∪ B_{x_t})) gives a valid pre-allocation that needs no owner
or has a valid owner. Evidence: the row above (n ≤ 4, every τ). By Corollary C₄⁰ it holds on every run in which the
4-good agent is not exposed w.r.t. r and not a 4-good r exposed after LB⁺'s rotation. With Theorem 1′₄, K4.CORE (whose
peeling never adds a 4-good agent) and K4.TIE it would give **TARGET₄ for every instance in which at most one agent
values four goods**, for every n.

## 7. Theorem C₄ as stated is false: LB₄ʳ needs unboundedly many rotations

**The cores H_t.** For t ≥ 1, H_t has the goods g_1, …, g_t, z, u, u′ and a_{j,i}, b_{j,i}, c_{j,i}
(1 ≤ j ≤ t, 1 ≤ i ≤ 3), m = 10t + 3, and n = 4t + 1 agents, in this index order:
- ℓ with R_ℓ = {g_1, z, u, u′} and values (8, 6, 5, 4);
- for each *gadget* j: x_{j,1}, x_{j,2}, x_{j,3} with R = {a_{j,i}, b_{j,i}, c_{j,i}, g_j} and values (8, 6, 4, 3), then
  y_j with R = {a_{j,1}, a_{j,2}, a_{j,3}, e_j} and values (8, 6, 4, 3), where e_j = g_{j+1} for j < t and e_t = z.

H_t is a connected k = 4 core with a strict profile (b, c are x's private goods, 6 + 4 < 8 + 3; u, u′ are ℓ's,
5 + 4 < 8 + 6; `k4/check4.py`'s `is_core` and type domains agree). It satisfies K4.D: y_j gets {a_{j,1}}, x_{j,1} gets
{b_{j,1}, c_{j,1}}, x_{j,2} and x_{j,3} get their {a, b}, and ℓ gets everything else; this allocation is EFX₀ with ℓ's
bundle the only one above two goods (checked by the raw definition for t ≤ 8, `k4/c4_chain.py check`).

**Proposition H.** With index insertion, for every q ≥ 0 with 3(t − q) > t, every pre-allocation that LB₄ʳ reaches
from Phase 1 with at most q nested rotations has no completion satisfying (OC₄). This holds for each of the three
upgrade policies, with the owner's needs from its base or from its bundle, chains ending at any non-frozen agent,
every O and every owner. In particular **LB₄ʳ fails on H_5** (n = 21, m = 53), and no fixed bound on the number of
nested rotations makes a fixed insertion order work.

*Proof.* *Phase 1.* ℓ is inserted and takes g_1. The x_{1,i} have lost g_1 (their least good) and take their tops
a_{1,i} (P-steps of key rank 0, before y_1, whose key has rank ≥ 1); then y_1, which has lost its three tops, takes
e_1 = g_2; then the x_{2,i} take a_{2,i}, and so on until y_t takes z. So Y_ℓ = g_1, Y_{x_{j,i}} = a_{j,i},
Y_{y_j} = e_j, and J consists of u, u′ and all b's and c's. The only needs are those of the y's (their three a's), so
every x is frozen and ℓ and the y's are free. No upgrade applies under any policy: a y's goods other than its pick are
picks, and ℓ and the x's have no needs or are frozen.

*Needs stay in gadgets.* In every valid pre-allocation reachable by rotations, every need of an agent is an a of its
own gadget, and ℓ keeps g_1 and needs nothing. By induction: a chain agent takes a good it needed, so it now holds an
a of its gadget, and its needs are the a's ranked above it; a rotated x_{j,i} takes O ⊆ {b_{j,i}, c_{j,i}, g_j} (its
goods in J ∪ B_end), and validity forbids needing the junk goods b, c, so its value-based needs are ⊆ {a_{j,i}}
(O = {c} or {g_j} would need b and is invalid); a rotated y needs only a's (every O is worth ≥ 3 = v(e_j)). Hence a
need chain lies in one gadget, ℓ is never frozen, and a rotation changes the bases of agents of a single gadget. So
after at most q rotations at least t − q gadgets are *untouched*: y_j holds e_j, and each x_{j,i} holds exactly
{a_{j,i}}, frozen, with b_{j,i}, c_{j,i} ∈ J.

*Counting.* Take any completion with owner o that satisfies (OC₄) (an owner is needed: ω = |NA| − σ with
σ = 2n − m = −2t − 1, so ω ≥ 2t + 1, and X_o has at least three goods). Every junk good is in X_o or in the slot of a
free agent other than o. If both of b_{j,i}, c_{j,i} are in X_o while x_{j,i} holds {a_{j,i}} (plus possibly a slot
good outside R_x), x_{j,i} strongly envies X_o (6 + 4 > 8; if X_o ⊆ R_x then X_o = {b, c, g_j} and 6 + 4 > 8 again).
Likewise ℓ ≠ o needs one of u, u′ outside X_o. These goods are private, so they are distinct, and each takes a slot
place. Now count, per gadget, slot places minus these forced goods. ℓ: at most 1 − 1 = 0. A touched gadget j: a free
x holding {a} gives at most 1 − 1, a frozen one −1, an x with a base of two or more goods 0, and y_j at most +1; an x
with the one-good base {b_{j,i}} (the only valid one-good base of an x) gets a slot in `lb4.c`'s owner test with
the owner's needs from its bundle, but it needs a_{j,i}, which must then be y_j's frozen pick, so at most one x of the
gadget has such a base and then y_j has no slot: at most +1 in all. An untouched gadget: y_j gives +1 and its three
frozen x's −3, so −2. A completion needs a nonnegative total, so (t − s) − 2s ≥ 0 for the number s ≥ t − q of
untouched gadgets, i.e. 3(t − q) ≤ t. ∎

**Computation** (`k4/c4_chain_runs.sh` → `results/k4_c4_chain.log`; `k4/c4_chain.py run` feeds H_t to `k4/c4_lb4w.c`,
which is `k4/lb4.c` with 64-bit good masks): with index insertion and all three policies, LB₄ʳ with ⌈2t/3⌉ − 1
rotations fails on H_t and with ⌈2t/3⌉ rotations succeeds, for t ≤ 4 (1, 2, 2, 3 rotations for t = 1, 2, 3, 4), as
Proposition H and the matching construction (rotate x_{j,1} to y_j in ⌈2t/3⌉ gadgets) predict. For t ≤ 3 these are
LB₄ʳ's own options; for t = 4 the owner's needs come from its base (`-w0`): with `-w1` the owner test enumerates every
set C ⊆ J above the minimum size (2^26 per owner at t = 4), which is out of reach, and so is a direct run on H_5.
Proposition H covers both. On H_2 and H_3, LB₄ (`-i2 -u1 -r1 -w1 -c1`, a search over insertion sequences) succeeds
with owner r and no rotation. On H_3, LB₄ʳ with two rotations succeeds for every one of the 171,074 insertion
sequences (`-i1 -u3 -r2 -w1 -c1`), 170,682 of them with owner r and no rotation: index insertion is a bad order here.

**What this changes.** The target "every run of Phase 1 works after at most three rotations" (the candidate Theorem
C₄ of `k4/lb4.md` §5 and ledger item 18) is refuted, and so is every version with a fixed bound on the rotations.
The number of rotations needed grows with the number of gadgets whose owner-side demand exceeds their terminals
(§6.1): each rotation repairs one gadget. The results of §2–§4c are statements about single runs and single
rotations and are unaffected. A proof of K4.D along these lines needs either a choice of the insertion sequence
(LB₄'s search handles H_t) or an unbounded sequence of rotations with a potential that guarantees termination and
progress; `attempts/k4-c4-pareto-moves.md` shows that the obvious potential has dead ends.

**The formal statement.** PR #35 (`formal/k4-c4-frame`) states Theorem C₄ in Lean as `EFX.LB4R.TheoremC4` (every τ)
and `TheoremC4index` (τ = [], index order), with at most three rotations, and lists eight modelling choices. By its
description (the Lean file itself was not read here), the proof of Proposition H goes through under each of them: the
profile is strict (choice 1); τ = [] is index insertion (2); the count bounds every completion satisfying (OC₄), not
only those LB₄ʳ tries first, in any search order (3, 4, 6); a rotated agent with a one-good base may have a slot (5),
which the per-gadget bound of +1 already allows (the only such base of an x is {b}, and then y_j holds that x's a as a
frozen pick; a y with a one-good base is counted at +1); chains may end at any non-frozen agent, which releases its
base (7, 8). No upgrade applies after Phase 1 on H_t under any policy. So if Proposition H is right, `TheoremC4index`
and `TheoremC4` are false, and of the two routes framed there only the one through C₄∃ (`TheoremC4exists`)
remains open.
